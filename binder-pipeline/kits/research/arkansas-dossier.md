## WARNING FOR FUTURE EDITORS — FIVE MINEFIELDS

1. **The State Fire Marshal moved in 2023 and most of the old statute is repealed.**
   Act 841 of 2023 relocated the State Fire Marshal from the Division of Arkansas State
   Police to the **Office of Fire Protection Services, Division of Emergency Management**
   (Ark. Code Ann. § 20-22-1009). It **repealed** §§ 12-13-102 through 12-13-107,
   12-13-109, 12-13-110 and 12-13-115 through 12-13-119, re-enacting the Fire Marshal's
   duties at § 20-22-1010. **The Arkansas Fire Prevention Code's own front matter still
   names the Arkansas State Police**, because the 2021 edition predates the move. Every
   web guide in existence — including our own state guide — still cites Arkansas State
   Police and/or the repealed §§ 12-13-1xx. Do not follow them. [V]

2. **§ 12-13-120 is still on the books and is still titled "Arkansas Fire Prevention
   Code." It is NOT the adoption authority.** It is a storm-shelter provision for Group E
   (educational) occupancies. Citing it for code adoption is the single easiest mistake
   to make here, because the title is exactly what you are looking for. [V]

3. **"Statewide code" and "statewide permit" are different things, and Arkansas has only
   the first.** The Arkansas Fire Prevention Code binds one- and two-family dwellings as a
   *construction standard* everywhere in the state, and local governments are forbidden to
   adopt a different one. Nothing requires a city or county to issue a permit, review a
   plan, or inspect a house. Writing "Arkansas has a statewide residential code" is true;
   letting a reader infer "so I need a permit" is the error the whole kit exists to
   prevent. [V]

4. **Arkansas deleted most of the IRC out of its own residential volume.** Energy,
   fuel gas, all plumbing chapters, all electrical chapters and **every appendix** are
   struck from AFPC Volume III and replaced by pointers to separate Arkansas codes. If you
   quote an IRC chapter number from a national source without checking the Arkansas
   deletions table, you will print a requirement that does not exist here. [V]

5. **The county-authority question has no clean "yes" statute.** Counties act under a
   general services power (§ 14-14-802) in which "building codes" is not an enumerated
   item, and under the planning-board subchapter (§ 14-17-201 et seq.) whose enumeration
   is "zoning, subdivision, setback, or entry control" — also not building codes. The
   counties that do run building departments lean on § 14-17-207(f). Do not write that
   counties "are authorized to adopt building codes" as though a statute says so
   plainly. It does not. [V]

## THE STRUCTURAL HEADLINE (the kit's thesis) — ONE CODE, AND USUALLY NO PERMIT

**Arkansas has a statewide-adopted residential building code, no statewide building
permit requirement, and no state residential inspection program. Whether a building
permit exists at all depends entirely on whether your city — or, far more rarely, your
county — chose to create one.**

The chain, each link verified:

- **The code exists statewide.** The **Arkansas Fire Prevention Code (AFPC), 2021
  Edition** is a state administrative rule, **015.01.22 Ark. Code R. 005**, effective
  **1 January 2023**. Its adopting text: *"The International Fire Code, 2021 edition, the
  International Building Code, 2021 edition, and the International Residential Code, 2021
  edition, as published by the International Code Council and the rules, as amended and
  adopted by the Arkansas State Fire Marshal, shall constitute the Arkansas Fire
  Prevention Code, 2021 edition. These Rules shall be effective January 1, 2023."* [V]
- **Volume III reaches your house.** AFPC Vol. III § R101.2: *"The provisions of this code
  shall apply to the construction, alteration, movement, enlargement, replacement, repair,
  equipment, use and occupancy, location, removal and demolition of detached one- and
  two-family dwellings and townhouses not more than three stories above grade plane in
  height…"* [V]
- **But the permit requirement is addressed to a building official who may not exist.**
  The AFPC defines **"BUILDING OFFICIAL shall mean any governmental official having
  authority to enforce that aspect of the Code."** § R103.1/R103.2 (unamended model text)
  has the building official *"appointed by the jurisdiction."* § R105.1 requires an owner
  to *"first make application to the building official and obtain the required permit"* —
  which is triggered by the existence of that office, not by state law directly. [V]
- **And local adoption is explicitly a choice.** AFPC Vol. I § 101.2.2: *"Each district,
  county, municipality, or other political subdivision of this state shall only adopt and
  enforce the provisions of the Arkansas Fire Prevention Code, 2021 Edition. The AFPC 2021
  Edition shall be the only foundation document available for modification by local
  jurisdictions **should they choose to adopt** more stringent provisions…"* — a
  uniformity clause whose own wording concedes the option. [V]
- **The State Fire Marshal does not fill the gap for houses.** The only *mandatory*
  inspection duty is § 20-22-1012(b)(1): *"shall inspect all places of public assembly,
  including factories or industrial plants normally employing ten (10) or more persons…"*
  General inspection under § 20-22-1012(a)(1) is *"may."* § 20-22-1010(b)(5) targets
  *"factories, asylums, hospitals, churches, schools, halls, theaters, and all other places
  in which numbers of people work, live, or congregate."* **No state plan review or state
  inspection program for new detached one- and two-family dwellings.** [V]

### Cities — express permit authority, permissive verb [V]
Ark. Code Ann. Title 14, Ch. 56, Subch. 2:
- **§ 14-56-201**: municipalities *"shall have the power to: (1)(A) … regulate the
  erection, construction, reconstruction, alteration, and repair of buildings."*
- **§ 14-56-202**, cities of the **first class** *"may: … (B) Provide that a house or
  structure not be erected within the city limits **except upon a permit** to be issued by
  an officer the governing body designates."*
- Cities of the **second class and incorporated towns** *"may: (A) **Enforce building and
  safety codes** …; (B) Provide that a house or structure not be erected **before a
  building permit is issued** by a building official the governing body designates."*

### Counties — general power only, nothing enumerated for building codes [V]
- **§ 14-14-802(b)(1)**: a county *"may provide through ordinance for the establishment of
  any service or performance of any function not expressly prohibited…"* The enumeration at
  (b)(2)(B)(iv) lists *"Planning, zoning, and subdivision control services."* **"Building
  codes" is not enumerated anywhere in § 14-14-802.**
- **§ 14-17-207(a)** — county planning board implementing ordinances are *"zoning,
  subdivision, setback, or entry control ordinances."* Building code again absent.
- **§ 14-17-207(f)**: *"The quorum court shall provide for the means of enforcing the
  official plan or zoning, subdivision, setback, and entry control ordinances…"* — the hook
  the few code-enforcing counties rely on.
- **Every verb is "may." Nothing is mandatory.**

### The design-elements ban — a real owner-builder protection, both levels [V]
- **§ 14-17-212** (counties, Acts 2019 No. 446): *"(b) A county shall not regulate
  residential building design elements."* Defined to include exterior color and cladding
  type, roof style and pitch, ornamentation, window and door placement, number and type of
  rooms, interior layout, and **minimum square footage**. Exceptions include *"(5) A
  regulation directly and substantially related to the requirements of applicable state or
  federal building or safety codes"* and *"(7) A regulation adopted as a condition for
  participation in the National Flood Insurance Program."*
- **§ 14-56-204** is the municipal mirror. [V — section confirmed present in the
  subchapter index; full text not individually pulled, so the kit states the county
  version with the citation and notes the city parallel rather than quoting it]

### Work exempt from permit where a permit regime exists — AFPC Vol. III § R105.2 [V]
Standard 2021 IRC list, **unamended by Arkansas**: detached one-story accessory structures
≤ 200 sq ft; fences ≤ 7 ft; retaining walls ≤ 4 ft (unsurcharged); water tanks ≤ 5,000 gal
with height-to-diameter ≤ 2:1; sidewalks and driveways; finish work; prefabricated pools
< 24 in deep; playground equipment; window awnings projecting ≤ 54 in; decks ≤ 200 sq ft
and ≤ 30 in above grade, not attached to a dwelling and not serving the required exit
door. Plus the standard minor-work exemptions for the trades.

## AR.1 — LICENSING AND THE OWNER-BUILDER EXEMPTION

### The structural point that drives everything [V]
Ark. Code Ann. Title 17, Chapter 25 holds **two separate regimes**. Subchapters 1–3 are
**commercial** and expressly exclude single-family residences. **Subchapter 5** (§§ 17-25-501
to 17-25-515) is **residential**, administered by the Residential Contractors Committee. A
person building a house is under Subchapter 5. Applying the commercial $50,000 threshold to a
house is the single easiest way to publish a wrong number, and most web guides do it.

### The exemption, verbatim — § 17-25-513(1), (2) [V]
> "The following shall be exempted from the licensing requirements of this subchapter:
> **(1) A person who acts as a residential building contractor in the construction of his or
> her residence unless he or she builds more than one (1) residence during any calendar year;**
> **(2) The owner of a single family residence acting as his or her own home improvement
> contractor on his or her own property;**"

Note (2) carries **no** annual cap in its text. Source: the Board's own publication,
*Arkansas Residential Contractors Licensing Law*, revised April 2026, p. 8.

### "Own residence" is DEFINED — 17 CAR § 295-101(6) [V, double-verified]
> "'Own residence', as found in Arkansas Code § 17-25-509(c) and § 17-25-513, means the
> personal residence, the principal place of abode, the domicile, **or a residence constructed
> for the occupancy of the person who owns the property**."

The final clause is what makes the exemption usable for a house you do not yet live in.
Verified both in the Board's publication and independently in the official Code of Arkansas
Rules. "Ownership" is separately defined at 17 CAR § 295-101(1) as the "sole and exclusive
right to sell or convey property."

### The exemption reaches MANAGING the job, not just doing it [V]
§ 17-25-502(2) defines "residential building contractor" to include one who "**assumes charge
in a supervisory capacity or otherwise manages the construction**" of a single-family
residence. So acting as your own general contractor is inside the exempt activity.

### NEGATIVE FINDINGS — conditions Arkansas does NOT impose [V]
Established by reading § 17-25-513 and the whole of 17 CAR pt. 295, not inferred:
- **No holding period.** No minimum time you must keep the house.
- **No "not for sale or lease" clause, no resale clawback.** Arkansas is unlike states with a
  one- or two-year no-sale rule. The rule's test is purpose at the time of building.
- **No owner-builder affidavit and no exemption form at state level.** The exemption is
  self-executing. The Board's forms index carries nothing for an exempt owner.
- **No dollar threshold on the owner-builder exemption.** The $2,000 figure is a *different*
  exemption, § 17-25-513(3), for small jobs, with an anti-splitting rule.
- **No CLB homeowner FAQ page exists** — the Board's subpages were enumerated in full.

### Other definitions worth printing [V]
- **§ 17-25-502(3)**: "single family residence" means "at least one (1) but **no more than four
  (4) units** of new construction for residential occupancy." A fourplex is still residential.
- **§ 17-25-101(a)(1),(c)**: the commercial threshold is **$50,000** including "labor and
  materials", with an anti-splitting rule at (b) — and (c) restates that the chapter covers
  everything "**excepting only single-family residences**."
- **§ 17-25-505(c)**: failure to procure a required license is a **Class A misdemeanor with
  each day in violation a separate offense**.

### THE THREE TRADE EXEMPTIONS — THE KIT'S SECOND THESIS [V]
All three exist. All three are worded differently, and the differences decide whether they
reach an unoccupied new build.

| Trade | Statute | Board / department | The wording, and what it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electrical | § 17-28-102(b) | Board of Electrical Examiners, **ADLL** | "before doing electrical work on **his or her primary residence** except as otherwise required by state law, rules, regulations, or **local ordinances**." "Primary residence" = "an **unattached single-family dwelling used as** the person's primary place of residence" (§ 17-28-101(9)) — present tense |
| Plumbing | § 17-38-302(1) | Committee of Plumbing Examiners, **ADH — not ADLL** | "Plumbing work done by a property owner in a building **owned and occupied** by him or her as his or her home **except when the license is required by local ordinance**". No rule extends this to future occupancy |
| HVACR | § 17-33-102(b)(1) | HVACR Licensing Board, **ADLL** | "Performs HVACR work in an **existing building or structure** owned and occupied by him or her as his or her home" — **textually excludes new construction**; the narrowest of the three |

Also verified: § 17-28-305(b)(2) lets a city or county "**by ordinance require a person, before
doing electrical work on his or her primary residence, to demonstrate a technical
competency**"; § 17-33-102(d) requires an exempt HVACR person to obtain "local permits and
inspections as may be required by local ordinance."

**The kit does NOT resolve whether these reach an unoccupied new build.** No Arkansas case or
board rule settles it. It prints the words, flags the divergence, and gives the reader a
write-in line to record what each board tells them. That is the honest position and it is
also the differentiator — no competing guide even notices the three tests differ.

### The subcontractor question — UNRESOLVED, and printed as such [H]
§ 17-25-513(4) shelters "a subcontractor **of a contractor licensed by** the Residential
Contractors Committee." An owner-builder is *exempt*, not *licensed*, so on the face of the
text (4) does not cover subs hired directly by an owner-builder. No CLB rule, FAQ, opinion
letter or Arkansas appellate case resolves this. **The kit does not print the inference.** It
prints § 17-25-513(5) — which shelters anyone "licensed as a contractor by another licensing
agency, board, or commission of the State of Arkansas" — and tells the reader to hire licensed
trades and verify each license, which is the correct action either way.

### § 18-44-115 Notice to Owner — a genuine owner protection [V]
On residential real estate of four or fewer units, no lien may be acquired unless the owner
received the statutory notice, and "**no lien may be claimed by any subcontractor, laborer,
material supplier, or other lien claimant unless the owner… has received at least one (1) copy
of the notice**." It is the residential contractor's duty to give it "before the commencement
of work." Reproduced in the Board's own "Important Notice" publication.

### Owner-supplied materials — DELIBERATELY NOT PRINTED [V]
§ 17-25-101(d)(2) carves owner-supplied materials out of a subcontractor's project cost for
wood framing, shingle roofing, painting, floor covering, concrete labor and playground
equipment — **but it sits in the COMMERCIAL chapter, which excludes single-family residences,
and there is no equivalent in the residential subchapter or 17 CAR pt. 295.** Whether it reads
across to the $2,000 residential test is unresolved. Do not tell a reader owner-supplied
materials fall outside the $2,000.

## AR.2 / AR.3 — ELECTRICAL, PLUMBING AND THE PERMIT ASYMMETRY

### THE NEC EDITION — Arkansas is on the 2026 NEC [V]
**This is the opposite of the usual trap.** Most states lag; Arkansas is current.
17 CAR § 210-401(a): "The Board of Electrical Examiners of the State of Arkansas hereby adopts
and incorporates herein the **National Electrical Code, 2026 edition** of the National Fire
Protection Association." (b)(1): "The National Electrical Code (NFPA 70), **2026**, shall be
the standard for the construction, installation, repair, and maintenance of electrical
facilities and the performance of electrical work."

Verified from the Board's own published administrative rules PDF, which its Electrical
Inspection and Licensing page links as "Administrative Rules of the Arkansas Board of
Electrical Examiners." Cross-checked against the ADLL proposed-rulemaking page, where 17 CAR
pt. 210 does **not** appear — so this is adopted, not pending. The rule text itself prints no
effective date; the kit therefore cites the rule and tells the reader to confirm the edition
before buying a code book.

**Arkansas's own amendments, which land on things you actually wire** [V]:
- § 210-401(b)(2): AFCI and GFCI "shall **not** be required on dwelling units circuits over 130
  volts, except for the requirements in Article 680 and 682."
- § 210-401(b)(3): AFCI/GFCI "shall not apply" within a dwelling unit to **(A)** laundry areas,
  except convenience outlets within **six feet** of a sink; **(B)** a refrigerator or microwave
  appliance.
- § 210-401(b)(4): island and peninsula receptacles — at least one within the island or
  peninsula for the first **9 sq ft** of countertop, one more per additional **18 sq ft**, and
  at least one within **two feet** of the outer end of a peninsula; additional outlets sited by
  the installer, designer or owner. (b)(5): a peninsula is measured from the connected
  perpendicular wall.
- § 210-401(e): also adopts **ANSI/NECA 1-2023** as the workmanship standard.
- § 210-401(f): "does not include any later amendments or editions" — a newer NEC does not take
  effect until the Board adopts it after notice and hearing.

### THE PERMIT ASYMMETRY — plumbing "shall", electrical "may" [V]
This is real, it surprises people, and no other guide states it.
- **Plumbing, MANDATORY where a utility exists.** § 17-38-204(c): a city, town, sewerage
  district, water district, sewer association, water association, utility gas system or county
  "having a system of either water, sewerage, or gas utility… **shall establish a system of
  permits and inspections**." § 17-38-204(b)(2) bars starting work "without the prescribed
  licenses, permits, and acceptable plan review… when required." § 17-38-204(f)(1): where a
  utility system exists but no local inspector was provided, the Department "**may take
  immediate charge and entire control of the plumbing inspection program**." ADH publishes
  state plumbing inspector coverage by county.
- **The plumbing CODE is statewide regardless.** § 17-38-103: "minimum standards **statewide in
  application**… to all types of buildings, private or public, **rural or urban**."
- **Electrical, LOCAL OPTION.** § 17-28-305(c): "Any city or county **may** establish by
  ordinance, rules, and regulations a system of permits and inspections."
- **But the state kept a reach-in.** 17 CAR § 210-1101(a): a state electrical inspector "may
  require electrical work to be **exposed for inspection, including the removal of
  sheetrock**" where the work was not subject to city inspection and there is evidence of
  serious violations.
- § 17-28-305(a): a state-licensed electrician "shall not be subject to examination or
  licensing by any city or county." § 17-28-305(d): an electrical inspector must hold a state
  license.

### The inspection list — AFPC Vol. III § R109, VERIFIED UNAMENDED [V]
R109.1.1 foundation "after poles or piers are set or trenches or basement areas are excavated";
R109.1.2 plumbing, mechanical, gas and electrical rough "prior to covering or concealment";
R109.1.3 floodplain, "upon placement of the lowest floor… prior to further vertical
construction"; R109.1.4 frame and masonry "after the roof, masonry, framing… are in place";
R109.1.5 any other inspection the building official requires; R109.1.6 final, "after the
permitted work is complete and prior to occupancy". § R110 certificate of occupancy is also
unamended.

## SEPTIC, WELLS AND WATER

### The rule, and its CURRENT title [V]
**"Rules Pertaining to Onsite Wastewater Systems"**, Arkansas State Board of Health, effective
**5 September 2024**, 81 pp. The older "Rules **and Regulations** Pertaining to…" form is
pre-2024 and still appears on legacy forms — cite the 2024 title. Promulgated under Ark. Code
Ann. § 14-236-101 et seq. and Act 96 of 1913 (§ 20-7-109).

### Who does what [V]
- **Authorized Agent** (§ 2.3) = "The Environmental Health Specialist assigned to the County" —
  ADH issues the permit through your county health unit. Where no Authorized Agent has been
  designated, applications go to the Department (§ 4.8).
- **Designated Representative (DR)** (§ 2.9) = a private licensed professional — engineer,
  surveyor, master plumber, registered sanitarian or similar — certified to make percolation
  tests, designs and inspections "subject to the Authorized Agent's final approval."
- **§ 4.10.1 makes the DR non-optional:** "Part I of the Permit Application form **shall be
  completed by a Designated Representative**… NO CHANGES OR ALTERATIONS MAY BE MADE TO THE
  SYSTEM PRIOR TO OR DURING CONSTRUCTION WITHOUT PRIOR APPROVAL OF THE AUTHORIZED AGENT."

### THE 10-ACRE EXEMPTION — the single best finding in the file [V]
**Ark. Code Ann. § 14-236-104(c):** "The requirements of this chapter shall not apply to any
individual sewage disposal system or alternate and experimental system which is situated on a
tract of land **ten (10) acres or larger**, in which the field line or sewage disposal line is
no closer than **two hundred feet (200′) to the property line**."

ADH's own published guidance confirms and sharpens it: the exemption applies to a single
residence on ten or more acres where all parts of the system are more than 200 feet from any
property line, "**including roads**", and the owner must still install and operate the system
to meet state requirements and avoid a nuisance.

**Why buyers never find it:** the exemption is **absent from the 2024 rule**. Full-text search
of the 81-page rule for "ten (10) acres", "10 acres" and "two hundred feet" returns nothing
relevant. Rule § 3 mirrors § 14-236-104(a) and (b) but omits (c). **Cite the statute, not the
rule**, and expect county staff to be unfamiliar with it.

Backstops that survive it: § 14-236-104(b) (health hazard or nuisance) and § 14-236-105, under
which a **municipality** may impose stricter local rules.

### Homeowner rights — septic and well [V]
- **Septic install:** § 14-236-102(b)(2) requires installer registration "**with the individual
  homeowner retaining all rights to install and repair his system** in accordance with the
  provisions of this chapter." "Installer" is defined as one who works "**for compensation**…
  **for others**" (§ 14-236-103(6)). The prohibitions at § 14-236-112(b) and § 14-236-114 are
  drafted around systems "**owned by any other person**." So a homeowner may install their own.
  **Three limits the kit prints:** the permit is still required; the DR must still design it;
  and rule § 14.1 ("a registered installer shall be present at the job site during all phases")
  read with § 2.24's "for others" leaves field practice varying by county — confirm locally.
- **Well drilling:** § 17-50-108(b) — "Nothing in this chapter… shall prevent a person who has
  not obtained a license… from constructing, altering, or repairing a water well or installing
  or repairing a pump or pumping equipment **for use by him or her on his or her own land**."
  **The catch:** § 17-50-107(a) still requires compliance with every construction standard.
  Exemption from the license, not from the rules.
- **NO well permit and NO notice of intent exists** for a private domestic well. [V negative]
  The only "permit" in 17 CAR pt. 11 is a contractor-side rig permit.
- **Well construction report:** due within **90 days**, must carry longitude and latitude
  (§ 11-401); "a copy shall be provided to the customer upon demand" (§ 11-403).

### Agency change — do NOT print the old name [V]
**Act 691 of 2023, § 5 abolished the Commission on Water Well Construction** and transferred
its authority to the **Arkansas Natural Resources Commission** (Natural Resources Division,
Arkansas Department of Agriculture). Existing licenses and rules were preserved. Act 691 § 120
amended only § 17-50-108(**a**) — a pure rename — leaving the homeowner exemption at (b)
untouched.

### Sequencing [V]
Rule § 4.3: the septic application and plans "shall be submitted to and receive the approval of
the Arkansas Department of Health or its Authorized Agent, **prior to construction of a
building or residence**." Rule § 4.1: where a sanitary sewer is within **300 feet** of the
point where the sewer exits the building and connection can be made without crossing another's
property, connection is mandatory and no onsite system may be used; § 14-236-113(b) refuses a
septic permit where community sewerage is "reasonably available or economically feasible."
**Whether a county building permit demands the septic permit first is a per-county fact and is
NOT claimed statewide.**

### Stormwater [V]
NPDES construction general permit **ARR150000**. Trigger: **one acre or more** disturbed,
including a larger common plan of development. Tiers: under 1 acre none; 1–5 acres automatic
coverage with documents posted on site; 5 acres and above a Notice of Intent. **Version
boundary inside this kit's first year:** the permit effective 1 Nov 2021 expires 31 Oct 2026;
the reissued permit runs 1 Nov 2026 – 31 Oct 2031. Agency is now the **Division of
Environmental Quality** within the Department of Energy and Environment, though the
adeq.state.ar.us domain and legacy PDFs still carry the old name.

## SITE PLAN STUDIO EXTRACTION

For `src/lib/siteplan/rules.ts`. Every number below is verbatim from the **Rules Pertaining to
Onsite Wastewater Systems (eff. 5 September 2024)** or **17 CAR Part 11** as marked. Nulls are
deliberate findings, not gaps.

### The complete statewide septic separation table — Rule § 6.2 [V]
The controlling phrase in every entry is *"Facilities used for the collection, treatment, and
renovation of wastewater shall be at least X feet from…"*. **Arkansas does NOT give the septic
tank and the absorption field different numbers** — one phrase covers tank, field and lines.

| Measured to | Feet | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| High-water mark of any body of water, **if within ¼ mile of a public water supply intake** on that body | **300** | § 6.2.1 |
| Any **spring used as a public water supply** | **300** | § 6.2.2 |
| **Any source of domestic water supply** — this is the private-well number | **100** | § 6.2.3 |
| High-water mark of any **stream or lake** | **100** | § 6.2.4 |
| **Pond on the same property** | **50** | § 6.2.5 |
| **Pond on adjacent property, if in the pond watershed** (never less than 50 in any case) | **100** | § 6.2.5 |
| **Any dwelling or building** | **10** | § 6.2.6 |
| **All property lines** | **10** | § 6.2.7 |
| **Any water service line** | **10** | § 6.2.8 |
| **Any sinkhole** | **100** | § 6.2.9 |

**These nine entries are the ENTIRE statewide separation table. There is no other.**

**Floors, not entitlements — § 6.1** [V]: "Minimum horizontal distances shall be used only where
ideal conditions indicate them to be sufficient and **greater distances shall be required where
local conditions demand**." Waivers go to the Department, or to Engineering where a public water
supply is involved.

### Well separation distances — 17 CAR § 11-502(c)(1) [V]
Header: "The following minimum lateral distances shall apply for common sources of
contamination listed" — **stated basis: clay and loam soils**.

| Source | Feet |
|---|---|
| Cess pools, leaching pit, pit privy | **100** |
| Subsurface seepage tile (= absorption/drain field) | **100** |
| Manure piles | **100** |
| **Septic tank** | **100** |
| Sewers (cast iron, watertight, leaded or mechanical joints) | **50** |
| Footing drains (no connection to a sewer or sewage sump) | **10** |
| Pump house floor drain (cast iron, watertight, free-fall discharge) | **2** |

**THE GRAVEL MULTIPLIER — § 11-502(c)(2)** [V]: "**When the upper formations are more pervious,
the lateral distances shall be increased (i.e., double the distance for highly pervious gravel
formations).**" In Ozark karst that silently turns 100 ft into 200 ft. Nobody publishes this.

**§ 11-502(b)(3)–(4)** [V]: "The lack of specific distances for other possible sources of
contamination such as streams, refuse disposal sites, excavations… **does not minimize their
potential hazard.** These must be evaluated in each situation."

**Well to building — § 11-502(h)** [V]: a well adjacent to a building must clear any projection
by not less than **10 feet**, and be at least **10 feet from any power line**. A well casing top
"**shall not terminate in the basement of any building**" or any below-grade pit or space not
properly drained.

**Geothermal closed-loop wells — § 11-502(e)** [V]: not closer than **50 feet** to a septic
tank, its field lines, or other potable water supply wells; waivable in writing before
construction.

### Vertical separation — Rule § 7.2 [V]
Measured from the **bottom of the proposed absorption trench**, by hydraulic conductivity class:

| | Low or Moderate | High |
|---|---|---|
| To true water table (§ 7.2.1) | **24 in** | **36 in** |
| To bedrock (§ 7.2.2), with redox features indicating moderate-or-longer SWT | **18 in** | **36 in** |
| To bedrock, no redox features or brief SWT | **24 in** | **36 in** |

**The sub-3-acre penalty — § 7.5 vs § 7.6** [V], rarely documented anywhere: a lot **under 3
acres** faces *stricter* seasonal-water-table depth minimums than a lot at or over 3 acres
(brief SWT 13 in vs 12; moderate 18 vs 16; long 24 vs 20). Also § 7.1: "No soil absorption
system shall be installed in fill material."

### Surface-discharging systems — additional setbacks, Rule § 9.7 [V]
Minimum lot size **3 acres**; discharge point to any adjacent dwelling **300 ft**; to an
adjacent property line not in the direction of flow **150 ft**; to a property line in the
direction of flow **200 ft**; to the dwelling served **100 ft**; discharge-area slope **≤ 12%**.
These are *in addition to* § 6.2.

### Internal component spacing (not property setbacks) [V]
Between absorption trenches **6 ft clear / 8 ft center-to-center** (§ 8.2); trench start to
distribution box **5 ft**, absorption area to tight-line trench **5 ft** (§ 8.10.6); interceptor
drain 5–10 ft upgrade of the absorption area (§ 2.25).

### EXPLICIT NEGATIVE FINDINGS — feet: null, with a note
Each checked by full-text search of the 2024 rule and 17 CAR pt. 11. These are findings, and
the studio must say so rather than draw a circle:
1. **Well to property line — NO statewide number.** The only lot-line figure anywhere is Rule
   § 5.2's **50 ft**, and that is a **SUBDIVISION lot-sizing** criterion, not a well setback for
   a single rural tract. Do not generalize it. (§ 5.2 also sets well-to-onsite-system at 100 ft
   on the same or adjacent lots, again for subdivisions.)
2. **Septic tank vs absorption field — no separate numbers.** The rule deliberately does not
   differentiate; both take § 6.2.
3. **Swimming pool — no statewide septic setback.** "Swimming" appears in the rule only in the
   wastewater-flow table.
4. **Basement — no statewide septic setback.** Only the general 10 ft to "any dwelling or
   building". (The *well* rules do address basements — see § 11-502(h).)
5. **Cut, embankment or steep slope — no statewide separation.** Slope is regulated indirectly
   (12% caps at § 9.7.6 and § 7.5). Confirm with the county health unit; § 6.1 lets the
   Authorized Agent demand more.
6. **Wet-weather ditch or drainage easement — no statewide setback.** § 6.2.4 covers "stream or
   lake" only.
7. **Minimum lot size for a septic system — none statewide for an individual lot.** Rule § 4.2
   sets a performance test instead: suitable primary **and** secondary areas, and no approval
   "regardless of lot size if soils are not suitable." The only hard acreages are 3 acres for
   surface discharge and 0.5 acre for drip dispersal in a **subdivision**.
8. **A distinct "public water supply WELL" septic setback — not stated as such.** The rule names
   public *intakes* and *springs* at 300 ft; a public supply well falls under § 6.2.3's 100 ft
   unless Engineering imposes more.

### Plot-plan requirements — Rule Appendix F [V]
The DR-prepared site plan must show: property lines defined and dimensioned, with "the distance
to 2 adjacent property lines… shown to tie the system to one location on the property";
"onsite wastewater system setbacks and their distances"; the location and elevation of the
water well and its distance from all system components and the water supply line; the location,
elevation and distances of all wells and onsite systems on adjoining properties; **all wells on
the property and adjacent properties within 100′** of the proposed system; all percolation test
holes, flagged; the corners of **both** the primary and secondary absorption areas, sized and
flagged; and the water supply identified by name if public.

### Owner-drawn acceptance
**Not accepted for the septic design.** Rule § 4.10.1 requires Part I — which includes the
system design and layout — to be completed by a **Designated Representative**. A homeowner may
*install* the system (§ 14-236-102(b)(2)) but may not *design* it. This is the cleanest
owner-drawn negative in the corpus so far and should be stated plainly in the studio.

### Building setbacks
Local zoning only. There is no state residential building setback. Note two Arkansas
specifics for `setbacksNote`: many counties have **no building department at all**, so the
zoning counter may be the only setback authority a builder ever meets; and since 2019 a county
"shall not regulate residential building design elements" including **minimum square footage**
(§ 14-17-212), with a municipal parallel at § 14-56-204.
