## GA.0 — Cover summary facts

1. **The exemption:** O.C.G.A. § 43-41-17(h) — build on your own land, for your own
   occupancy, no license, at any project cost. One-sale-per-24-months rule.
2. **What you're exempt from:** residential contractor licensing, which attaches over
   **$2,500** of work (§ 43-41-2(9)) — one of the lowest thresholds in the country.
3. **Code stack (effective January 1, 2026):** 2024 IRC/IBC/IPC/IMC/IFGC/ISPSC with 2026
   Georgia Amendments; **2023 NEC**; energy = **2015 IECC** with GA Supplements &
   Amendments (2020/2022/2022-additional/2023 — NOT updated in the 2026 cycle).
4. **Two mandatory tests:** blower door **< 5 ACH50** and duct leakage **≤ 6 cfm25/100
   sq ft** (unless ducts entirely inside the envelope), both by a certified DET verifier.
5. **The septic gate:** no "physical development of a lot" where a septic system will be
   used until the County Health Department issues the construction permit (DPH Rule
   511-3-1-.03(2)); permit valid 12 months.
6. **The Georgia twist:** the eight mandatory state codes bind statewide whether or not
   the county adopted or enforces them (§ 8-2-25(a)); permits/inspections are a local
   power (§ 8-2-26(a)) — some counties have no building department at all.

## GA.1 — Owner-builder exemption

**The exemption.** An owner may build on their own land without a license, acting as
their own contractor, if the building is solely for their own (family/firm) occupancy
and not for sale or lease. — O.C.G.A. § 43-41-17(h):
> "Nothing in this chapter shall preclude any person from constructing a building or
> structure on real property owned by such person which is intended upon completion for
> use or occupancy solely by that person and his or her family, firm, or corporation and
> its employees, and not for use by the general public and not offered for sale or
> lease. In so doing, such person may act as his or her own contractor personally
> providing direct supervision and management of all work not performed by licensed
> contractors."
Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-43-professions-and-businesses/ga-code-sect-43-41-17/ [VERIFIED]

**24-month rule.** One prior sale of a self-built structure within 24 months (from that
structure's CO date) kills the exemption for the next build. — § 43-41-17(h):
> "However, if, under this subsection, the person or his or her family, firm, or
> corporation has previously sold or transferred a building or structure which had been
> constructed by such person acting without a licensed residential or general contractor
> within the prior 24 month period, starting from the date on which a certificate of
> occupancy was issued for such building or structure, then such person may not, under
> this subsection, construct another separate building or structure ... or it shall be
> presumed that the ... building [was not intended] solely for occupancy by that person
> and his or her family, firm, or corporation." [bracketed condensation — full sentence on file]
Source: same page [VERIFIED]

**No delegation; codes still apply.** — § 43-41-17(h):
> "Further, such person may not delegate the responsibility to directly supervise and
> manage all or any part of the work relating thereto to any other person unless that
> person is licensed under this chapter and the work being performed is within the scope
> of that person's license." ... "In any event, however, all such work must be done in
> conformity with ... any applicable county or municipal resolutions, ordinances, codes,
> permitting, or inspection requirements."
Source: same page [VERIFIED]

**No state affidavit.** § 43-41-17(h) contains no affidavit/disclosure requirement
(contrast NC G.S. 87-14). Many Georgia counties require a notarized owner-builder
affidavit as local practice. [HEDGE: statutory absence verified against the FindLaw
text; county practice varies — kit asks the buyer to record their county's answer.]

**Licensing threshold.** "Residential contractor" licensure attaches where total value
of the work exceeds $2,500. — § 43-41-2(9) (see (10) residential-basic, (11)
residential-light commercial):
> "any contractor who may contract for ... and perform any activity or work as a
> contractor requiring licensure under this chapter ... where the total value of the
> work or activity ... exceeds $2,500.00"
Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-43-professions-and-businesses/ga-code-sect-43-41-2/
[VERIFIED — extraction elided connective clauses; re-pull the unbroken sentence at print]

**Criminal penalty.** Unlicensed contracting is a misdemeanor, minimum $500 fine per
offense. — § 43-41-12(a), penalty tail (two independent extractions agree):
> "shall be guilty of a misdemeanor and shall, upon conviction, be punished for each
> such offense by a fine of not less than $500.00 or imprisonment of three months, or
> both fine and imprisonment in the discretion of the court."
Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-43-professions-and-businesses/ga-code-sect-43-41-12/ [VERIFIED]

**Civil teeth.** An unlicensed contractor's contract is unenforceable — the reason an
owner-builder must verify every sub's license (a void contract also voids its
warranties in practice). — § 43-41-17(b):
> "As a matter of public policy, any contract entered into on or after July 1, 2008, for
> the performance of work for which a residential contractor or general contractor
> license is required by this chapter ... and which is between an owner and a contractor
> who does not have a valid and current license required for such work in accordance
> with this chapter shall be unenforceable in law or in equity by the unlicensed
> contractor."
Source: FindLaw § 43-41-17 [VERIFIED — SB 503 (2024) renamed "general contractor" to
"commercial general contractor"; confirm current phrasing at print]

**Hiring subs — specialty trades.** Specialty contractors may contract directly with an
owner without a residential license, incl. incidental other work up to the greater of
$10,000 or 25% of contract value. — § 43-41-17(f):
> "Nothing in this chapter shall preclude a specialty contractor from offering or
> contracting to perform or undertaking or performing for an owner limited, specialty,
> or specific trade contractor work." / cap: "...does not exceed the greater of
> $10,000.00 or 25 percent of the total value at the time of contracting of the work to
> be performed."
Source: FindLaw § 43-41-17 [VERIFIED — subsection letter per structure listing; see Open
Questions #2]

**Hiring subs — licensed trades.** Chapter 14 licensees (electrical, plumbing,
conditioned air, low-voltage, utility) may contract directly with the owner, same
$10,000/25% incidental cap. — § 43-41-17(e):
> "Furthermore, nothing in this chapter shall preclude a person licensed under Chapter
> 14 of this title to perform plumbing, conditioned air contracting, utility
> contracting, electrical contracting, or low-voltage contracting from offering to
> perform, performing, engaging in, or contracting to engage in the performance of
> construction work or services directly with an owner..."
Source: FindLaw § 43-41-17 [VERIFIED — same letter caveat]

**Trade licenses are statewide.** — § 43-14-8(a)-(c):
> (a) "No person shall engage in the electrical contracting business as an electrical
> contractor unless such person has a valid license from the Division of Electrical
> Contractors..." (b) same form for master and journeyman plumbers; (c) "No person shall
> engage in the business of conditioned air contracting as a conditioned air contractor
> unless such person has a valid license from the Division of Conditioned Air
> Contractors."
Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-43-professions-and-businesses/ga-code-sect-43-14-8/ [VERIFIED]

**Homeowner may self-perform the trades.** Electrical, plumbing, HVAC, low-voltage on a
dwelling the individual owns or occupies — subject to codes and local inspection. —
§ 43-14-13(d):
> "This chapter shall not prohibit an individual from installing, altering, or repairing
> plumbing fixtures, air-conditioning and heating, air-conditioning and heating
> fixtures, utility systems, or electrical or low-voltage wiring services in a
> residential dwelling owned or occupied by such individual; provided, however, that all
> such work must be done in conformity with all other provisions of this chapter, the
> rules and regulations of the board, and any applicable county or municipal
> resolutions, ordinances, codes, or inspection requirements."
Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-43-professions-and-businesses/ga-code-sect-43-14-13/
[VERIFIED — identical text on two independent extractions]
Scope note: the carve-out is personal to "an individual" — it authorizes the owner's own
hands, not an unlicensed helper; anyone hired must hold the § 43-14-8 license. [HEDGE:
inference from the exemption's wording, corroborated by local-government summaries of
§ 43-14-13 — print as scope guidance, not as a quoted prohibition.]

**Workers' comp.** The Act does not reach employers with fewer than three regular
employees. — § 34-9-2(a)(2):
> "...nor to any person, firm, or private corporation, including any public service
> corporation, that has regularly in service less than three employees in the same
> business within this state..."
Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-34-labor-and-industrial-relations/ga-code-sect-34-9-2/ [VERIFIED]
Statutory-employer note (§ 34-9-8, "principal, intermediate, or subcontractor"): whether
an owner-builder can be a "principal contractor" is fact/case-law dependent. [HEDGE:
kit advises collecting subs' certificates of insurance; prints no "you're safe" claim.]

## GA.2 — Permit application checklist

### Code editions in force (August 2026)

**Effective January 1, 2026** DCA adopted: 2024 IRC, IBC, IPC, IMC, IFGC, ISPSC (each
with 2026 Georgia Amendments), **2023 NEC** (2026 GA Amendments), 2024 IFC (Safety Fire
Commissioner) — announced as "new mandatory state codes," adopted under O.C.G.A.
§ 8-2-23. Sources: https://dca.georgia.gov/announcement/2025-12-09/new-codes-jan-2026 ;
https://dca.georgia.gov/community-assistance/construction-codes/current-state-minimum-codes-construction [VERIFIED]

**The packet text itself** (title "Revised January 1, 2026"):
> "The INTERNATIONAL RESIDENTIAL CODE FOR ONE- AND TWO-FAMILY DWELLINGS, 2024 Edition
> ... shall constitute the official Georgia State Minimum Standard One- and Two-Family
> Dwelling Code."
Source (county-posted copy of the DCA packet, read directly):
https://cherokeega.com/DSC/_resources/documents/2026%20Ga%20Amen%202024%20Codes%20Combined.pdf [VERIFIED]

**Georgia guts three IRC parts.** Plumbing (Part VII), electrical (Part VIII), and
energy (Part IV/Ch. 11) are deleted from the IRC and replaced by the state IPC, NEC, and
Georgia Energy Code — trade work is inspected to those codes, not IRC chapters:
> "Part IV, Energy Conservation (Chapter 11), is deleted... Part VII, Plumbing (Chapters
> 25 through 33), is deleted... Part VIII, Electrical (Chapters 34 through 43), is
> deleted from the INTERNATIONAL RESIDENTIAL CODE..."
Source: same 2026 packet [VERIFIED]

**No mandatory fire sprinklers** in one- and two-family dwellings. — O.C.G.A. § 8-2-4,
quoted inside the DCA packet:
> "Neither the state residential and fire building code nor any residential and fire
> building code adopted by a political subdivision of the state adopted after May 24,
> 2010, shall include a requirement that fire sprinklers be installed in a single-family
> dwelling or a residential building that contains no more than two dwelling units."
Source: same 2026 packet [VERIFIED]

### Energy code — edition and the two mandatory tests

**Edition.** Still the **2015 IECC** with GA Supplements and Amendments (2020, 2022,
2022-additional, 2023); not touched by the 2026 cycle. — Ga. Comp. R. & Regs.
110-11-1-.34:
> "The INTERNATIONAL ENERGY CONSERVATION CODE, 2015 Edition ... shall constitute the
> official Georgia State Minimum Standard Energy Code."
Sources: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/georgia/Ga-Comp-R-Regs-R-110-11-1-.34 ;
DCA current-codes page [VERIFIED]

**Blower door is mandatory — do not print a visual-inspection option.** — GA amended
§ R402.4.1.2 (2020 packet, eff. 1/1/2020), read from the DCA PDF:
> "R402.4.1.2 Testing. All one and two-family dwelling units shall be tested and
> verified to less than five air changes per hour at 50 Pascals (ACH50) for Climate
> Zones 2, 3, and 4. ... A written report of the results of the test shall be signed by
> the party conducting the test and provided to the code official. ... Testing shall be
> conducted by a certified duct and envelope tightness (DET) verifier."
Source: https://dca.georgia.gov/document/publications/iecc2020amendmentspdf/download (p. 18) [VERIFIED]

**Duct testing is mandatory** — total leakage ≤ 6 cfm25/100 sq ft (rough-in or
post-construction), EXCEPT ducts and air handlers entirely inside the envelope. — GA
amended §§ R403.3.3, R403.3.4 (2020 packet, pp. 19-20):
> "R403.3.3 Duct testing (Mandatory). Ducts shall be pressure tested to determine air
> leakage..." / "The total leakage shall be less than or equal to 6 cubic feet per
> minute (113.3 L/min) per 100 square feet (9.29 m2) of conditioned floor area..." /
> "Exceptions: 1. A duct air leakage test shall not be required where the ducts and air
> handlers are located entirely within the building thermal envelope."
Source: same DCA PDF [VERIFIED — the 2023 amendment packet does not touch these sections]

### Mandatory vs. permissive; who requires permits

**Eight mandatory codes apply statewide with no local adoption.** — § 8-2-25(a),
enumerating § 8-2-20(9)(B)(i): (I) IBC, (II) NEC, (III) IFGC, (IV) IMC, (V) IPC,
(VI) IRC, (VII) IECC, (VIII) IFC:
> "...the state minimum standard codes enumerated in subdivisions (9)(A)(i)(I) through
> (9)(A)(i)(VIII) and (9)(B)(i)(I) through (9)(B)(i)(VIII) of Code Section 8-2-20 shall
> have state-wide application and shall not require adoption by a municipality or
> county."
Sources: https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-8-buildings-and-housing/ga-code-sect-8-2-25/ ;
https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-8-buildings-and-housing/ga-code-sect-8-2-20/ [VERIFIED]

**Permissive codes require local adoption** (IPMC 2018/2021 GA, IEBC 2018/2021 GA, NGBS
2008/2011 GA). — § 8-2-25(b):
> "The state minimum standard codes enumerated in subdivisions (9)(A)(i)(IX) through
> (9)(A)(i)(XIV) and (9)(B)(i)(IX) through (9)(B)(i)(XI) of Code Section 8-2-20 shall
> not be applicable in a jurisdiction until adopted by a municipality or county."
Sources: same + DCA current-codes page [VERIFIED]

**Permits are a local power — enforcement is optional for the government, the code is
not optional for you.** — § 8-2-26(a) intro + (a)(4):
> "The governing body of any municipality or county adopting any state minimum standard
> code shall have the power:" ... "(4) To require permits and to fix charges therefor."
Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-8-buildings-and-housing/ga-code-sect-8-2-26/ [VERIFIED]
Consequence: in a county with no building department there is no permit to pull — but
§§ 8-2-25(a) and 43-41-17(h) still bind the owner to the mandatory codes. [HEDGE: no
official roster of non-enforcing counties located; worksheet says call and record.]

**Private professional providers.** Applicants may retain private plan reviewers /
inspectors. — § 8-2-26(g). Source: same page [VERIFIED in substance; HEDGE: exact

### Septic (the hard state gate)

**No site work where septic will be used until the county issues the construction
permit.** — DPH Rule 511-3-1-.03(2) (Ch. 511-3-1, Jan. 2016 edition in force; authority
O.C.G.A. §§ 31-2A-6, 31-2A-11):
> "No person may begin the physical development of a lot or structure where an on-site
> sewage management system will be utilized, nor install an on-site sewage management
> system or component thereof, without having first obtained from the County Health
> Department a construction permit for the installation of an onsite sewage management
> system."
Source (official DPH PDF, read directly):
https://dph.georgia.gov/document/publication/envhealthonsiteruleschapter511-3-1jan2016pdf/download [VERIFIED]

**Clock and lifetime.** — Rule 511-3-1-.03(2)(a), (3)(a):
> "The County Board of Health shall approve or disapprove such application within twenty
> days after the receipt of a completed application." / "On-site sewage management
> construction permits shall remain valid for not more than twelve months from the date
> of issue."
[VERIFIED — same PDF]

**Site inspection first.** Permits issue "only after a site inspection by the County
Board of Health shows favorable findings relative to absorption rates, soil
characteristics, groundwater, rock..." (Rule 511-3-1-.03(3)) [VERIFIED — same PDF]

**Sequencing vs. the building permit.** Rule 511-3-1 does NOT say a building permit may
not issue before septic approval — the state gate is on beginning physical development.
Counties nearly universally require the septic permit before issuing the building
permit. [HEDGE: sequencing is county ordinance/practice, not quotable state rule — kit
prints the physical-development gate verbatim and tells the buyer to expect the septic
permit as a building-permit attachment.]

## GA.3 — Inspection sequence

**The structural fact: Georgia deletes IRC Chapter 1.** Inspection scheduling, permit
administration, and CO issuance are governed by each local government's administrative
ordinance; the IRC chapter remains only as a model. — 2026 GA Amendments to the 2024 IRC:
> "*Delete Chapter 1 'Scope and Administration' without substitution. Chapter 1 to
> remain in the Code as a reference and guide for local governments to use in the
> development of their own Administrative Procedures. (Effective January 1, 2026)"
Source: 2026 combined amendments PDF (Cherokee-posted DCA packet) [VERIFIED]

**Model sequence to print as worksheet** (IRC R109 as guide + universal GA practice):
footing/foundation → underslab plumbing → rough plumbing / rough electrical / rough
mechanical-gas (separate trade inspections against IPC/NEC/IMC-IFGC, since those parts
are deleted from the IRC) → framing → insulation → energy testing (DET blower door +
duct reports) → trade finals → building final / CO. [HEDGE: no statewide inspection
list exists because Chapter 1 is deleted — print as a model with blanks for the county's
actual required-inspection list.]

**Energy test is a de facto final gate.** The DET verifier's signed blower-door report
must be "provided to the code official" (R402.4.1.2 as amended) [VERIFIED]; CO timing is
local [HEDGE].

**Septic final.** — Rule 511-3-1-.03(4): "No person may backfill or use an on-site
sewage management system until a final inspection has been made by the County Board of
Health" and written approval issued; later site changes that adversely affect the system
render the approval void ((4)(b)). [VERIFIED — official DPH PDF]

**Who inspects trades; reinspections.** State trade boards license people, they do not
inspect houses; the local inspector (or § 8-2-26(g) private professional provider)
inspects trade work. Reinspection fees are per-jurisdiction. [HEDGE: worksheet blanks.]

## GA.5 — Forms index

1. **Septic construction permit application** — no statewide form number. Rule
   511-3-1-.03(2)(a): "Application for such a construction permit shall be made in
   writing on forms provided by the County Board of Health." County example: Hall County
   "Septic Tank Permit Application" (hallcounty.org). [VERIFIED that forms are
   county-issued; names vary]
2. **NPDES Notice of Intent** — GAR100001 NOI, EPD storm-water forms page:
   epd.georgia.gov/forms-permits/watershed-protection-branch-forms-permits/storm-water-forms/npdes-construction [VERIFIED]
3. **Land Disturbance Permit application** — from the certified Local Issuing Authority
   (county/city); where none, EPD district office. [VERIFIED structure per § 12-7-7;
   form names local]
4. **GDOT residential driveway application** — from the District Area Office; regs:
   "Only original forms may be used. Residential driveway applications can be obtained
   from the appropriate Area Office." Plus the "Permit Application Information Sheet"
   (Appendix B). [VERIFIED]
5. **Notice of Commencement** — no state form; draft to the § 44-14-361.5(b) content
   list, file with the clerk of superior court, post on site. [VERIFIED contents;
   template availability HEDGE — some clerks publish fill-ins]
6. **Owner-builder affidavit** — NO state form (§ 43-41-17(h) requires none); many
   counties require their own notarized affidavit. Worksheet blank: "county affidavit
   required? Y/N — form name: ____". [HEDGE by design]
7. **Homeowner trade permits** — owner applies under § 43-14-13(d) where the county
   permits trades separately; some jurisdictions use a homeowner disclaimer form.
   [HEDGE: county-specific]
8. **Building permit application** — local. Verified example: Fulton County "Building
   Permit Application and Site Plan Checklist" (fultoncountyga.gov, Public Works →
   Permits & Plan Review → Permit Application Forms). [VERIFIED example]
9. **DET testing reports** — blower door + duct leakage reports signed by the certified
   DET verifier, "provided to the code official" (GA IECC R402.4.1.2). [VERIFIED]
