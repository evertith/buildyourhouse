# Virginia Owner-Builder Permit Kit — Research Dossier

Researched August 2026 against primary sources: law.lis.virginia.gov (Code of
Virginia and Virginia Administrative Code), dhcd.virginia.gov, vdh.virginia.gov,
vdot.virginia.gov, and official locality sites. Every claim below carries its
citation, a verbatim quote of the operative language where one was obtained,
the source URL, and a [VERIFIED] or [HEDGE] tag. Quotes were extracted from the
live statute/regulation pages in August 2026; re-pull any quote at layout time.

Terminology note: Virginia's building code is the Uniform Statewide Building
Code (USBC), codified at 13VAC5-63. Part I is the Virginia Construction Code
(VCC); the residential volume is published as the Virginia Residential Code
(VRC). VAC section mapping used throughout: USBC §103 = 13VAC5-63-30,
§108 = 13VAC5-63-80, §109 = 13VAC5-63-90, §110 = 13VAC5-63-100,
§113 = 13VAC5-63-130, §116 = 13VAC5-63-160.

## VA.0 — Cover summary facts (headline claims for the cover sheet)

1. **The exemption.** An owner may build one primary residence for his own use
   in any 24-month period without a contractor license — Code of Virginia
   § 54.1-1101(A)(7). [VERIFIED — see VA.1 #3]
2. **The code.** The 2021 USBC (2021 I-Codes, 2020 NEC) has been in force since
   January 18, 2024, and has been the ONLY permitted edition since January 18,
   2025. The next (2024-based) edition is not expected before 2027. [VERIFIED —
   see VA.2 #1–#3]
3. **The lien agent.** Virginia puts a "mechanics' lien agent" line on every
   one/two-family dwelling permit — designated at the OWNER's option, and if
   not, the permit must say "None Designated" (§ 36-98.01). Designating one
   forces lien claimants to give notice within 30 days or lose lien coverage
   for earlier work. [VERIFIED — see VA.2 #16–#18]
4. **The septic gate.** On an unsewered lot the VDH construction permit (or
   OSE-supported approval) comes first: the building official may refuse the
   building permit until state "functional design" approval is in hand
   (USBC § 103.5; 12VAC5-610-240). [VERIFIED — see VA.2 #9–#10]
5. **The driveway.** Most Virginia roads outside cities are state-maintained.
   A new entrance onto one requires a VDOT land use permit (form LUP-A +
   LUP-PE) BEFORE construction — 24VAC30-73-60(A). [VERIFIED — see VA.2 #14]
6. **The blower door.** A blower-door test is mandatory for every new dwelling
   (no visual-inspection option), max 5 ACH50, and Virginia lets a licensed GC,
   HVAC contractor, home inspector, RDP, or certified rater run it
   (13VAC5-63-264). [VERIFIED — see VA.2 #5]

## VA.1 — Owner-Builder Exemption Walkthrough

### 1. Contractor licensing is the default; "contractor" is defined by contract value
- Claim: Anyone who contracts to build for others must hold a DPOR contractor
  license; classes are set by dollar value, and Class C starts at just $1,000.
- Citation: Code of Virginia § 54.1-1100 (definitions).
- Verbatim ("Contractor"): "any person, that for a fixed price, commission,
  fee, or percentage undertakes to bid upon, or accepts, or offers to accept,
  orders or contracts for performing, managing, or superintending in whole or
  in part, the construction, removal, repair, or improvement of any building
  or structure"
- Verbatim (Class C): "(i) the total value referred to in a single contract or
  project is over $1,000 but less than $30,000, or (ii) the total value of all
  such construction, removal, repair, or improvements undertaken by such
  person within any 12-month period is less than $250,000"
- Class B: single contract "$30,000 or more, but less than $150,000"; Class A:
  "$150,000 or more" (or $1M+ annually). (Same section, verbatim captured.)
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title54.1/chapter11/section54.1-1100/ [VERIFIED]
- Kit consequence: every sub the owner hires for a job over $1,000 must hold at
  least a Class C license.

### 2. The exemption statute is chapter-wide
- Claim: § 54.1-1101's exemptions switch off the ENTIRE licensing chapter —
  Chapter 11 of Title 54.1 — which contains both the contractor article and
  the tradesman article (§§ 54.1-1128 through 54.1-1135).
- Citation: § 54.1-1101(A), opening line; chapter table of contents.
- Verbatim: "The provisions of this chapter shall not apply to:"
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title54.1/chapter11/section54.1-1101/ [VERIFIED]

### 3. The owner-builder exemption itself
- Claim: An owner may build (or supervise building) his own primary residence
  without a license — limited to ONE residence per 24 months, and it must be
  for his own use. This is the anti-speculation line: Virginia does not give
  the exemption for a house built to sell.
- Citation: § 54.1-1101(A)(7).
- Verbatim: "Any person who performs or supervises the construction, removal,
  repair, or improvement of no more than one primary residence owned by him
  and for his own use during any 24-month period"
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title54.1/chapter11/section54.1-1101/ [VERIFIED]
- Note: unlike NC (which conditions the exemption on 12-month occupancy after
  completion), Virginia's text polices intent through "for his own use" and
  the one-per-24-months cap. No statutory holding period was found in the
  section text. [HEDGE on any claim about a specific resale waiting period —
  the statute states none; do not print one.]

### 4. Related exemptions worth a sidebar
- Family gift: § 54.1-1101(A)(8): "Any person who performs or supervises the
  construction, removal, repair, or improvement of a house upon his own real
  property as a bona fide gift to a member of his immediate family" [VERIFIED]
- Own rentals (repair/improvement only): § 54.1-1101(A)(10): "Any person who
  performs or supervises the repair or improvement of residential dwelling
  units owned by him that are subject to the Virginia Residential Landlord and
  Tenant Act" [VERIFIED]
- Same URL as #3.

### 5. The certificate-of-occupancy string attached to the exemption
- Claim: An owner who used the exemption (subdivisions A(7), A(10), A(11),
  A(12)) must obtain a certificate of occupancy BEFORE conveying the property
  to a third-party purchaser — unless the purchaser waives in writing.
  Violation is a Class 1 misdemeanor; a third violation within 36 months is a
  Class 6 felony.
- Citation: § 54.1-1101(B) and penalty provisions; section title is
  "Exemptions; failure to obtain certificate of occupancy; penalties."
- Verbatim (B): exempt persons "shall obtain a certificate of occupancy for
  any building constructed, repaired or improved by him prior to conveying
  such property to a third-party purchaser" (limited written-acknowledgment
  exception).
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title54.1/chapter11/section54.1-1101/ [VERIFIED]
- Also § 54.1-1101(C): exempt persons "shall comply with the provisions of the
  Uniform Statewide Building Code." [VERIFIED — the exemption never waives
  permits or inspections.]

### 6. What the owner signs at the permit counter instead of a license number
- Claim: The permit applicant must either prove licensure or file a WRITTEN
  STATEMENT SUPPORTED BY AN AFFIDAVIT that he is not subject to licensure; the
  building official is forbidden to issue the permit otherwise.
- Citation: § 54.1-1111 ("Prerequisites to obtaining business license;
  building, etc., permit").
- Verbatim (exemption path): "a written statement, supported by an affidavit,
  that he is not subject to licensure or certification as a contractor or
  subcontractor pursuant to this chapter."
- Verbatim (the prohibition): "It shall be unlawful for the building official
  or other authority to issue or allow the issuance of such permits unless the
  applicant has furnished his license or certificate number issued pursuant to
  this chapter or evidence of being exempt from the provisions of this
  chapter."
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title54.1/chapter11/section54.1-1111/ [VERIFIED]
- [HEDGE: each locality prints its own owner-exemption affidavit form; there
  is no single statewide form. Instruct the buyer to ask the local building
  department for its "contractor exemption" or "owner affidavit" form.]

### 7. Self-performing electrical / plumbing / HVAC on your own house
- Claim: Tradesman licensure (§ 54.1-1129) sits inside the same Chapter 11
  that § 54.1-1101(A)(7) switches off, and the statutory definition of
  "tradesman" only reaches people working "for the general public for
  compensation." Read together, an owner doing his own electrical, plumbing,
  or HVAC work on his own primary residence is outside the licensing scheme —
  but every trade still needs its permit and must pass USBC inspections.
- Citations: § 54.1-1128 (definition); § 54.1-1129 (necessity for licensure);
  § 54.1-1101(A) intro + (A)(7); § 54.1-1101(C).
- Verbatim ("Tradesman"): "any individual who engages in, or offers to engage
  in, work for the general public for compensation in the trades of
  electrical, plumbing and heating, ventilation and air conditioning."
- Verbatim (§ 54.1-1129): "no individual shall engage in, or offer to engage
  in, work as a tradesman ... unless he has been licensed"
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title54.1/chapter11/section54.1-1128/
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title54.1/chapter11/section54.1-1129/ [VERIFIED as to each quoted text]
- [HEDGE: the self-performance conclusion is a synthesis of the two statutes,
  not a single quotable sentence ("owners may wire their own house" appears
  nowhere verbatim). Print the two statutory bases and tell the buyer to
  confirm with the local building official — some departments quiz owner
  applicants before issuing trade permits. Note the only express tradesman-
  article exemption is trivial: § 54.1-1131(C) exempts single-family residence
  work "where the value of the work performed is less than $250 and such
  individual does not hold himself out to the general public as a tradesman."]

### 8. Workers' compensation when hiring helpers
- Claim: The Workers' Compensation Act reaches an employer once THREE OR MORE
  employees are regularly in service; below that, employees are excluded from
  the Act's definition.
- Citation: § 65.2-101 (definition of "Employee", subdivision 2h) and
  § 65.2-800.
- Verbatim (exclusion): "Employees of any person, firm or private corporation
  ... that has regularly in service less than three employees in the same
  business within this Commonwealth" (excluded unless coverage is voluntarily
  elected).
- Verbatim (§ 65.2-800): "Every employer subject to the compensation
  provisions of this title shall insure the payment of compensation to his
  employees in the manner hereinafter provided."
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter1/section65.2-101/
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter8/section65.2-800/ [VERIFIED]

### 9. "Statutory employer" liability for subs' workers
- Claim: § 65.2-302 makes an owner liable for compensation to a subcontractor's
  workers only when the subcontracted work is part of the owner's own "trade,
  business or occupation."
- Citation: § 65.2-302(A).
- Verbatim: when an owner subcontracts work of that kind, "the owner shall be
  liable to pay to any worker employed in the work any compensation under this
  title which he would have been liable to pay if the worker had been
  immediately employed by him."
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title65.2/chapter3/section65.2-302/ [VERIFIED as quoted]
- [HEDGE: whether a one-time owner-builder is "in the trade or business" of
  construction is a case-law question; Virginia courts have generally held
  homeowners building for themselves are not, but that conclusion is not in
  the statute. Print the risk-management advice — require every sub to carry
  its own workers' comp certificate — rather than a legal assurance.]
- [HEDGE: no Virginia statute was found requiring workers'-comp certification
  as a condition of a residential building permit (NC's G.S. 87-14(a)(2) has
  no Virginia twin; § 2.2-4332 covers public contracts only). Localities may
  still put a workers'-comp question on their application forms.]

### 10. Liability for using unlicensed subs
- Claim: Knowing violations of the licensing chapter are Class 1 misdemeanors,
  and an unlicensed contractor generally cannot enforce his contract — but the
  owner-builder should not rely on that as protection.
- Citation: § 54.1-1115(A)–(C); § 54.1-1115.01.
- Verbatim (§ 54.1-1115(C)): "A construction contract entered into by a person
  undertaking work without a valid Virginia contractor's license shall not be
  enforceable by the unlicensed contractor undertaking the work unless the
  unlicensed contractor (i) gives substantial performance within the terms of
  the contract in good faith and (ii) did not have actual knowledge that a
  license or certificate was required"
- Verbatim (§ 54.1-1115.01): "Any contractor that directly employs or
  otherwise contracts with a person who is not credentialed by the Board for
  work requiring a credential under this chapter shall be solely responsible
  for any monetary penalty or other sanction resulting from the act of
  employing or contracting with a person who lacks the proper credential ..."
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title54.1/chapter11/section54.1-1115/
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title54.1/chapter11/section54.1-1115.01/ [VERIFIED]
- Verification step for the buyer: DPOR license lookup at
  https://www.dpor.virginia.gov/ (License Lookup).

## VA.2 — Permit Application Checklist

### 1. Which code edition is in force (August 2026)
- Claim: The 2021 USBC (13VAC5-63) took effect January 18, 2024. During the
  first year applicants could choose the 2018 or 2021 edition; since
  January 18, 2025, all permits issue under the 2021 USBC.
- Citation: 13VAC5-63; DHCD codes page.
- Verbatim (DHCD): "The effective date of the 2021 Uniform Statewide Building
  Code, 2021 Statewide Fire Prevention Code, 2021 Industrialized Building
  Safety Regulations and the 2021 Amusement Device Regulations is Jan. 18,
  2024."
- https://www.dhcd.virginia.gov/codes ; https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title13/agency5/chapter63/ [VERIFIED]

### 2. What the 2021 USBC incorporates
- Claim: The 2021 USBC is built on the 2021 IBC, 2021 IRC, 2021 IECC, and the
  2020 National Electrical Code (NFPA 70-20).
- Citation: 13VAC5-63 Documents Incorporated by Reference.
- Verbatim: IBC/IRC/IECC "2021 Edition"; "NFPA 70-20, National Electrical
  Code."
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title13/agency5/chapter63/section9999/ [VERIFIED]

### 3. The 2024 cycle is underway but NOT in force
- Claim: The Board of Housing approved the NOIRA for the 2024 Code Development
  Cycle on March 10, 2025; the cdpVA proposal window opened April 1, 2025.
  DHCD's own staff guidance says the 2024-based VRC "will likely be effective
  in 2027." Nothing about the 2024 cycle changes a permit pulled in 2026.
- Citation: DHCD 2024 code development cycle pages; DHCD 2021 VRC FAQ
  (updated 2025-01-08).
- Verbatim (DHCD FAQ): "The process to update the VRC by incorporating the
  2024 I-Codes, including the 2024 IRC is anticipated to begin in the Spring
  of 2025 and the 2024 VRC will likely be effective in 2027."
- https://www.dhcd.virginia.gov/sites/default/files/DocX/technical-memos-bulletins/2021-vrc-faq.pdf [VERIFIED]

### 4. Residential energy — ceiling insulation jumped to R-60
- Claim: Under the 2021 VRC, minimum ceiling insulation in Climate Zones 3 and
  4 rose from R-49 to R-60 (VRC N1102.1.3) — DHCD confirms in writing, and
  notes the 2024 IRC later walked it back, but R-60 governs unless the local
  official approves a code modification under VCC 106.3.
- Citation: VRC § N1102.1.3; DHCD 2021 VRC FAQ.
- Verbatim (DHCD FAQ): "The minimum insulation R-Value for ceilings in Climate
  Zones 3 and 4 increased from 49 in the 2018 VRC, to 60 in the 2021 VRC."
- Same FAQ URL as #3. [VERIFIED]

### 5. Residential energy — blower door is mandatory
- Claim: Every new dwelling must be blower-door tested — the visual-inspection
  option was eliminated back in the 2018 VECC — with a maximum leakage of
  5 ACH50 (a Virginia amendment; the unamended 2021 IECC would demand 3.0).
  Virginia names who may run the test.
- Citation: 13VAC5-63-264 (energy chapter amendments), provision R402.4.1.2 /
  R402.4.1.3 (VRC N1102.4.1.2/.3).
- Verbatim (R402.4.1.2): "The building or dwelling unit shall be tested and
  verified as having an air leakage rate not exceeding five air changes per
  hour in Climate Zone 4."
- Verbatim (testers): testing may be conducted by "a Virginia licensed general
  contractor, a Virginia licensed HVAC contractor, a Virginia licensed home
  inspector, a Virginia registered design professional, a certified BPI
  Envelope Professional, a certified HERS rater, or a certified duct and
  envelope tightness rater."
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title13/agency5/chapter63/section264/ [VERIFIED]
- [HEDGE: Virginia now spans Climate Zones 3 and 4 (DHCD FAQ discusses zones
  3 and 4; the 2021 maps moved ~19 southeastern localities to 3A). The
  companion provision R402.4.1.3 extends the 5-ACH50 rate across zones 3–5 per
  the VAC text, but the exact zone phrasing of R402.4.1.3 could not be pulled
  cleanly — re-verify the sentence in 13VAC5-63-264 before print.]

### 6. Residential energy — duct testing
- Claim: Duct leakage testing has been required in Virginia since the 2018
  VECC and continues under the 2021 code (provisions around R403.3.3–R403.3.5
  in 13VAC5-63-264; a written, signed test report goes to the code official).
- Verbatim (R403.3.5 fragment): "A written report of the results of the test
  shall be signed by the party conducting the test and provided to the code
  official."
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title13/agency5/chapter63/section264/ [VERIFIED as to the report sentence]
- [HEDGE: the full duct-testing trigger sentence and its exceptions (notably
  whether ducts + air handler entirely inside the thermal envelope are exempt
  from testing under the Virginia amendments) could not be extracted verbatim
  from 13VAC5-63-264. Before print, pull R403.3.3–R403.3.5 from the section
  text directly. Do NOT print "ducts inside conditioned space are exempt"
  without that check.]

### 7. What a complete application is, and who may file it
- Claim: A permit must be obtained before work begins; the owner may apply
  personally — the applicant may be owner, lessee, their agent, the RDP,
  contractor, or subcontractor.
- Citation: USBC § 108.1 (13VAC5-63-80).
- Verbatim: "Application for a permit shall be made to the building official
  and a permit shall be obtained prior to the commencement of any of the
  following activities" — and the application may be filed by "the owner or
  lessee of the relevant property or the agent of either or by the RDP,
  contractor, or subcontractor associated with the work or any of their
  agents."
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title13/agency5/chapter63/section80/ [VERIFIED]
- Small-structure exemptions exist (e.g., detached sheds: "One story detached
  structures used as tool and storage sheds, playhouses, or similar uses,
  provided the building area does not exceed 256 square feet" — § 108.2).
  [VERIFIED, same URL]

### 8. Plans without an architect's seal; review clock; permit life
- Sealed plans: § 54.1-402(A)(1) lets non-licensees prepare plans for
  "Single- and two-family homes, townhouses, and multifamily dwellings,
  excluding electrical and mechanical systems, not exceeding three stories"
  (plans must identify the author and occupation).
  https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title54.1/chapter4/section54.1-402/ [VERIFIED]
- Review clock: USBC § 110.1 requires the building official to examine the
  application "within a reasonable time after filing" — Virginia sets no
  fixed statutory day-count for residential plan review (contrast NC's
  15-business-day clock). 13VAC5-63-100. [VERIFIED as quoted; the absence
  claim is a careful negative — phrase as "the USBC sets no fixed deadline."]
- Permit abandonment: USBC § 110.6: "A permit shall be considered abandoned if
  work is not commenced within six months after issuance or if authorized work
  is suspended or abandoned for a period of six months." Extensions up to one
  year per request. https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title13/agency5/chapter63/section100/ [VERIFIED]
- Completion limit: USBC § 110.7 gives single-family dwelling permits a
  three-year completion window from issuance, extendable on a showing of
  substantive progress (approved inspections). [HEDGE: paraphrase from the
  section — pull the exact § 110.7 sentence before print.]

### 9. Septic gates the building permit (unsewered lots)
- Claim: The building official may hold the building permit until VDH approval
  of the onsite sewage system is supplied; VDH may not be bypassed.
- Citation: USBC § 103.5 (13VAC5-63-30); § 32.1-164(B)(1).
- Verbatim (USBC 103.5): the building official may "refuse to issue a permit
  until the applicant has supplied certificates of functional design approval
  from the appropriate state agency" (water supply and sewage disposal are on
  the § 103.5 functional-design list).
- Verbatim (§ 32.1-164(B)(1)): "A requirement that the owner obtain a permit
  from the Commissioner prior to the construction, installation, modification
  or operation of a sewerage system or treatment works ..."
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title13/agency5/chapter63/section30/
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title32.1/chapter6/section32.1-164/ [VERIFIED]

### 10. The septic permit itself
- Claim: No septic construction without a written VDH permit; the construction
  permit dies after 18 months; an operation permit follows final inspection.
- Citation: 12VAC5-610-240; 12VAC5-610-300(A); 12VAC5-610-340; applications go
  to the local health department (12VAC5-610-250).
- Verbatim (240): "No person or owner shall construct, operate, expand or
  modify a sewage disposal or handling system without a written permit from
  the commissioner."
- Verbatim (300 A): "All sewage disposal construction permits are null and
  void when ... more than 18 months elapse from the date the permit was
  issued."
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincodefull/title12/agency5/chapter610/ [VERIFIED]
- Alternative systems are governed by 12VAC5-613 (Alternative Onsite Sewage
  System Regulations). [VERIFIED existence; cite without detail]

### 11. Private-sector soil work (OSE/PE) and the shot clock
- Claim: VDH MUST accept private site evaluations and designs from a licensed
  onsite soil evaluator (OSE) or a PE consulting one, and must act within 15
  working days on a single-lot submission — or the design is DEEMED APPROVED.
- Citation: § 32.1-163.5; § 32.1-164(G).
- Verbatim: "the Board, Commissioner, and Department of Health shall accept
  private site evaluations and designs, in compliance with the Board's
  regulations ... designed and certified by a licensed professional engineer,
  in consultation with a licensed onsite soil evaluator, or by a licensed
  onsite soil evaluator."
- Verbatim (deadline): "Within 15 working days from the date of written
  submission ... the Department shall (i) issue the requested letter, permit
  or approval or (ii) set forth in writing the specific reasons for denial" —
  and if missed, "the designs, evaluations or subdivision reviews shall be
  deemed approved and the appropriate letter, permit or approval shall be
  issued."
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title32.1/chapter6/section32.1-163.5/ [VERIFIED]

### 12. Private wells
- Claim: A written VDH construction permit is required before drilling,
  altering, or deepening a private well; the owner or agent applies at the

- Citation: 12VAC5-630-220(B); -230(A); -330 (regulations amended effective

- Verbatim: "After November 6, 2024, no person shall construct, alter,
  abandon, or increase the depth of a private well ... without a written

- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincodefull/title12/agency5/chapter630/ [VERIFIED]
- [HEDGE: water-quality sampling for new drinking wells sits in

### 13. Erosion & stormwater (VESMP) — the thresholds and the owner-builder shortcut
- Claim: Under the consolidated Virginia Erosion and Stormwater Management Act
  (administered locally under DEQ oversight; regulation 9VAC25-875 effective
  July 1, 2024), land disturbance of 10,000 sq ft or more triggers erosion
  control — 2,500 sq ft for single-family work in a Chesapeake Bay
  Preservation Area — and NO land-disturbing activity may begin until the
  local authority issues land-disturbance approval. For a single detached
  house, an "agreement in lieu of a plan" replaces the engineered plan.
- Citations: § 62.1-44.15:34(A); § 62.1-44.15:24 (definition); 9VAC25-875-530(B).
- Verbatim (prohibition): "A person shall not conduct any land-disturbing
  activity until (i) he has submitted to the appropriate VESMP authority an
  application that includes a permit registration statement, if required, a
  soil erosion control and stormwater management plan or an executed agreement
  in lieu of a plan, if required, and (ii) the VESMP authority has issued its
  land-disturbance approval."
- Verbatim (10,000): "Soil erosion control requirements and water quantity
  technical criteria adopted pursuant to this article shall apply to any
  activity that disturbs 10,000 square feet or more."
- Verbatim (CBPA single-family): "For land-disturbing activities for
  single-family detached residential structures, soil erosion control and
  water quantity technical criteria shall apply to any land-disturbing
  activity that disturbs 2,500 square feet or more of land."
- Verbatim (AILOP definition, § 62.1-44.15:24): a contract between the VESMP
  authority and the owner specifying compliance methods for "a single-family
  detached residential structure" (or low-impervious farm structure).
- Verbatim (9VAC25-875-530(B)): "For a single family detached residential
  structure with less than one acre of land disturbance, an agreement in lieu
  of a plan may be used when either (a) it is located within a common plan of
  development or sale with an approved stormwater pollution prevention plan
  consistent with 9VAC25-875-500 and a permit, if required; or (b) the
  single-family detached residential is located outside of a common plan of
  development or sale."
- The authority "may waive the Responsible Land Disturber certificate
  requirement for an agreement in lieu of a plan" (§ 62.1-44.15:34).
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/62.1-44.15:34/
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title62.1/chapter3.1/section62.1-44.15:24/
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title9/agency25/chapter875/section530/ [VERIFIED]

### 14. VDOT entrance permit for a driveway onto a state road
- Claim: Outside incorporated cities and towns, most Virginia public roads are
  VDOT-maintained (Virginia is one of the few states where counties generally
  do not own roads). A new driveway connection to one requires VDOT approval
  and an entrance permit BEFORE construction; VDOT district staff issue them.
- Citations: 24VAC30-73-60(A); 24VAC30-73-20(C); definition in 24VAC30-73-10;
  § 33.2-240.
- Verbatim (the gate): "No entrance of any nature may be constructed within
  the right-of-way until the location has been approved by VDOT and an
  entrance permit has been issued."

### 16. Mechanics' lien agent line on the permit
- Claim: Every building permit for a one- or two-family dwelling must carry
  either the mechanics' lien agent's name, address and phone — included at
  the APPLICANT's request — or the words "None Designated."
- Citation: § 36-98.01 (echoed in USBC § 108.5).
- Verbatim (entire operative text): "In addition to any information required
  by the Uniform Statewide Building Code, a building permit issued for any
  one- or two-family residential dwelling unit shall at the time of issuance
  contain, at the request of the applicant, the name, mailing address, and
  telephone number of the mechanics' lien agent as defined in § 43-1. If the
  designation of a mechanics' lien agent is not so requested by the applicant,
  the building permit shall at the time of issuance state that none has been
  designated with the words 'None Designated.'"
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/36-98.01/ [VERIFIED]
- Contrast for the kit: in NC the lien agent is mandatory at $40,000+; in
  Virginia it is the owner's OPTION — but a smart one (see #18).

### 17. Who can be the lien agent
- Citation: § 43-1 (definition).
- Verbatim: "Such person shall be an attorney at law licensed to practice in

  deposits ..."
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title43/chapter1/section43-1/ [VERIFIED]

### 18. Why designating one protects the owner
- Claim: If a lien agent is on the permit, anyone wanting lien rights must
  notify the agent within 30 days of first furnishing labor or materials;
  late notice limits the lien to work performed on or after the date notice is
  finally given.
- Citation: § 43-4.01.
- Verbatim: notice is due "within 30 days of the first date that he performs
  labor or furnishes material"; failure "shall not bar a person from claiming
  a lien" but "such lien is limited to labor performed or materials furnished
  on or after the date a notice is given."
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title43/chapter1/section43-4.01/ [VERIFIED]

### 19. Asbestos / lead statements — NOT applicable to new construction
- Claim: Virginia's permit-time asbestos certification (§ 36-99.7) applies
  only to renovation/demolition of buildings whose initial permit predates
  January 1, 1985 — and single-family dwellings are excepted even then. Drop
  from the new-construction checklist; note only for buyers demolishing an old
  house on the lot first.
- Verbatim: "A local building department shall not issue a building permit
  allowing a building for which an initial building permit was issued before
  January 1, 1985, to be renovated or demolished until the local building
  department receives certification from the owner or his agent that the
  affected portions of the building have been inspected for the presence of
  asbestos."
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title36/chapter6/section36-99.7/ [VERIFIED — include only as a demolition footnote]

## VA.3 — Inspection Sequence

### 1. Nothing counts until it is inspected and approved
- Citation: USBC § 113.1 (13VAC5-63-130).

1. "Inspection of footing excavations and reinforcement material for concrete
   footings prior to the placement of concrete."
2. "Inspection of foundation systems during phases of construction necessary
   to ensure compliance with this code."
3. "Inspection of preparatory work prior to the placement of concrete."
4. "Inspection of structural members and fasteners prior to concealment."
5. "Inspection of electrical, mechanical and plumbing materials, equipment,
   and systems prior to concealment."
6. "Inspection of energy conservation material prior to concealment."
7. "Final inspection."
- https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title13/agency5/chapter63/section130/ [VERIFIED]

  the blower-door/duct test reports (VA.2 #5–#6) land at final.
