# Michigan Owner-Builder Dossier — research for the MI Permit Kit (six documents)

Compiled August 2026. Statute text was read from the OFFICIAL Michigan Compiled Laws at legislature.mi.gov
(pattern: `legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-339-2403`), which states the MCL is complete through PA 91
of 2026. Administrative rules were read from the OFFICIAL Michigan Administrative Code PDFs at
ars.apps.lara.state.mi.us (pattern: `/AdminCode/DownloadAdminCodeFile?FileName=R%20408.30500%20%20to%20408.30547g.pdf`).
Agency facts, forms, fee schedules and the jurisdiction list were read from LARA Bureau of Construction Codes and
EGLE pages and PDFs directly. Every claim is tagged:

  [V] = verified against a primary source (statute or rule text read directly, or the administering agency's own
  page/form). Safe to print with the citation.
  [H] = hedge — could not be pinned to a primary source, is locally variable, or is a proven-negative that can only
  be stated carefully. Print only with the hedge language and a verification step.

WARNING FOR FUTURE EDITORS — THREE CITATION MINEFIELDS. Michigan has repealed more law than most states and the
dead citations are still everywhere online.

  1. **2016 PA 407 (Skilled Trades Regulation Act) repealed all three trade acts effective April 4, 2017.**
     DEAD: MCL 338.881–338.892 (Electrical Administrative Act — including the famous MCL 338.883 homeowner
     exemption), MCL 338.3511–338.3569 (State Plumbing Act), MCL 338.971–338.988 (Forbes Mechanical Contractors
     Act). Any guide citing MCL 338.883 is quoting dead law. Live citations are MCL 339.5737(3)(g), MCL
     339.6107(2)(d), MCL 339.5819. Note MCL 339.5109(5) keeps old cross-references resolving, but the citations
     themselves should never appear in a kit.
  2. **1999 PA 245 gutted the middle of Act 230 effective July 31, 2001.** REPEALED: MCL 125.1502, 125.1503,
     125.1508, 125.1509, 125.1509a. The section numbered 125.1509 today is "Contract with private organization,"
     a completely different subject added by 2012 PA 103. Anything citing MCL 125.1509 for enforcement
     responsibility is quoting dead law — the live sections are 125.1508a and 125.1508b. MCL 125.1524 still
     contains a dead cross-reference to "section 8"; do not reproduce that clause without flagging it.
  3. **MCL 339.2011 is the PUBLIC WORKS seal rule ($15,000), not the residential one.** The residential
     3,500 sq ft figure lives in MCL 339.2012(1)(d) (exemption from the architecture article) and MCL 339.2014(e)
     (exemption from the prohibition on filing unsealed plans). Cite both; they do different jobs.

THE STRUCTURAL HEADLINE (this is the kit's thesis) — THE MICHIGAN SPLIT: Michigan is the state where the rules are
uniform and the *government* is not. One statewide construction code binds everywhere, with no local amendment and
no unregulated county. But its administration is split four ways on every parcel — building, electrical, mechanical
and plumbing are each separately assigned to a local unit, a county, or the State — and **the State is the default**,
not the backstop. Locals opt IN by ordinance. The practical consequence, which no competing Michigan guide states:
your building permit and your plumbing permit can come from two different governments, and your framing inspection
is gated on the other government's approval.

## MI.0 — Cover summary facts (the split, in headlines)

1. [V] The State is the default enforcing agency. MCL 125.1508b(1): "Except as otherwise provided in this section,
   **the director is responsible** for administration and enforcement of this act and the code. A governmental
   subdivision **may by ordinance assume** responsibility for administration and enforcement of this act within its
   political boundary." "Director" = director of LARA (MCL 125.1502a(1)(q), (r)). This inverts how every summary
   describes it ("LARA covers areas with no local department").

2. [V] Four disciplines, assigned separately. LARA BCC *Statewide Jurisdiction List*, explanatory page: "There are
   three (3) levels of jurisdiction (state, county, local) for each of the four (4) code disciplines (building,
   electrical, mechanical, and plumbing)." Levels defined verbatim: COUNTY = "that unit of government receives code
   enforcement services from a county enforcing agency"; LOCAL = "that unit of government has their own program";
   STATE = "the Bureau of Construction Codes is responsible for code enforcement in that unit of government."

3. [V] The counts, derived by tallying revision 8/21/2026 of the List (read 27 August 2026): **1,824 units of
   government across all 83 counties; 252 (13.8%) have at least one discipline assigned to a different agency than
   the others.** Per-discipline STATE counts: building 59, electrical 184, mechanical 241, plumbing 264.
   [H] Print these as "a picture of how common the problem is, not as a lookup," because the List is revised after
   every Construction Code Commission meeting. A second independent parse (different parser, 1,778 of ~1,856 rows)
   returned building 59 / electrical 184 / mechanical 240 / plumbing 262 — agreement to within 2 on every
   discipline, which is good corroboration. The kit prints the first parse.

4. [V] It changes, and it changed this year. List explanatory page: updated "after every Construction Code
   Commission meeting to show changes; i.e. units of government which have newly assumed responsibility or those
   who have returned responsibility to the state or county level." Live example on the current revision: every unit
   in **Luce County** — the county, the Village of Newberry, and its townships — shows STATE for all four
   disciplines effective **01/01/2026**. Whole-county STATE for building also appears in Baraga, Mackinac,
   Manistee and Ontonagon.

5. [V] No unregulated corner, with two real carve-outs. MCL 125.1508a(1): "This act and the code apply throughout
   the state." MCL 125.1524 repeals local construction regulations. BUT: MCL 125.1510(8) exempts a building
   "incidental to the use for agricultural purposes of the land" not used in retail trade (the exemption is also
   definitional, at MCL 125.1502a(1)(g) and (bb)), and R 408.30505 exempts detached accessory structures ≤200 sq ft
   and similar. Zoning is never preempted — MCL 125.1502a(1)(o) excludes zoning from "construction regulation."

## MI.1 — The exemption (licensure), and the three trade exemptions stacked on it

6. [V] The builder exemption exists TWICE, and most guides quote only one.
   - Outside the definition: MCL 339.2401(a)(iii) — "residential builder" includes "a person that erects a
     residential structure **except for the person's own use and occupancy on the person's property**."
   - Express exemption: MCL 339.2403(b) — "Notwithstanding article 6, a person may engage in the business of or act
     in the capacity of a residential builder … without a license under this article, if the person is … **an owner
     of property, with reference to a structure on the property for the owner's own use and occupancy**."

7. [V] NO threshold, NO holding period, NO not-for-sale window. Article 24 (MCL 339.2401–339.2412) was read in full;
   there is no project-cost trigger and no time condition. Independently corroborated: all 154 compiled sections of
   2016 PA 407 were downloaded and text-searched for "for sale / offered for sale / sell / lease / rental / within
   N months / within N years" — no provision anywhere conditions any homeowner exemption on not selling or renting
   within any period. **State this affirmatively; it is a real differentiator.**
   [H] TRAP FOR EDITORS: the only 18-month figure nearby is MCL 339.2411(1), a complaint-filing limitations period
   against a LICENSEE. Do not let it drift into the exemption discussion.

8. [V] The penalty everyone gets wrong. MCL 339.601(4) sets the general Occupational Code penalty at "not more than
   $500.00, or imprisonment for not more than 90 days." MCL 339.601(6) begins "**Notwithstanding subsections (4)
   and (5)**" and carves residential builders out: first offense "a fine of not less than $5,000.00 or more than
   $25,000.00, or imprisonment for not more than 1 year, or both"; second offense two years; an offense "that
   causes death or serious injury" is a **felony**, up to four years. Separately MCL 339.2412(1) bars suit for
   compensation without alleging and proving licensure, (3) bars liens, and (4) allows a prosecutor or the AG to
   seek $5,000–$25,000 civil. The live state guide currently prints the $500/90-day figure and attributes it to
   MCL 339.2412 — wrong statute AND wrong numbers.

9. [V] Trade exemptions — three different rules, do not flatten them.
   - **Electrical, MCL 339.5737(3)(g):** no license for "any installation, alteration, or repair of electrical
     equipment by a homeowner in a **single family home and accompanying outbuildings** owned and occupied **or to
     be occupied** by **the individual who is performing** the installation, alteration, or repair." Widest scope;
     "or to be occupied" expressly reaches new construction. Permit still required: MCL 339.5731(1) — the "Except
     as otherwise provided in section 737" qualifier attaches only to the licensure sentence, not the permit
     sentence.
     [H] Enacted-text defect: the Legislature's own compiler's note says the chapeau's "A licensee" "evidently
     should read 'A license'." Quote verbatim if quoting.
   - **Plumbing, MCL 339.6107(2)(d):** no license for "the installation by a homeowner of his or her own plumbing,
     building sewer, or private sewer in his or her single-family dwelling **if a permit is secured**." NOTE WHAT IS
     ABSENT: **no occupancy condition at all.** Do not import one. MCL 339.6125(2) makes the homeowner a
     statutorily authorized permit holder.
   - **Mechanical, MCL 339.5819:** MUCH narrower — reaches only "a **heating or refrigerating system**," not
     mechanical work generally. Requires own + occupy (or will), **personal** installation, a sworn affirmation in
     the permit application, permits from the municipal enforcing agency, and a post-installation inspection.
     [H] Enacted text is garbled ("in which the installation is done is completed"); reproduce verbatim if quoting.

10. [V] THE TRADE TRAP — the electrical exemption is defeasible by local ordinance; plumbing and mechanical are
    not. MCL 339.5733(1): article 7 "does not apply in the jurisdiction of a municipality that adopts or has
    adopted an ordinance" meeting four tests (standards at least as stringent, substantially similar enforcement,
    comparable penalties, inspection of electrical wiring). MCL 339.5733(2) lists the only classes such an
    ordinance may not require a license or permit for — section 737(3)**(c), (d), (e), or (f)**. The homeowner
    exemption is **737(3)(g)**, conspicuously absent. By contrast MCL 339.6125(1): "A governmental subdivision may
    not exempt itself from the licensing requirements of this article and may not engage in or require local
    licensing"; MCL 339.5811 bars local mechanical licensing. **No competing Michigan guide states this asymmetry.**

11. [V] The $600 exemption is not the owner-builder's. MCL 339.2403(f) exempts a project under $600 aggregate, then
    kills the workaround: it "does not apply if the work of a construction is only a part of a larger or major
    operation … or in which a division of the operation is made in contracts of amounts less than $600.00, **to
    evade this act**."

12. [V] What you actually sign. MCL 125.1510(1) requires of EVERY applicant "a detailed statement in writing,
    **verified by affidavit of the individual making it**, of the specifications" plus full plans to scale and a
    site plan naming the owner in fee. This is a PLANS affidavit, not an occupancy affidavit. All 53 sections of
    Act 230 were grepped for affidavit/attest/sworn/notariz/own use/for sale/homeowner — **no homeowner occupancy
    affidavit exists in Act 230**, confirmed against LARA's BCC-324, whose attestation block covers only that the
    statements/specifications/plans "are true and complete" and that the signer is authorized under 125.1510(2).
    [V] THE ONE REAL EXCEPTION: MCL 339.5819 does require a homeowner occupancy affirmation — on the MECHANICAL
    permit application only. The kit must not say "Michigan has no statutory homeowner affidavit" flatly.
    [H] A local unit may still impose its own homeowner affidavit by ordinance under MCL 125.1508b(11).

13. [V] The anti-circumvention machinery behind "a licensed contractor may not work under your homeowner permit."
    MCL 125.1510(4) requires this sentence in 8-point boldface immediately above the signature: "Section 23a of the
    state construction code act of 1972, 1972 PA 230, MCL 125.1523a, prohibits a person from conspiring to
    circumvent the licensing requirements of this state relating to persons who are to perform work on a
    residential building or a residential structure. Violators of section 23a are subjected to civil fines."
    MCL 125.1523a: fine "not less than $100.00 or more than $500.00," enforced by the county prosecutor or AG.
    MCL 125.1510(2): nobody is recognized as your agent/attorney/engineer/architect/builder unless a written
    contract, power of attorney or letter of authorization is FILED with the enforcing agency giving their license
    number and expiry. MCL 125.1510(3): a licensed (or required-to-be-licensed) residential applicant must give
    license number, workers' comp carrier, EIN and UIA number.
    [V] LARA states the operating rule itself for mechanical: "a homeowner must secure a permit for work they are
    performing at their residence, and mechanical contractors are responsible for permits on work they are
    performing on behalf of an owner." Use this instead of a city handout.

## MI.2 — Application, the five approvals, the seal trap, code editions, fees

14. [V] The five approval gates come from the State's own form. BCC-324 (04/2024) p.3 carries an "Environmental
    Control Approvals" block: **A Zoning · B Fire District · C Health Department · D Soil Erosion · E Flood Zone**,
    each with Required?/Approved/Date/Number/By. Best organizing device available for a Michigan checklist.

15. [V] THE SEAL TRAP — the 3,500 sq ft figure is real; the measure is not what anyone assumes.
    - The prohibition: MCL 339.2014(e) — it is a violation to submit unsealed plans, and "this subdivision does not
      apply to a public work costing less than $15,000.00 or **a residential building containing not more than
      3,500 square feet of calculated floor area**." The seal duty it enforces is MCL 339.2007(2).
    - The design exemption: MCL 339.2012(1)(d) — a person not licensed may plan/design/direct construction of "a
      detached 1- and 2-family residence building not exceeding 3,500 square feet in calculated floor area."
    - **THE DEFINITION, MCL 339.2012(2):** "'Calculated floor area' means that portion of the total gross area
      measured to the outside surfaces of exterior walls intended to be **habitable space**." And: "'Habitable
      space' means space in a building used for living, sleeping, eating, or cooking. Habitable space **does not
      include** a heater or utility room, a crawl space, a basement, an attic, a garage, an open porch, a balcony,
      a terrace, a court, a deck, a bathroom, a toilet room, a closet, a hallway, a storage space, and other
      similar spaces not used for living, sleeping, eating, or cooking."
      A house marketed at 4,200 sq ft can sit comfortably under the threshold. This is the kit's headline trap.
    - [V] SECOND, UNCAPPED EXEMPTION: MCL 339.2012(1)(c) — "An owner doing architectural, engineering, or surveying
      work upon or in connection with the construction of a building on the owner's property for the owner's own
      use to which employees and the public are not generally to have access." No area limit.
    - [V] LARA's own form states it: "The seal and signature is not required for one- and two-family dwellings less
      than 3,500 square feet of calculated floor area and public works less than $15,000 in total construction
      cost."

16. [V] CODE EDITIONS — the second trap. LARA's own published notice, "2021 Code Injunction — Residential Part 5
    and Energy Part 10," still linked from the Bureau's Part 5 and Part 10 entries when read August 2026: on
    **July 7, 2025** the Michigan Court of Claims issued a stipulated order in *Home Builders Assoc et al. v LARA
    et al.* temporarily preventing LARA from "taking additional steps necessary to apply or implement" two rule
    sets that would have adopted the 2021 IRC and 2021 IECC residential provisions and "were set to become
    effective on **August 29, 2025**." Consequence in LARA's words: "While this stipulated order remains in place,
    the **2015** versions of the IRC and residential provisions of the IECC standards … will remain valid and in
    effect." The two rule sets (2022-16-LR Part 5; 2021-48 LR Part 10) were still listed among the Bureau's
    currently-open rules at that date. This is why "adopted codes by state" tables show Michigan on 2021.

17. [V] Residential Code adopting rule: **R 408.30500** — "The provisions of the international residential code,
    **2015 edition**, including appendices A, B, C, D, E, F, G, J, K, N, O, P, R, and S except for Sections
    R 104.2, R 104.3, R 104.5, R 104.7, R 104.8, R 104.8.1, R 105.3, R 105.3.1, R 105.3.2, R 105.6, R 105.9,
    R 108.3, R 108.4, R 108.5, R 108.6, **R 109.1**, R 112.2, R 112.3, R 112.4, R 113.1 to R 113.3, R 113.4,
    R 114.1 and R 114.2, R 313.1.1 to R 313.2.1, … E3902.15, E3902.16, E3902.17, and AJ102.4, the IBC-2015,
    IECC-2015, IMC-2015, IPC-2015, **NFPA 70-2014** listed in chapter 44 govern…". History: 2015 AACS; 2021 AACS;
    2022 MR 2, Eff. Feb. 8, 2022.
    [H] TWO DATES, DO NOT CONFLATE: Feb 8 **2016** is the base adoption of the 2015 MRC per LARA's Code Books page;
    Feb 8 **2022** is when the current wording of R 408.30500 was last amended.
    [H] DO NOT WRITE "Michigan deletes IRC Chapter 1." It does not. R105.1 (permit required), R109 and R110 are all
    KEPT; only the enforcement/fee/appeal/violation/stop-work machinery is struck, because the Act supplies it.
    Precedence rule, R 408.30501b amending R101.3: "The Stille-DeRossett-Hale single state construction code act …
    **takes precedence over all provisions of this code**."

18. [V] Residential energy is **Part 10**, R 408.31059: "The **residential provisions of the international energy
    conservation code, 2015 edition**, except for sections R107.2 to R107.5, R301.2, R301.3, R402.3.2, and Table
    R303.1.3(3), govern…". MRC cross-reference at R 408.30524.
    **DO NOT CITE PART 10a** — R 408.31087 et seq. is the COMMERCIAL energy code (2021 IECC + ASHRAE 90.1-2019,
    eff. Apr 22, 2025) and expressly excludes residential buildings.
    - [V] Climate zones: **5A / 6A / 7 only**, county table at R 408.31060e Table 301.1, matched by MRC Table
      N1101.10 at R 408.30547c. Zone 7 = ten UP counties (Baraga, Chippewa, Gogebic, Houghton, Iron, Keweenaw,
      Luce, Mackinac, Ontonagon, Schoolcraft); 5A = 35 southern counties; 6A = 38.
    - [V] **Duct leakage 4 cfm @ 25 Pa per 100 sq ft — labeled "(mandatory)"** (R 408.31066 / N1103.2.2). Rough-in
      4 cfm, or 3 cfm with no air handler installed. Real exemption: "the total leakage test is not required for
      ducts and air handlers located entirely within the building thermal envelope."
    - [V] **Air leakage 4 ACH50 — labeled "(prescriptive)"** (R 408.31069 / N1102.4.1.2). Base 2015 IECC says
      3 ACH50 "(Mandatory)"; Michigan changed BOTH the number and the status. **The asymmetry — ducts mandatory,
      blower door prescriptive — trips up almost every summary.**

19. [V] SEPTIC — the mechanism, not just the agency. Michigan has **no statewide sanitary code** for onsite
    wastewater. A house system is lawful as a groundwater discharge needing no state permit under Mich. Admin. Code
    **R 323.2210(a)(i)**: sanitary sewage may be discharged without a Part 31 permit where "the discharge is less
    than 1,000 gallons per day and the disposal system is approved by the county, district, or city health
    department that has jurisdiction in accordance with **either the requirements of the local sanitary code or**
    the provisions of the publication entitled 'Michigan Criteria for Subsurface Sewage Disposal,' April 1994."
    That either/or IS the product point. (R 323.2210(a)(ii) covers <6,000 gpd and requires the MCSSD specifically.)
    - [V] Local authority: MCL 333.2441 — local health department regulations "shall be at least as stringent as
      the standard established by state law" and "supersede inconsistent or conflicting local ordinances." Also
      MCL 333.2433(2)(a), 333.2435(d), 333.2473(2). EGLE: onsite wastewater is a required LHD service, and EGLE
      "provides wastewater contract oversight of local health departments" rather than permitting.
    - [V] The MCSSD (April 1994, originally Michigan Department of Public Health, now hosted by EGLE) is by its own
      terms a **guideline** that "does not have the force or effect of law" as to a landowner, but a health
      department that adopted it is expected to follow it and "do[es] not have the authority to approve proposals
      which do not meet this criteria" except by variance. It is a floor: "nothing in these criteria shall prevent
      local health departments … from adopting and enforcing requirements more stringent."
    - [H] **DO NOT PRINT "Michigan is the only state without a septic code" IN THE PRODUCT'S OWN VOICE.** It is a
      49-other-state claim no Michigan source can establish. The Senate Fiscal Agency analysis of SB 771 (2026)
      says only "**According to testimony**, Michigan is the only state that does not have a uniform standard for
      onsite wastewater treatment systems" — that hedge is doing real work. Safe forms: "one of the very few
      states," or attribute it to the SFA explicitly. SB 771 had not passed the Senate as of August 2026; Part 128
      of the Public Health Code does not exist (MCL 333.12801/12802 return HTTP 400).
    - [V] FREE IRONY: Michigan regulates OUTHOUSES statewide (MCL 333.12771, R 325.421 et seq.) but not septic.
    - [H] No statewide septic fee, setback, perc procedure or drainfield sizing figure exists. Print none.

20. [V] SOIL EROSION — the numbers are in the RULES, not Part 91. All of MCL 324.9101–9123 was grepped for "acre"
    and "500 feet": zero hits. Delegated by MCL 324.9104(1) to Mich. Admin. Code Part 17, R 323.1701–R 323.1714.
    - [V] **R 323.1704(1):** a landowner "shall obtain a permit from the appropriate enforcing agency before
      commencing an earth change which disturbs **1 or more acres of land** or which is **within 500 feet of the
      water's edge of a lake or stream**, unless exempted in R 323.1705."
    - [V] **R 323.1701(1)(d):** "lake" requires "a surface area of water that is equal to, or greater than, 1
      acre" — so a small farm pond does not trigger the 500-foot rule.
    - [V] **R 323.1711(2) — THE HARD STOP:** "A local agency or general law township **shall not issue a building
      permit** to a person engaged in an earth change if the change requires a permit under part 91 or these rules
      **until** the county or local enforcing agency has issued the required state-prescribed permit." Cite as a
      RULE; there is no statutory equivalent in Part 91.
    - [V] R 323.1705 exemptions include an earth change "stabilized within 24 hours," normal driveway maintenance
      that does not widen or lengthen, and a discretionary agency waiver on affidavit for a change under **225
      square feet**.
    - [V] Statute layer: county enforces countywide unless a municipality has assumed it (MCL 324.9105(1)); a
      municipal ordinance must be department-approved and may be **more restrictive**, and an unapproved ordinance
      "has no force or effect" (MCL 324.9106(1)–(2)); permit and existing violations transfer with the land with
      signed written notice filed before transfer (MCL 324.9112(2)–(5)); the control duty applies regardless of
      threshold (MCL 324.9116); minor residential activities exempt (MCL 324.9115a(2)).
    - [V] EGLE FAQ closes the builders' loophole: "cutting trees and removing stumps to accommodate future
      development activities is not 'logging' and permits are required."

21. [V] STATE FEES (state-enforced jurisdictions only), BCC Fee Schedule eff. April 1, 2024: to $1,000 = $75 (one
    inspection); $1,000–$10,000 = $75 + $10/$1,000 over; $10,001–$100,000 = $165 + $3/$1,000 over $10,000;
    $100,000–$500,000 = $435 + $2/$1,000 over $100,000; $500,000+ = $1,235 + $3/$1,000 over $500,000. Certificate
    of Occupancy $50 (required for all building permits except demolition). Additional inspection $100. Re-open
    expired permit $75. First $100 non-refundable. Plan review for state-issued permits = 30% of the permit fee.
    Trade permits each carry a $75 non-refundable application fee plus per-item charges.
    - [V] The fee is NOT based on your stated cost: it uses the Bureau's **Square Foot Construction Cost Table**.
      Use group R-3 (one- and two-family) runs IA $122.74 → **VB $95.34** per sq ft across the nine construction
      types; "unfinished basements must be computed separately at 20% of table cost." The live state guide's claim
      that Michigan uses the ICC Building Valuation Data tables is wrong for state-enforced work.
    - [V] Local fees: MCL 125.1522(1) requires them to "bear a reasonable relation to the cost, including
      overhead," and contains a quotable anti-diversion clause — a local unit "shall only use fees generated under
      this section for the operation of the enforcing agency or the construction board of appeals, or both, and
      shall not use the fees for any other purpose."

## MI.3 — Inspections: what Michigan fixes and what it leaves open

22. [V] Michigan strikes IRC **R109.1** — the parent "types of inspections" section — so there is no Michigan
    statutory list of required inspections. The replacement is deliberately open: MCL 125.1512(1), "An enforcing
    agency shall **periodically inspect** all construction undertaken pursuant to a building permit issued by it to
    insure that the construction is performed in accordance with conditions of the building permit and is
    consistent with requirements of the code."
    [H] Note the drafting convention in R 408.30500: single sections are listed as "R 109.1" and ranges as
    "R 113.1 to R 113.3." Only R109.1 itself is struck; the R109.1.x subsections survive.
    [H] DO NOT print the enumerated titles of R109.1.1/.1.2/.1.3/.1.5/.1.6 as Michigan-sourced — they live in the
    2015 IRC book text, which ICC blocks (403 to both curl and WebFetch). Present the sequence as a working order
    confirmed against the permit card.

23. [V] THE ONE ORDERING RULE MICHIGAN DOES FIX — R 408.30509 amending R109.1.4: "Inspection of framing
    construction shall be made after the roof, all framing, firestopping, draftstopping, and bracing are in place
    **and after the plumbing, mechanical, and electrical rough inspections are approved**. Masonry inspections
    shall be made after the completed installation of base course flashing as specified in section R703.7.5 … and
    water-resistive barrier as specified in section R703.2 … and after the masonry construction is completed."
    This inverts what owner-builders expect: framing is LAST of the four, and the rough-ins must be **approved**,
    not merely called. Compounding trap — if one of those permits is a different government's, the gate is on
    another agency's approval.

24. [V] R 408.30509a amending R109.4: work shall not proceed beyond each successive inspection without approval;
    the failure notice "shall include specific reference to the code chapter and section numbers in violation
    **in writing**"; nothing that fails may be covered or concealed until authorized.

25. [V] THE CLOCKS THAT RUN AGAINST THE AGENCY — Michigan is unusually generous and owner-builders never invoke
    these:
    - MCL 125.1511(1): grant or deny "within 10 business days, except that in case of an unusually complicated
      building or structure, action shall be taken within 15 business days. **Failure … shall be deemed a denial**
      of the application for purposes of authorizing the institution of an appeal to the appropriate board of
      appeals." Silence is an appealable denial, not limbo.
    - MCL 125.1512(2): "An inspection shall be made between 8 a.m. and 6 p.m. on business days, or when
      construction is actually being undertaken," except on probable cause of immediate danger or with permission.
      Also: "A person other than the owner, his agent, architect, engineer or builder shall not accompany an
      inspector … except with the consent of an owner."
    - MCL 125.1512(3): before a stop order the agency must give written notice "to appear and show cause why the
      construction should not be stopped"; only on failure to show good cause "within 1 full working day" is the
      order posted.
    - MCL 125.1513: certificate of use and occupancy "**within 5 business days** after receipt of a written
      application therefor" and payment; and "at least **12 hours' notice** of the time of any final inspection."
    [H] There is NO statutory deadline for an inspector to appear after a request. The State publishes a service
    standard on its own forms (respond within 2 business days, inspection typically within 5) which binds nobody.

26. [V] Certificate of USE AND OCCUPANCY (Michigan's statutory name). MCL 125.1513: "A building or structure
    hereafter constructed shall not be used or occupied in whole or in part until a certificate of use and
    occupancy has been issued by the appropriate enforcing agency." Temporary certificate available on request.
    R 408.30510 amending R110.3 fixes seven contents, including "(f) **The edition of the code under which the
    permit was issued**" — worth keeping given how Michigan's edition has moved.

27. [V] PERMIT EXPIRY — 180 days to commence, 180 days of suspension/abandonment, and the sentence people miss,
    printed in capitals on BCC-324 p.3: "A PERMIT WILL BE CLOSED WHEN NO INSPECTIONS ARE REQUESTED AND CONDUCTED
    WITHIN 180 DAYS OF THE DATE OF ISSUANCE OR THE DATE OF A PREVIOUS INSPECTION." The clock resets on
    **inspections**, not effort — which is exactly how a part-time owner-builder loses a permit over a Michigan
    winter.
    [H] SOURCING CAVEAT: there is NO permit expiration anywhere in Act 230 (all 53 sections grepped). The 180-day
    rule is verified from BCC-324 and flows from adopted IRC/IBC 105.5; Michigan's Part 4 rules amend adopted 105.1
    and 105.2 but not 105.5, which implies the ICC text applies as adopted — that is an INFERENCE. Attribute the
    180-day rule to the form, not to a statute.

28. [V] Penalties under the Act, MCL 125.1523(1): misdemeanor, "not more than $500.00 or imprisonment for not more
    than 90 days," for anyone who **knowingly** violates the act/code, builds contrary to a permit condition, fails
    to comply with an order, makes a false statement, refuses entry, or unreasonably interferes with an inspection.
    Note the mens rea is "knowingly" throughout — an honest mistake caught at inspection is not a crime. §23(1)(g)
    and (h) reach officials who wrongly issue or withhold permits, which cuts in the owner's favor. §23(2): a
    separate offense for each DAY of non-compliance with a stop order and each WEEK for any other order. §23(3): a
    local unit may designate violations as municipal civil infractions instead.

29. [V] Michigan-specific technical amendments worth printing in the sequence table:
    - Footing depth, R 408.30522 amending R403.1.4: "All exterior footings and foundation systems shall extend
      **42 inches below actual grade**," with an exception letting the building official MODIFY the depth on
      evidence of freezing degree days, soil type, ground water, snow depth, exposure, or other identified
      conditions.
    - Vapor retarders, R 408.30522a amending R601.3: Class I or II on the interior side of frame walls in zones 5,
      6, 7, 8 and marine 4, with stated exceptions.
    - Equipment grounding, R 408.30536 amending E3908.8.1/.2: flexible metal conduit and liquid-tight flexible
      metal conduit are **not permitted as an equipment grounding conductor** — a real, checkable divergence from
      base NEC 250.118, and the rule text itself is annotated "[Michigan Electrical Code Rules Part 8 250.118
      amended]".

## MI.4 — Who issues the permit (the kit's differentiator)

30. [V] HOW A LOCAL UNIT ASSUMES ENFORCEMENT — two routes.
    - Legacy (already enforcing on Dec 28, 1999), MCL 125.1508b(4): return a notice of intent; the form "shall set
      forth the date return receipt is required, which date shall not be less than 60 days," then registered mail
      gives "15 additional days."
    - New election, MCL 125.1508b(6): pass an ordinance AND "submit, in addition to the ordinance, an application
      to the commission for approval." Commission decides "within 90 days of receipt." Approval standards include
      qualified registered personnel and "that timely field inspection services will be provided."

31. [V] WHAT HAPPENS IF IT DOES NOT — by operation of statute. MCL 125.1508b(4): "If the governmental subdivision
    does not respond by the end of the 15 additional days, it shall be **conclusively presumed** that the
    governmental subdivision does not intend to continue to administer and enforce this act and the code and **the
    director shall assume the responsibility** … unless the county within which the governmental subdivision is
    located submits a notice of intent to continue." Parallel structure at MCL 125.1508a(2). Residual at
    125.1508b(8).

32. [V] COUNTY ACTS FOR ITS TOWNSHIPS — MCL 125.1508b(5): a county already enforcing on Dec 28, 1999 that submits
    its own notice "is responsible for the administration and enforcement … for each governmental subdivision
    within the county that does not submit a notice of intent."

33. [V] DISCONTINUING — 12 months, MCL 125.1508b(7): a subdivision "may rescind that ordinance and transfer the
    responsibility … to the director … However, that action shall not take effect until **12 months** after the
    passage of an ordinance to that effect. **A structure commenced under an effective code shall be completed
    under that code.**" That last sentence is gold for an owner-builder: your permit survives the handoff. Same
    protection on a state withdrawal, MCL 125.1509b(3).

34. [V] THE STATE CAN TAKE IT BACK — MCL 125.1509b: performance evaluation triggered "at the request of the local
    enforcing agency or upon the receipt of a written complaint"; the commission "may issue a notice of intent to
    withdraw the responsibility." A real homeowner remedy worth a sidebar.

35. [V] THE PRIVATE-CONTRACTOR TRAP — MCL 125.1509 (the current section at that number): a governmental subdivision
    may contract with a **private organization** to receive applications, take fees, perform plan reviews and
    inspections using registered personnel, approve temporary utilities, and "process and deliver … Building
    permits [and] Temporary or permanent certificates of use and occupancy." So the person at the counter may work
    for a private firm even where the List says LOCAL. MCL 125.1502a(1)(i) confirms a building official "may also be
    an employee of a private organization." §9(2) requires conflict-of-interest provisions in contracts entered or
    renewed after April 20, 2012 absent a local COI ordinance.

36. [V] DEFINITIONAL TRICK WORTH CALLING OUT — MCL 125.1502a(1)(v): "'Governmental subdivision' means a county,
    city, village, or township **that, in accordance with section 8a or 8b, has assumed responsibility**." A
    township that never assumed enforcement is not a "governmental subdivision" as the Act uses the term. The term
    is opt-in by definition, not a synonym for "local government." "Enforcing agency" at (1)(t); an enforcing
    agency must be registered under article 10 of the skilled trades regulation act and "qualified by experience or
    training" (MCL 125.1508b(3)).

37. [V] Joint enforcement is permitted — MCL 125.1508b(2), "Governmental subdivisions may provide by agreement for
    joint enforcement of this act."

38. [V] Offices the Jurisdiction List does NOT cover, each assigned by a different statute: zoning (township/city/
    village, never preempted); county or multi-county DISTRICT health department (septic, wells); Part 91 soil
    erosion (county unless the municipality assumed it); county road commission (separate body from county
    government) or MDOT on a state trunkline; local fire authority; EGLE for wetlands/inland lakes and streams/
    floodplain/high-risk erosion and critical dunes.
    [V] Official LHD directory: MDHHS locator at michigan.gov/mdhhs/inside-mdhhs/county-offices/lhd (state-run;
    EGLE links to it). Backup: malph.org/resources/directory (trade association, not a state agency).

## MI.5 — Forms, and what needs no permit

39. [V] State form numbers, all Rev. 04/24: **BCC-324** building, **BCC-339** electrical, **BCC-9** mechanical,
    **BCC-327** plumbing. All four carry the §23a warning above the signature and the same 180-day expiry text.
    Online submission at aca-prod.accela.com/lara.

40. [V] Work exempt from permit, R 408.30505 amending R105.2 — the exemption is from the PERMIT only:
    "Exemption from the permit requirements of the code shall not be deemed to grant authorization for any work to
    be done in any manner in violation of the provisions of the code or any other laws or ordinances." Owner-builder
    relevant items: one-story detached accessory structures ≤200 sq ft; fences ≤7 ft; retaining walls ≤4 ft measured
    bottom-of-footing to top-of-wall unless supporting a surcharge; sidewalks and driveways ≤30 in. above adjacent
    grade, not over a basement, not part of an accessible route; detached decks/porches/patios ≤200 sq ft AND ≤30
    in. above grade AND not attached to and not within 36 in. of a dwelling AND not serving an egress door;
    prefabricated above-ground pools <24 in. deep and ≤5,000 gal; painting, papering, tiling, carpeting, cabinets,
    counter tops and similar finish work; playground equipment.
    Statutory additions: MCL 125.1510(7) ordinary repairs; MCL 125.1510(8) agricultural buildings not used in
    retail trade.

41. [V] WELLS — statewide construction code, LOCAL permit. Part 127, MCL 333.12701–12715; MCL 333.12714 directs the
    construction code, promulgated at R 325.1601 et seq.
    - [V] LHDs issue. EGLE *State of Michigan Water Well Manual* (Aug 2023): "The Well Construction Code allows the
      state of Michigan to delegate well permitting duties to the LHDs," and "LHDs are responsible for issuing
      water well construction permits for single family residential homes." Step 1 of the process is "Individual
      applies for a well construction permit **PRIOR TO DRILLING** the well." "The permitting process can vary
      greatly from LHD to LHD."
    - [V] R 325.1612(g) / Rule 112: a local code that is "not less restrictive than these rules" governs too, and a
      local board of health that needs stricter requirements "**shall do so**" and file them with the director.
      Failure to comply with a local code requirement is a violation of Rule 112.
    - [V] Post-construction testing is statewide and the OWNER is responsible. R 325.1661(2)–(3): before placing a
      new/repaired/reconditioned system into service and after chlorine is flushed, samples shall be collected and
      "organisms of the coliform group shall not be present"; "**the water supply owner shall be responsible for
      collecting the water sample**" or arranging it, and the driller/pump installer must notify the owner of that
      responsibility.
      [H] **NITRATE/ARSENIC ARE NOT A STATEWIDE REQUIREMENT.** The rule set was grepped for "nitrate": zero hits.
      Coliform is the only statewide post-construction test; anything else is a local add-on. Do not print nitrate
      testing as a Michigan requirement.
    - [V] OWNER-BUILDER GEM — MCL 333.12703(2): sections 12701–12715 "shall not prevent a person from constructing
      a well or installing a pump on property owned or leased by the person which is intended for use only in a
      single family house which is that person's permanent residence … The person shall submit the drilling record
      required by section 12707 and comply with the rules and construction code." You may drill your own well; you
      still owe the record (MCL 333.12707, within 60 days) and full code compliance. This waives CONTRACTOR
      REGISTRATION, not the local permit.
    - [V] Penalty: MCL 333.12715(1), misdemeanor.
    - [H] "Well first" areas — many LHDs require an acceptable water supply before a septic or building permit.
      This is local practice, described in the Manual as varying by LHD. Do not print as a statewide rule.

42. [V] OTHER EGLE PERMITS (statewide, via Part 13) worth listing accurately:
    - Wetlands, Part 303, MCL 324.30304 — no permit → shall not deposit fill, dredge/remove soil, construct or
      maintain a use or development, or drain surface water from a wetland. Jurisdictional test at MCL
      324.30301(1)(n): not a wetland unless a water of the United States, contiguous to the Great Lakes/an inland
      lake or pond/a stream, **more than 5 acres**, has a documented endangered/threatened species, or is rare and
      imperiled — so an isolated wetland under 5 acres is generally outside Part 303. Single-family relief at
      MCL 324.30311(5) (rebuttable presumption where ≤2 acres of wetland affected).
    - Inland lakes and streams, Part 301, MCL 324.30102(1) — includes an artificial waterway "within 500 feet of
      the ordinary high-water mark."
    - Floodplain, Part 31, MCL 324.3104(4). Fee stacking relief at MCL 324.3104(13): a project needing Part 31 plus
      Part 301/303/323/325 "is subject to only the single highest permit fee."
    [H] MCL 324.30301/30304/30305/30311 carry a conditional repeal tied to EPA withdrawing Michigan's CWA §404
    assumption (2013 PA 98). Not triggered; all in force.
