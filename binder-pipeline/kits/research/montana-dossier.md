# Montana Owner-Builder Dossier — research for the MT Permit Kit (six documents)

Compiled August 2026. Statute text was read from the OFFICIAL Montana Code Annotated on the Legislature's own
server at `mca.legmt.gov` (pattern:
`mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0500/chapter_0600/part_0010/section_0020/0500-0600-0010-0020.html`), which serves
the MCA 2025 and prints a `History:` line on every section naming each session law that amended it — the only
reliable way to catch a repeal. Agency facts, form sets, adopted code editions, and the certified-jurisdiction
list were read from the Department of Labor & Industry Building Codes Program pages at `bsd.dli.mt.gov` directly.
Every claim is tagged:

  [V] = verified against a primary source (statute text read directly, or the administering agency's own page).
  Safe to print with the citation.
  [H] = hedge — could not be pinned to a primary source, is locally variable, or is a proven-negative that can
  only be stated carefully. Print only with hedge language and a verification step.

WARNING FOR FUTURE EDITORS — TWO CITATION MINEFIELDS.

  1. **MCA Title 39, chapter 9 ("Contractor Registration") is DEAD LAW as of January 1, 2026.** The chapter
     heading on the MCA server now reads "CHAPTER 9. CONTRACTOR REGISTRATION(Renumbered and Repealed)."
     DEAD: 39-9-101, 39-9-102, 39-9-103, 39-9-201, 39-9-204, 39-9-206, 39-9-207, 39-9-401 — all "Repealed …
     (Repealer effective January 1, 2026)," by Sec. 14, Ch. 481, L. 2025 and Sec. 19, Ch. 644, L. 2025.
     MOVED: 39-9-205 → **37-45-205**; 39-9-211 (the exemptions) → **37-45-104**, both by Sec. 20, Ch. 644,
     L. 2025. The live chapter is **MCA Title 37, chapter 45 — Construction Contractors**. Our own Montana state
     guide, and effectively every third-party Montana owner-builder guide, still cites the dead chapter and still
     calls the credential a "registration."
  2. **The DLI plumbing page states conditions that appear in no STATUTE — but they are in the RULES.**
     [CORRECTED — see the process note at the end.] The page says a homeowner may skip the plumbing permit if
     "the residence is for the owner's personal use and not built on speculation of resale or intended as a
     rental property." That sentence is genuinely not in 50-60-506(4) (which requires only ownership of
     residential property + personal performance) and not in 37-69-102(1)(a) (which requires a single-family
     residence used exclusively for the owner's personal use). **But it IS in the Administrative Rules**:
     **ARM 24.301.361(3)** bars issuing a plumbing permit to anyone but a licensed master plumber "in a residence
     that is built on speculation of sale or rent and is not the owner's primary residence in which they will
     reside," and **ARM 24.301.431(3)** does the same on the electrical side, limiting owner permits to property
     "maintained for their personal, private use" and not "built on speculation of resale or intended as rental
     property." The agency was summarizing the RULE, not the statute.
     **THE REAL MONTANA LESSON, and it is bigger than the Michigan one:** Montana keeps an unusual share of what
     actually governs a house in ARM Title 24 ch. 301 rather than in the MCA — adopted editions, state
     amendments, permit fees, inspection procedure, permit duration, and real conditions on the exemptions.
     **Reading only the statute will mislead you in Montana.** An early draft of this kit printed "a widely
     repeated Montana rule that is not in any statute" and framed it as agency overreach. That was wrong in
     substance and has been corrected in MT.1. Always ask whether the answer lives in the MCA or the ARM.

THE STRUCTURAL HEADLINE (this is the kit's thesis) — **THE MONTANA INVERSION**: Montana writes one statewide
building code and then, by statute, declines to apply it to your house. A residential building of fewer than five
dwelling units is outside the state building code unless the local legislative body affirmatively adopted it, and
the state "may not enforce" it for those buildings. Across most of Montana's land area there is genuinely no
building permit, no plan review, and no building inspection. **But three duties survive that exemption, and each
lives in a different place**: an electrical permit (Title 50 ch. 60 part 6, which has its own scope and its own
exceptions list that says nothing about houses), enforced at the power meter and backed by a misdemeanor; an
energy code that the very same section puts back three subsections later, enforced by **builder
self-certification in writing**; and the whole of water, wastewater, and sanitation law in Titles 76 and 85, which
chapter 60 never touched. The practical consequence no competing Montana guide states: **your homeowner
electrical exemption is a LICENSE exemption only, while your homeowner plumbing exemption reaches the PERMIT
itself** — a mirror-image asymmetry, in one chapter, that decides what paperwork you owe.

## MT.0 — Cover summary facts (the inversion, in headlines)

1. [V] The state building code does not reach your house. **MCA 50-60-102(1)(a)**: "the state building code, as
   defined in 50-60-203(3), **does not apply to**: (a) residential buildings containing less than five dwelling
   units or their attached-to structures, any farm or ranch building of any size, and any private garage or
   private storage structure of any size used only for the owner's own use, located within a county, city, or
   town, **unless the local legislative body by ordinance or resolution makes the state building code applicable
   to these structures**." And **50-60-102(2)**: "the state **may not enforce** the state building code under
   50-60-205 for the buildings and equipment referred to in subsection (1)."
   NOTE THE PRECISION: this is a **scope limit, not an enforcement gap**. The widespread phrasing "the code still
   applies, there's just nobody to inspect it" is wrong and the kit says so explicitly.

2. [V] Where no local code is adopted, the state code applies and the state enforces it — for everything
   50-60-102(1) did not carve out. **50-60-205(1)**: "If a county, city, or town does not adopt a building code as
   provided in 50-60-301, the state building code applies within the county, city, or town and the state will
   enforce the code in these areas."

3. [V] **The energy code applies to your house anyway.** 50-60-102(5)(a): "for purposes of promoting the energy
   efficiency of home design and operation, the provisions of the state building code relating to energy
   conservation adopted pursuant to 50-60-203(1) **apply to residential buildings**, except: (i) farm and ranch
   buildings; and (ii) any private garage or private storage structure attached to a residential building and used
   only for the owner's own use." 50-60-102(5)(b): enforceable "(i) by the department only for those residential
   buildings containing five or more dwelling units or otherwise subject to the state building code; and (ii)
   **through the builder self-certification program provided for in 50-60-802** for those residential buildings
   containing less than five dwelling units and not otherwise subject to the state building code."

4. [V] **You certify the energy code to yourself, in writing.** 50-60-802(1): "a person who begins construction on
   a residential building in Montana after October 1, 1993, **shall certify in writing to the building owner at
   the conclusion of construction** that the residential building has been constructed in compliance with the
   energy-efficient construction standards adopted under the provisions of 50-60-203(1)." An owner-builder is both
   the person who begins construction and the building owner. 50-60-802(2) adds a parallel certification where a
   local government with a code program adopted voluntary energy standards the building meets or exceeds.

5. [V] **The electrical permit survives the exemption, and it is enforced at the meter.** Part 6 (Electrical
   Installations) has its own exceptions list at **50-60-602** — utility signal/communications and traffic
   devices, petroleum refineries, mines, low-voltage signal and optical fiber — and **nothing about residential
   buildings of fewer than five dwelling units**. 50-60-604 imposes the permit and inspection duty. **50-60-605**:
   "Except for temporary connections that the department of labor and industry may authorize by rule for a period
   **not exceeding 14 days** without a preconnection inspection, **power suppliers may not connect with or
   energize an electrical installation under this part unless the owner or a licensed electrical contractor has
   delivered to the power supplier an electrical permit covering the installation**." "Power suppliers" is defined
   in the same section as "Individuals, firms, **cooperatives**, corporations, or municipalities selling
   electricity" — a rural electric co-op is bound. **50-60-607**: energizing an installation for which no
   electrical permit has been issued "is guilty of a **misdemeanor**."

6. [V] **The state code is a CEILING, not a floor** — the inverse of nearly every other state. 50-60-301(2)(a):
   "Except as provided in subsection (2)(b), a county, city, or town **may not adopt or enforce a building code
   that is more stringent than the building code adopted by the department** or as required by state law." The
   only exception is voluntary incentive-based energy standards, and 50-60-301(2)(b): "New construction is not
   required to meet local standards that exceed state energy conservation standards unless the building contractor
   elects to receive a local incentive." Consequence for the kit: the local-amendment hunting that dominates CO,
   CA, and WA is largely absent inside the building code; what varies locally is everything the building code is
   not (zoning, subdivision, floodplain, access, addressing, fire district, fees).

## MT.1 — The exemptions: contractor, electrical, plumbing, and the energy duty

### The construction contractor license (NEW — effective January 1, 2026)

7. [V] **37-45-201(1)**: "An individual or business entity **may not engage in business as a construction
   contractor without a current license from the department**." The application must include (a) SSN;
   (b) "**proof of compliance with workers' compensation laws**"; (c) IRS EIN if any; (d) names and addresses of
   partners / owner / corporate officers and registered agent / LLC manager or members and registered agent.
   **There is no examination and no competency requirement in the statute** — this is an insurance and
   accountability credential wearing the word "license."

8. [V] **37-45-101** (purpose, En. Sec. 1, Ch. 644, L. 2025): "to protect the public health, safety, and welfare
   of the public through the regulation of construction contractors." The old chapter's framing was
   workers'-comp compliance; the new purpose clause is a public-protection clause.
   [H] EDITOR'S NOTE: the chapter still uses BOTH "license" (new sections) and "registered"/"register" (renumbered
   sections 37-45-104(12) and (24)). Do not read significance into which word a subsection uses; the kit says so
   on the page.

9. [V] **37-45-102(1)** — "Construction contractor" means, **except as provided in 37-45-104**, a person, firm, or
   corporation that: "(a) **in the pursuit of an independent business**, offers to undertake, undertakes, or
   submits a bid to construct, alter, repair, add to, subtract from, improve, move, wreck, or demolish **for
   another** a building … **or** (b) in order to do work similar to that described in subsection (1)(a) **on the
   construction contractor's property, employs members of more than one trade on a single job or under a single
   building permit**, except as otherwise provided."
   → **(1)(b) is the owner-builder hook.** Working on your OWN property, employing members of MORE THAN ONE TRADE
   on a single job, puts you inside the definition — and you then need 37-45-104(13) to get back out. The
   load-bearing word is *employs*; engaging separate licensed businesses is a different thing, and it is the same
   distinction that decides workers'-comp exposure.

10. [V] **37-45-104(13) — THE OWNER-BUILDER EXEMPTION.** The chapter does not apply "to **an owner working on the
    owner's property, whether occupied by the owner or not**, but this exemption does not apply to an owner who is
    otherwise covered by this chapter who constructs an improvement on the owner's property **with the intention
    and for the purpose of promptly selling the improved property, unless the owner has continuously occupied the
    property as the owner's primary residence for at least the last 12 months**."
    → **NO occupancy requirement** — "whether occupied by the owner or not" is express. The only condition is the
    anti-flip clause, and even that has a 12-month primary-residence escape. State this affirmatively; guides that
    print "a home you own and live in" are adding words.

11. [V] Other 37-45-104 exemptions an owner-builder meets:
    - (8) work "of a casual, minor, or inconsequential nature" whose aggregate contract price for labor, materials
      and all other items is "**less than $2,500 a job**" — expressly NOT where the work "is only a part of a
      larger or major operation" or is split into sub-$2,500 contracts "for the purpose of evasion."
    - (9) a farmer or rancher engaged in a farming, dairying, agriculture, viticulture, horticulture, or stock or
      poultry operation.
    - (12) "an owner who contracts for work to be performed by a registered construction contractor," with the
      same anti-flip proviso as (13).
    - (15) a Montana-licensed architect, civil or professional engineer, or professional land surveyor acting
      solely in a professional capacity.
    - (16) "an **electrician or plumber, licensed in Montana, operating within the scope of the license**."
    - (18) a person engaging in the activities as an employee with wages as sole compensation.
    - (20) a water well contractor licensed under Title 37, chapter 43, performing water well work.
    - (23) "a person working on the person's own residence, if the residence is owned by a person other than the
      resident."
    - (24) "**an independent contractor who has no employees**. However, an independent contractor may voluntarily
      elect to register under this chapter."
    History line confirming the move: "En. Sec. 10, Ch. 500, L. 1995; amd. Sec. 7, Ch. 548, L. 1997; amd. Sec. 6,
    Ch. 294, L. 2019; **Sec. 39-9-211, MCA 2023; redes. 37-45-104 by Sec. 20, Ch. 644, L. 2025.**"

12. [V] **37-45-202 — the workers'-compensation shield. This is the most commercially valuable paragraph in the
    kit for anyone paying for labor.** "A person who, pursuant to an oral or written contract, **engages a
    construction contractor who is licensed under this chapter on the date of the contract is not liable as an
    employer for workers' compensation coverage under 39-71-405, for unemployment insurance coverage, or for wages
    and fringe benefits** for: (1) the licensed construction contractor; (2) the employees of the licensed
    construction contractor; or (3) a subsequent subcontractor or the employees of a subsequent subcontractor…"
    → The trigger is **licensed on the date of the contract**. Verify and keep the printout that day.

13. [V] **37-45-301(1)(d)** — unprofessional conduct to hire or classify a person as an independent contractor if
    the person lacks an **independent contractor exemption certificate** required by 39-71-417, or the department
    has suspended, revoked, or denied it.
    [V] **37-45-203** — (1) the department sets the license application fee; (2) fees to a special revenue
    account; (3) the department shall provide a consumer and industry education program; (4) fines under 37-1-109
    or 37-1-406 go to the uninsured employers' fund (39-71-503).
    [H] NO FEE FIGURE APPEARS IN STATUTE. The kit prints none.

### Electrical — a license exemption, not a permit exemption

14. [V] **37-68-103(3)(a)**: "Except as provided in subsection (3)(b), this chapter **does not require an
    individual to hold a license** to perform electrical work on the individual's own property or residence if the
    property or residence is **maintained for the individual's own use**." Generous on its face — no single-family
    limit, no occupancy requirement, no not-for-sale clause.
    **THE TRAP, provable from inside the same section:** compare the wording of its neighbors —
    - 37-68-103(2): "The **licensing or inspection** provisions of this chapter do not apply to regularly employed
      maintenance electricians…"
    - 37-68-103(7)(a): "The **licensing and inspection** provisions of this chapter do not apply to an
      apprentice…"
    - 37-68-103(3)(a): "does not require an individual **to hold a license**" — the word "inspection" is absent,
      and so is the word "permit."
    The Legislature knew how to exempt someone from inspection; for the homeowner it wrote something narrower. And
    it could not have been otherwise: the permit duty lives in a different title (50-60-604), and an exemption in
    Title 37 cannot waive a duty in Title 50.

15. [V] **37-68-103(3)(b) — the one carve-out**: "Subsection (3)(a) **does not include an exemption for an
    individual who is performing electrical work on a grid-tied generator** located at the individual's own
    property or residence." Grid-tied solar, grid-tied wind, and interconnected standby generators need a licensed
    electrician even where the rest of the house does not.
    [V] Other 37-68-103 items: (4) an electrical contractor's work must be under the direction of a licensed
    master electrician, or a licensed journeyman "for residential construction consisting of less than five living
    units in a single structure"; (5) plugging an appliance into an existing approved outlet is not "installing."

16. [V] **50-60-604** — "The department of labor and industry or an authorized representative or a county, city,
    or town certified to perform an inspection pursuant to 50-60-302 shall inspect electrical installations, issue
    electrical permits for these installations, and establish and charge a reasonable and uniform fee…" Plus: "As
    part of any inspection, the inspector **shall require proof of licensure** from any person who is required to
    be licensed who is involved with or, in the inspector's judgment, appears to be involved with electrical
    installations if the person is on the site," with violations reported to the board of electricians.
    [V] DLI Electrical Permits page corroborates and extends: "An electrical permit is required for any
    installation in any new construction, remodeling or repair, **except as provided by Section 50-60-602, MCA**"
    and "**State electrical permits are required on all electrical work performed in Montana, except in cities,
    counties and towns certified to issue electrical permits and conduct inspections.**"

### Plumbing — the mirror image, in two titles with two different conditions

17. [V] **50-60-506(4) — the PERMIT exemption**: "This part does not prohibit **the owner of residential
    property** from making an installation for all sanitary plumbing and potable water supply piping **without a
    permit** if **the owner personally does the work**."
    → Two conditions only: ownership of residential property, and personal performance. **No** single-family
    limit. **No** occupancy condition. **No** not-for-sale or not-for-rental clause.
18. [V] **37-69-102(1)(a) — the LICENSE exemption, narrower**: licensure is not required "when **an owner of a
    single-family residence used exclusively for the owner's personal use** installs all sanitary plumbing and
    potable water supply piping."
    → You need BOTH to do your own plumbing unlicensed and unpermitted, so the operative test is the narrower one.
19. [V] Other plumbing carve-outs that matter on a rural build:
    - 37-69-102(1)(h): no license "in the case of a private water supply, [for the] installation of the pump,
      waterline, or pressure tank, regardless of whether the pump, waterline, or pressure tank is located inside
      or outside the structure being served."
    - 37-69-102(1)(c) and (2); 50-60-503: farms and ranches not connected to public water supply or sewage
      disposal are outside the plumbing chapter and the state plumbing part entirely.
    - 50-60-506(1) minor replacement/repair; (2) installations exempt under 37-69-102 or 50-60-503;
      **(3) no STATE permit "whenever the installation occurs in an area governed by a county, city, or town and
      where there is in effect a county, city, or town building code that covers plumbing installations and that
      provides inspection procedures."**
20. [H→V] **THE AGENCY-PARAPHRASE FINDING.** DLI Plumbing Permits page, verbatim: "Homeowners do not need to
    obtain a plumbing permit provided he/she is doing the plumbing installation themselves and the residence is
    for the owner's personal use and **not built on speculation of resale or intended as a rental property**."
    The final clause is in neither statute. The kit prints both statutes, prints the agency sentence, and tells
    the reader what to do with the difference — *not* treat it as permission, but know which words are law and
    which are summary. This is the kit's teachable habit and it is what makes the dead Title 39 citations
    discoverable.
    [V] The same page also states: "Farms and ranches, mines, mills, smelters, railroads, and public utilities are
    exempt from state plumbing permit requirements."

### Absences worth printing

21. [V] **50-60-203(5)(a)**: the department "may not include in the state building code … a requirement for the
    installation of a fire sprinkler system in a single-family dwelling or a residential building that contains no
    more than two dwelling units." (5)(b): no mandated solar or EV wiring/equipment. (6): may not prohibit or
    limit the use of electric, natural gas, propane, or other energy source.
    [V] 50-60-203(2): the department may adopt national codes by reference "in whole or in part" and "may adopt
    rules more stringent than those contained in national codes" — so read the ARM rule, not just the model code.

## MT.2 — The land approvals that actually gate a Montana build

22. [V] **MCA 76-4-121 — THE SINGLE MOST VALUABLE SENTENCE IN THE KIT for a rural land buyer.** "A person **may
    not** dispose of any lot within a subdivision, erect any facility for the supply of water or disposal of
    sewage or solid waste, **erect any building or shelter in a subdivision that requires facilities for the
    supply of water or disposal of sewage or solid waste, or occupy any permanent buildings** in a subdivision
    until: (1) a certificate of subdivision approval has been issued pursuant to 76-4-114 indicating that the
    reviewing authority has approved the subdivision application and that **the subdivision is not subject to a
    sanitary restriction**; (2) the certifying authority has provided certification pursuant to 76-4-127 that the
    subdivision will be provided with adequate municipal or county water and/or sewer district facilities and
    adequate storm water drainage; or (3) the subdivision is otherwise exempt from review under 76-4-125."
    → It forbids **erecting** and **occupying** — not merely permitting. It sits in Title 76, entirely outside the
    building-code chapter, so the residential exemption never touches it. "No building permit required" and "you
    may build" are different sentences, and this is where the difference bites.

23. [V] **76-4-122(2)** — a county clerk and recorder may not accept a plat, certificate of survey, or
    townhome/condominium declaration for filing until one of: (a) approval of "the local health officer having
    jurisdiction" filed with the reviewing authority plus a certificate of subdivision approval; (b) a 76-4-127
    certification of municipal or county water/sewer service; or (c) an acknowledged certification of exemption
    placed on the document — and "**The certification must quote in its entirety the wording of the applicable
    exemption.**" 76-4-125(3) states the same requirement from the other direction.
    → This is HOW YOU VERIFY A PARCEL FROM THE PUBLIC RECORD. Pull the certificate of survey or plat at the county
    clerk and recorder and read its face for one of the three; look specifically for the words "sanitary
    restriction" and for any later release.

24. [V] **76-4-125(1)** lists the exclusions (including the exclusions cited in 76-3-201, 76-3-207(1)(f), and
    76-25-402(1)(a)–(h); divisions to add land to an approved parcel with no water/sewage facilities on the added
    parcel; divisions for purposes other than constructing water/sewage/solid-waste facilities as DEQ specifies by
    rule; certain 76-4-127-certified categories; certain remainders; and cabin or home sites under 77-2-318(2)).
    **76-4-125(2)**: "a local health officer may require that … the remainder include acreage or features
    sufficient to accommodate a **replacement drainfield**."

25. [V] **MCA 85-2-306 — exempt wells. "Exempt" means exempt from the PERMIT, not from filing.**
    - **85-2-306(3)(a)(iii)** — outside a stream depletion zone: no permit where the appropriation "is **35 gallons
      a minute or less, and does not exceed 10 acre-feet a year**, except that a **combined appropriation from the
      same source by two or more wells or developed springs exceeding 10 acre-feet, regardless of the flow rate,
      requires a permit**."
    - **85-2-306(3)(a)(iv)** — inside a stream depletion zone: "is **20 gallons a minute or less, and does not
      exceed 2 acre-feet a year**," with the same combined-appropriation limit.
    - **85-2-306(2)** — inside a **controlled ground water area**, ground water may be appropriated "only …
      according to a permit received pursuant to 85-2-508" or a rule under 85-2-506. The exemption does not apply.
    - **85-2-306(3)(b)** — **BEFORE** appropriating, file a "correct and complete **notice of intent to
      appropriate groundwater**." Defects notified within 10 business days; uncorrected notice terminates after
      60 days; within 10 business days of a complete notice the department "shall review the notice for compliance
      with subsection (3)(a) and **shall authorize or deny** the notice." Five years to complete, one extension of
      up to five more.
    - **85-2-306(3)(c)(i)** — "**Within 60 days** of completion of the well or developed spring and appropriation
      of the ground water for beneficial use, the appropriator shall file a **notice of completion**." It must
      establish the work was done "in substantial accordance with the notice of intent … authorized by the
      department" ((3)(c)(iii)); if not cured in time "the department authorization expires and a new notice of
      intent … is required" ((3)(c)(iv)).
    - **85-2-306(3)(d)** — "A certificate of water right may not be issued until a correct and complete notice has
      been filed … **The date of filing of the notice of completion is the date of priority of the right.**"
    - **85-2-306(1)** — you generally need a possessory interest in the property where the water is used and
      exclusive rights in the well works; a person without a possessory interest must give the landowner at least
      **30 days' written notice**.
    [H] IMPORTANT — the combined-appropriation rule is now expressed **in the statute itself**, in volume terms
    ("exceeding 10 acre-feet, regardless of the flow rate"), alongside a stream-depletion-zone tier. Do NOT print
    the older ARM 36.12.101 litigation narrative (Clark Fork Coalition) as the operative rule. The kit prints the
    statutory text only. 85-2-306 has a very long History line — re-read it before any reprint.
    [H] NOT VERIFIED: our state guide's claim that "the state's exempt-well rules tightened effective January 1,
    2026." The 2026 date could not be tied to a specific session law from the truncated History line. The kit does
    not print any such date; it prints the statutory text as published in the MCA 2025 and tells the reader to
    check the History line.

26. [H] **Septic permitting is county-level and is deliberately not resolved in the kit.** Verified statutory
    hooks only: the local health officer's approval role (76-4-122(2)(a)) and the replacement-drainfield authority
    (76-4-125(2)). Everything else — site evaluation, who may perform it, soil pits vs percolation testing,
    setbacks, minimum depth to groundwater or bedrock, whether a homeowner may install their own system, and which
    inspection must precede cover — is asked as a question list, not answered. DEQ's subdivision/engineering
    program and county environmental health are the two offices named.

## MT.3 — Inspections: two completely different regimes

31. [V] Where a certified program has jurisdiction: it examines plans and directs inspection during construction
    (50-60-106(2)(a)), may order remedies in writing "during and in the course of construction" (50-60-106(2)(d)),
    and issues certificates of occupancy (50-60-106(2)(e); 50-60-107).
    **Outside one: no plan review, no building inspection, and NO CERTIFICATE OF OCCUPANCY**, because there is no
    program to issue one. The only inspection most rural Montana houses receive is the electrical one.

32. [V] **50-60-106(2)(c) — the department's single-family checklist and its clock.** Certified jurisdictions
    shall "make available to building contractors at a price that is commensurate with reproduction costs a
    checklist devised by the department pursuant to 50-60-118 for single-family dwellings and provide to
    contractors who attach a completed checklist to the plans submitted for examination the relevant building
    permit or notice of plan disapproval **within 10 working days** of the contractor's submission." Ask for it by
    name — it is the closest thing Montana publishes to a statewide submittal list, and it comes with a clock.

33. [V] **50-60-302 — certification mechanics.** (1) no local enforcement without department certification; the
    current adopted code, fee list, and enforcement plan must be filed and approved; **(1)(c) all inspectors "must
    themselves be properly and currently state-licensed as journeymen in that craft or occupation or be certified
    by a nationally recognized entity … approved by the department."** (3) on revocation "the state resumes its
    original jurisdiction," with the local government retaining responsibility for finishing inspections and
    issuing certificates of occupancy on projects it already permitted. (4) voluntary decertification requires
    **90 days'** written notice, with the same retained responsibility.

34. [V] Fee governance, useful for a reader questioning a bill: 50-60-106(2)(g) requires construction-related fees
    to be "necessary, reasonable, and uniform," used only for permit review and code enforcement, **reduced if
    they accumulate above 36 months of need**, with up to 0.5% remitted to the department for the building codes
    education program (50-60-116). And 50-60-301(4)(a): when the same single-family dwelling plan is built at more
    than one site, after the first examination the local government **shall** adjust the plan fee to cover only
    zoning, footings/foundations/basements, curbs, gutters, landscaping, utility connections, street requirements,
    sidewalks, and other exterior-specific items.

35. [H] **No Montana statute enumerates the residential inspection ladder.** The kit's sequence is labeled TYPICAL
    and annotated per rung with what happens when no building program has jurisdiction, and the reader is told the
    permit card governs where one exists.

## RESEARCH PROCESS NOTE

Four parallel research sub-agents were dispatched (statutes; codes and the state/local split; local jurisdiction
sampling; water and septic). **All four reported late** — after the kit's first build was complete, zipped, and
delivered. In the interval every fact in the first edition was verified first-hand by the kit author via `curl`
against `mca.legmt.gov` and `bsd.dli.mt.gov`, with HTML stripped in-shell.

That redundancy turned out to be worth having, in both directions:

- **The agents corroborated the whole statutory spine independently** — 50-60-102, 76-4-121, 85-2-306, the Part 6
  electrical chain, both homeowner exemptions, and the certified-jurisdiction list all matched. Two independent
  derivations agreeing is a stronger warrant than either alone.
- **They reached what the author could not**, principally ARM Title 24 ch. 301 and ARM Title 17 ch. 36, which is
  where Montana keeps the code editions, the amendments, the fees, the permit mechanics, and — the correction
  that mattered — real conditions on the homeowner exemptions.
- **They caught one substantive error in printed content.** The first edition ran a callout headed "A widely
  repeated Montana rule that is not in any statute," framing the DLI plumbing page's spec-and-rental condition as
  agency gloss with no legal force. It has force: it is in ARM 24.301.361(3) and 24.301.431(3). The callout was
  rewritten, the section heading changed, the comparison table amended, and the lesson inverted from "read the
  statute, not the agency page" to "**always check whether the answer is in the MCA or the ARM**" — which is the
  more useful habit in this state anyway, and is now also in the kit's standing source note.

**Process lesson for the next kit: do not treat silence from a research agent as failure, and do not treat the
author's own first-hand verification as a substitute for the agents' reach.** The two cover different ground —
the author got the statutes fast and correctly; the agents got the administrative rules, which is where the
governing detail actually lives in Montana. Build the first edition on whatever is verified, but keep the second
pass budgeted, because the late material changed a headline.
