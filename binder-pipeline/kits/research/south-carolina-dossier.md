## WARNING FOR FUTURE EDITORS — SEVEN MINEFIELDS

0. **THE CODES ROLL OVER ON 1 JANUARY 2027 — four months after this kit shipped.** On
   **26 August 2025** the Council adopted the **2024** IBC/IRC/IFC/IPC/IMC/IFGC and the
   **2023 NEC**, and "established the implementation date for local jurisdictions as
   January 1, 2027." The 2009 IECC does **not** move with them — the 2024 modification
   index repeats that it "has been statutorily adopted as the energy standard." Because
   § 6-9-130(A) fixes a job to the codes in effect on the permit **issuance** date, a
   permit issued before 1 Jan 2027 keeps the whole build on the 2021 stack. Two rollover
   landmines: (a) 2024 R301.2.1 and R301.2.2.1 **drop the Council maps and the ATC site
   in favor of the ASCE Hazard Tool**, so SC.2's map guidance dies at the rollover;
   (b) the SC amendments **renumber** — R318.1→R305.1, R318.4→R305.4, R318.5→R305.5,
   R313→R309. A full cross-reference pass is required. [V]
   *Source: "2024 South Carolina Building Codes Modifications Index," v.1 dated
   11/3/2025, llr.sc.gov/bcc/PDFfiles/2024 Final Modification Index with Hyperlinks.pdf*

1. **DHEC no longer exists, and scdhec.gov serves nothing at all.** 2023 Act No. 60
   (S.399) abolished the Department of Health and Environmental Control effective
   1 July 2024, splitting it into the **Department of Environmental Services (SCDES,
   des.sc.gov)** and the **Department of Public Health (dph.sc.gov)**. Septic, private
   wells and coastal all went to SCDES. The old domain was not redirected — it fails.
   County pages at Anderson, Oconee, York, Georgetown, Horry and Kershaw were still
   linking septic guidance to the dead host in September 2026. [V]

2. **The state's own published code page for Title 48 Chapter 39 is STALE.** 2026 Act
   No. 146 (H.4189), signed 15 May 2026, rewrote the coastal chapter's definitions —
   § 48-39-10(V) "'Department' means the Department of Environmental Services";
   § 48-39-10(C) "'Division' means the Division of Coastal Management of the Department
   of Environmental Services" — and struck OCRM from § 48-39-45 and § 48-39-320(C). As
   of September 2026 scstatehouse.gov/code/t48c039.php still prints the old DHEC/OCRM
   text. **Cite the acts, never the code page.** Regs. Chapter 30 IS current (retitled
   "Department of Environmental Services—Coastal Division"). [V]

3. **The Building Codes Council's own website serves a stale code document.** Its Codes
   menu link named *Residential Code* (llr.sc.gov/bcc/pdffiles/Residential Code.pdf) is
   a SCANNED copy of S.C. State Register Vol. 43, Issue 5, 24 May 2019, Document
   No. 4868 — the **2018** IRC modification summary. Verified by rendering the PDF to
   PNG and reading it; it is image-only, so it cannot even be searched. The text in
   force is **Chapter 8, Article 12 of the Code of Regulations** (2021 IRC, last amended
   eff. 27 May 2022). Never source an amendment from the Council's PDF. [V]

4. **$5,000 is the wrong number to lead with.** It is the *residential builder*
   threshold (§ 40-59-20(6)). The number that actually decides whether the person on
   your lot is legal is **$500**, the residential *specialty* threshold
   (§ 40-59-20(7)), raised from $200 by 2022 Act No. 186. And Chapter 11 general and
   mechanical contracting is **$10,000**, raised from $5,000 by 2023 Act No. 69 eff.
   19 May 2023. Three numbers, two chapters, two boards. Plus a *fourth* $5,000: the
   point at which a licensed specialty contractor must carry a bond (§ 40-59-240(D)). [V]

5. **The exemption cite is § 40-59-260, and § 40-59-30 is a different section.**
   § 40-59-30 is "License requirement; enforcement of contracts; restraining orders" and
   contains no exemption at all; § 40-59-20 is Definitions. A prior verification pass
   corrected our published guide from -30 to -260; that correction was right. [V]

6. **The energy code is set by STATUTE, not by the code council.** R.8-1230: "IRC
   Chapter 11 Energy Efficiency. The Building Codes Council does not adopt IRC
   Chapter 11." What applies is § 6-10-30, the Energy Standard Act: "The 2009 edition of
   the International Energy Conservation Code is adopted as the Energy Standard." Last
   moved by 2012 Act No. 143 eff. 1 Jan 2013. The Council has no power over it. This is
   the whole explanation for SC's 2009 energy code, and no competitor guide gives it. [V]

## THE STRUCTURAL HEADLINE (the kit's thesis)

South Carolina inverts the question that dominates most states in this series.
**§ 6-9-10(A): "All municipalities… and counties in this State shall enforce building,
energy, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, gas, and fire codes… The municipality or
county shall enforce only the national building and safety codes provided in this
chapter."** The 2003 amendment substituted "enforce" for "adopt" and "shall enforce" for
"may adopt." § 6-9-30(A) then requires each county to appoint a building official "so
that the unincorporated area of the county is under the jurisdiction of a building
official." There is no general unincorporated gap. [V]

So the kit's thesis is not *will anyone inspect this house* — it is **which of the small
obligations attached to your exemption will you forget**, and **who are you allowed to
pay**. Three obligations, each with a hard consequence:

- § 40-59-260(C) — personally appear and sign; take the disclosure statement.
- § 40-59-260(E) — file a notice at the register of deeds afterwards. "Failure to do so
  **revokes the statutory exemption**." No fine, no cure period, no deadline in days.
- § 40-59-260(A)(1) and (F) — three permitted labor sources and no fourth.

### The hole in "no no-code areas in SC" [V]
**§ 6-9-30(B)** lets a municipality or county that "determines that it is unable to
arrange for services for any annual period at costs totally within the schedule of fees
recommended in the appendices to the building codes" submit an affidavit to the Council
and be "exempt from the requirements of this chapter" for up to five years, renewable at
five-year intervals. **The Council publishes no roster of who holds one.** The kit
therefore says "assume a permit is required everywhere," and gives the reader the
question to ask if a rural office says otherwise. Our published guide's flat "there are
no no-code areas in SC" is very nearly right but is not unconditionally true.

## SC.1 — LICENSING AND THE OWNER-BUILDER EXEMPTION

### § 40-59-260 in full [V]
(A) three conditions: (1) owner does the work himself, with his own employees, or with
licensed contractors or registered entities or individuals; (2) sole occupancy by owner
or family and not intended for sale or rent; (3) general public does not have access.

(B) "proof of the sale or rent **or the offering for sale or rent** of the structure by
the owner-builder within **two years after completion or issuance of a certificate or
occupancy** is **prima facie evidence** that the project was undertaken for the purpose
of sale or rent, **unless otherwise approved by the commission**." "Sale" or "rent"
"includes an arrangement by which an owner receives compensation in money, provisions,
chattel, **or labor** from the occupancy." Closing sentence: "This section does not
exempt a person who is employed by the owner and who acts in the capacity of a builder
or a specialty contractor of any kind."
*("a certificate or occupancy" is the Code's own typo. Reproduce as-is when quoting.)*

(C) "an owner must personally appear and sign the building permit application," and the
agency "shall provide the person with a disclosure statement, provided by the
department, in substantially the following form" — full text held and printed verbatim
in SC.1.

(D) at that same moment the agency "shall provide the owner with all forms necessary to
comply with subsection (E)."

(E) the register-of-deeds notice, "indexed under the owner's name in the grantor's
index." "Failure to do so revokes the statutory exemption."

(F) nothing authorizes hiring an unlicensed or unregistered person.

### Two limits that appear ONLY in the disclosure, not in the statute [V]
(A) exempts an owner improving "the structure, group of structures, or appurtenances."
The disclosure says "You may build or improve a **one-family or two-family residence**."
(A) says nothing about supervision. The disclosure says "You must **supervise the
construction yourself**." Neither is reconciled anywhere in § 40-59-260 — and the
disclosure is the document the owner signs. The kit prints both and says so.

### No cap of any kind [V]
Read § 40-59-260 and § 40-11-360(5) end to end: **no square footage limit, no
one-house-per-N-years counter.** Guides that print one have borrowed it from another
state.

### The Chapter 11 twin [V]
§ 40-11-360(5) is the parallel owner exemption under the Contractor's Licensing Board,
in almost identical words, but **without** the "unless otherwise approved by the
commission" escape. § 40-11-360(7) separately exempts "an owner constructing a farm
building or portable storage building with less than five thousand square feet of floor
space and used only for livestock or storage."
**§ 40-11-420(C)**: where the owner is performing work under § 40-11-360(5), (6) or (7),
"the owner must obtain the building permit and must list on the building permit
application the **name and license number of all licensed contractors** performing work
on the project." A concrete documentation burden. (Drafting artifact: 2023 Act No. 69
§ 13 deleted the (A) designator from § 40-11-360, so § 40-11-420(C)'s reference to
"Section 40-11-360(A)(5)" now points at a subsection that no longer exists. Do not print
the "(A)".)
**§ 40-11-300(A)** binds the OWNER: unlawful "for an owner… to divide work into portions
so as to avoid the financial or other requirements of this chapter."

### Thresholds and credentials [V]
| Over | Triggers | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| $500 | residential specialty contractor credential | § 40-59-20(7) |
| $5,000 | residential builder license | § 40-59-20(6) |
| $5,000 | bond for a licensed specialty contractor (not less than $10,000) | § 40-59-240(D); Reg. 106-2 |
| $10,000 | Chapter 11 general or mechanical contractor | § 40-11-30 |

**License by examination — only three trades:** plumbers, electricians, HVAC installers
and repairers (Reg. 106-2, effective 1 July 2004). **Everything else on the
thirteen-trade list is a REGISTRATION**: vinyl and aluminum siding, insulation, roofers,
floor covering, masons, dry wall, carpenters, stucco, painters and wall paperers, solar
panel installers. This closes an ambiguity in § 40-59-20(7), whose license/registration
split sentence names only ten of the thirteen and silently omits roofers, insulation and
floor covering; Reg. 106-2 resolves it by naming only three as licensed. [V]

Scope limits worth printing (Reg. 106-1):
- (c) residential HVAC is **capped at five tons cooling and 175,000 BTU/HR heating per
  unit** — a bigger unit is Chapter 11 work.
- (h) **Masons** covers "poured-in-place concrete foundations (e.g. footings or
  reinforced slabs)" — the footing sub is a mason, not a carpenter.
- (e) insulation installers exclude exterior roofing foam and reflective coating.
- (m) solar: roof mounting only by a builder, a registered Roofer, or a GC with roofing;
  wiring only by a builder, a licensed Residential Electrician, or a mechanical
  contractor with electrical.
- Max **three** classifications per registrant (§ 40-59-240(A)); a fourth means sitting
  the builder examination.
- A specialty contractor "is not authorized to construct additions… without supervision
  by a residential builder," and is "prohibited from undertaking work outside the scope
  of his license or registration, including employing, hiring, and contracting or
  subcontracting with others to perform such work on his behalf."

### Lien leverage — the reason to check a credential [V]
- **§ 40-59-30(B)**: an unlicensed/unregistered person "may not file a mechanics' lien
  or bring an action at law or in equity to enforce the provisions of a contract."
  Notwithstanding § 29-5-10.
- **§ 29-5-15(A)**: a contractor filing a lien "must record his contractor license
  number or registration number on the lien document." (B): a frivolous lien draws "a
  fine up to five thousand dollars, the loss of his registration or contractor license,
  or both."
- **§ 29-5-20(A)**: two-way fee shift — "If the party defending against the lien
  prevails, the defending party must be awarded costs of the action and a reasonable
  attorney's fee," capped at the amount of the lien.
- **§ 29-5-90**: 90 days from ceasing work to BOTH serve the owner AND file a sworn
  statement of account. **§ 29-5-120(A)**: 6 months to commence suit and file a notice
  of pendency, or the lien dissolves.
- **§ 29-5-40**: "in no event shall the aggregate amount of liens set up hereby exceed
  the amount due by the owner on the contract price of the improvement made."
- **§ 29-5-80**: notice of nonresponsibility, in writing, for work not yet done.
- **§ 29-5-20(D)**: subsection (B) does not apply to individual laborers whose lien is
  under $2,000.

### Notice of Project Commencement — § 29-5-23 [V]
Filed with the clerk of court or register of deeds by "any person entering into a direct
agreement with, or with the consent of, an owner." **Within fifteen days of the
commencement of work**, fee **fifteen dollars**. Contents: name and address of filer;
name and address of owner or developer; general description of the improvement; location
of the project. A location notice must be posted at the job site with statutorily
prescribed wording, plus the contractor's name and address. "The failure to file a
notice of project commencement shall render the provisions of Sections 29-5-20(B) and
29-5-60(B) **inapplicable**" — i.e. the cap on second-tier liens goes away. Filing "shall
not constitute a cloud, lien, or encumbrance upon, or defect to, the title."
**HONEST CAVEAT PRINTED IN THE KIT:** the section is drafted for a contractor-led job
(the posted notice begins "The contractor on the project has filed…") and does not say
whether an owner acting as their own builder files it in their own name. The kit tells
the reader to ask the clerk before the fifteen days run out. [V + flagged H]

### Workers' compensation [V]
**§ 42-1-360(2)**, disjunctive: the Act does not apply to "any person who has regularly
employed in service **less than four employees** in the same business within the State
**or** who had a total annual payroll during the previous calendar year of **less than
three thousand dollars** regardless of the number of persons employed during that
period." The Workers' Compensation Commission counts part-time workers and family
members as employees, and **issues no exemption certificate** — its own guidance says
the Commission "does not certify that employers are not subject to the Act," so any
exemption affidavit a permit office wants is that office's own form.
§ 42-1-380: election effective 30 days after written notice. § 42-1-390: withdrawal
effective 60 days after written notice.
§ 42-1-400 statutory employer turns on "a part of his trade, business or occupation" —
printed as the statute, with no conclusion drawn. The Commission answers the general
version with "Maybe."

## SC.2 / SC.3 — CODES, DESIGN CRITERIA, INSPECTIONS

### Where the amendments actually live [V]
**§ 6-9-55(A)**: the Council "shall promulgate as regulations… any provision of or
amendment to any building code that would affect construction requirements for
one-family or two-family dwellings," and no such provision "shall be enforced until the
effective date of the regulations." So **the regulation IS the amendment**, not a
summary of one: S.C. Code of Regulations **Chapter 8, Articles 8 through 14**, one free
PDF at scstatehouse.gov/coderegs/.

### Which code edition governs YOUR house [V]
**§ 6-9-130(A)**: "Buildings must be inspected in accordance with the codes in effect for
the locality **on the date of the issuance of the original building permit**," falling
back to the application submission date. **R.8-236(B)** says the same from the
regulation side. A new edition mid-build does not move the job.

### The SC IRC modifications that change a house [V]
All from Chapter 8, Article 12.
- **R.8-1230** — IRC Chapter 11 (energy) NOT adopted.
- **R.8-1202 / R.8-1203** — wind and seismic design categories come from "previously
  published maps by the South Carolina Building Codes Council," and "the local building
  official may delineate" them, bounded above by the Applied Technology Council website.
- **R.8-1213** — sprinklers "shall not be required" in one- and two-family dwellings or
  in townhouses built to R302.2.
- **R.8-1208** — R303.4 whole-house mechanical ventilation NOT adopted.
- **R.8-1212** — R312.2 window fall protection NOT adopted (nor .1, .2).
- **R.8-1247 / R.8-1106** — surge protection deleted twice (IRC E3606.5 "deleted without
  substitution"; NEC Article 230.67 "does not apply in this State").
- **R.8-1104** — NEC 210.8(F) outdoor outlets "does not apply in this State."
  **R.8-1251** — walk-out basement receptacles excluded from basement GFCI.
- **R.8-1245** — IRC P2903.10 hose bibb "deleted without substitution."
- **TERMITES — R.8-1215/1216/1217.** Seventh treatment method via "Section 27-1085 of
  the Rules and Regulations for the Enforcement of the SC Pesticide Control Act and
  enforced by the **Clemson University Department of Pesticide Regulation**." In "very
  heavy" termite areas foam plastics "shall not be installed on the exterior face or
  under interior or exterior foundation walls or slab foundations located below grade";
  6-inch clearance above grade; 6-inch crawl space termite inspection gap; and a
  "continuous 6 inch strip centered along the sill plate… left open for termite activity
  inspection." **This is an insulation purchasing constraint, not paperwork.**
- **CRAWL AND SLAB — R.8-1221/1222/1223/1225.** Unvented crawl: ASTM E1745 Class A
  retarder, 6-inch laps, 6 inches up the stem wall, plus one of four conditioning
  options (1 cfm per 50 sq ft exhaust, 1 cfm per 50 sq ft conditioned supply, M1601.5
  plenum in existing structures, or dehumidification per manufacturer). R408.8 "deleted
  without substitution." Slab: minimum **10-mil** Class A retarder, 6-inch laps, with
  five exceptions. Access: 18×24 in through the floor, 16×24 in through a perimeter wall.
- **PIERS — R.8-1220 / R.8-1226.** Exterior bearing-wall girder piers: solidly filled,
  "a minimum of one #4 dowel mid-depth," min nominal 8 in, max height **4×** nominal
  thickness (10× only on accepted engineering practice). Interior: 10×.
- **R.8-1207** — R302.13 fire protection of floors IS adopted, modified, with exceptions
  for floors over a crawl space and for 2×10 nominal or greater framing.
- **R.8-1210** risers 7¾ in (masonry stairs 8 in). **R.8-1211** guards over 30 in and
  where a downward slope exceeds 3V:12H within 36 in; "Insect screening shall not be
  considered as a guard." **R.8-1219** habitable attic is a story above grade plane with
  a four-part exception.
- **R.8-1224 / R.8-1227** — truss design drawings "provided to the building official at
  the time of inspection" and "with the shipment of trusses delivered to the job site,"
  twelve enumerated contents.
- **R.8-1234** dryer duct max 35 ft; **R.8-1232** backdraft damper, "screens shall not be
  installed at the duct termination"; **R.8-1241** water service "not less than 12 inches
  deep and not less than 6 inches below the frost line"; **R.8-1239** shower liner test
  at the final plumbing inspection, 15 minutes.
- **APPENDICES ADOPTED — R.8-1255/1256/1257**: AH Patio Covers, AJ Existing Buildings,
  **AQ Tiny Houses**. Under R.8-236(D) an appendix is unenforceable unless named at
  adoption; these three were named.
- **R.8-1218** — the ONLY SC flood modification (R322.1): floodways per ASCE 24; "Where
  there is a conflict with this code and a locally adopted flood ordinance, the more
  restrictive provision shall apply."

### Why the kit prints no wind speed table [V]
**§ 6-9-105(C)**: "Where a boundary for a physical or climatological condition is
referenced in a code, the council… is required to define the boundary so that it
approximates the physical or climatological area, using logical geographic features such
as major highways, waterbodies, or ridgelines. **Political boundaries may not be used
unless they approximate the physical area.**" A wind-speed-by-county table is an
approximation of a boundary the statute specifically declines to draw on county lines.
The kit prints the mechanism and a write-in line. Our published guide prints county
ranges; see the error list.

### Penalties [V]
§ 6-9-80(B): first violation, civil fine "not more than two hundred dollars," and
"seven calendar days to remedy the violation **or submit a plan**." (C): thereafter "not
to exceed two thousand dollars. Each day a violation continues is a separate offense."
(A): an "adjacent or neighboring property owner who would be damaged by the violation"
may seek injunctive relief or mandamus.
§ 6-10-50(E): energy non-compliance — the official "shall revoke the permit."
§ 6-10-50(F): no occupancy without a certificate of occupancy.

### The permit as evidence — § 15-3-640 [V]
Two provisions no owner-builder guide prints:
1. "A building permit for the construction of an improvement to real property **must
   contain in bold type notice** to the owner or possessor of the property of his rights
   under this section to contract for a guarantee of the structure being free from
   defective or unsafe conditions beyond eight years after substantial completion."
2. "a certificate of occupancy issued by a county or municipality, in the case of new
   construction… **shall constitute proof of substantial completion**… unless the
   contractor and owner, by written agreement, establish a different date."
Repose is **eight years** (2005 Act No. 27 § 2 cut it from thirteen). § 15-3-670(A)
withdraws the defense from "a person in actual possession or control, as owner" who knew
or should have known of the defect — an owner-builder who stays in the house is on both
sides of it. § 15-3-670(B): a code violation "does not constitute per se fraud, gross
negligence, or recklessness, but… may be admissible as evidence."

### Two exemptions that live in the licensing chapter, not the code [V]
- **§ 40-59-265** (2022 Act No. 186, eff. 16 May 2022) — a **statewide statutory list of
  work exempt from building permit application requirements** and from Chapter 59
  licensing. Full list printed in SC.5. Most states leave this to the local official.
- **§ 6-9-65** — farm structures are outside the building code, **but only if** "before
  constructing a farm structure" the owner files an affidavit with the building official
  stating it is being built as one, with a statement of intended use. FEMA NFIP standards
  still apply, a permit may still be required, and a structure "originally qualifying as
  a 'farm structure' but later converted to another use" is not one.

## COASTAL AND FLOOD

*(Findings recovered from the coastal researcher; raw sources in
scratchpad/sc-coastal/. Agency history at the top of this dossier.)*

### The beachfront machinery is geometric, not flood-zone based [V]
- **Baseline** = crest of the primary oceanfront sand dune (§ 48-39-280(A)); the dune is
  defined at Regs. 30-1(D)(45) as min 36 in high and roughly continuous over 500
  shore-parallel feet.
- **Setback line** = **40 × the average annual erosion rate**, and **never less than
  20 ft** landward of the baseline (§ 48-39-280(B); Regs. 30-1(D)(48)).
- **Seaward of the baseline**: no new construction or reconstruction except a short
  statutory list; the only route is a **special permit** with a binding removal
  agreement (§ 48-39-290(A), (D)).
- **Partly seaward of the setback line**: a new habitable structure needs a written
  **certification/notification — not a permit — and there is no fee**. Conditions:
  **≤ 5,000 sq ft heated space**, sited "as far landward on the property as
  practicable," drawing + footprint + cross-section, no erosion-control device integral
  to the house, no part on the primary dune or seaward of the baseline
  (§ 48-39-290(B)(1)(a); Regs. 30-16(A)(1)). Via SCDES ePermitting.
- **All other construction between the lines** needs a department permit
  (§ 48-39-290(B)(4)); a Major Beachfront Critical Area Permit fee is **$1,000**.
- **Hard-armor ban** (§ 48-39-290(B)(2)(a)): no new seawall/bulkhead/revetment seaward of
  the setback line; a grandfathered device destroyed >50% above grade after 30 June 2005
  **must be removed at the owner's expense**; no enlarging, strengthening or rebuilding.
- "Destroyed beyond repair" = **>66⅔%** of replacement value (§ 48-39-270(11)).
- Dune vegetation destruction seaward of the setback line prohibited (§ 48-39-310).
  Seller must disclose baseline and setback line (§ 48-39-330).
- Enforcement $100–$1,000/day; unpermitted activity is an "act of concealment" defeating
  the three-year limitations period (§ 48-39-170(C)).
- **The "40-year retreat policy" phrase was DELETED by 2018 Act No. 173.** Do not print
  it; guides still quote it.
- Critical Area Permit (§ 48-39-130) reaches docks, bulkheads and tidelands across the
  eight coastal counties (Beaufort, Berkeley, Charleston, Colleton, Dorchester, Horry,
  Jasper, Georgetown — § 48-39-10(B)). Beachfront lines exist only on Atlantic-fronting
  beaches (27 beaches in the SCDES viewer at gis.des.sc.gov/shoreline/). **[H]** that
  this is 5 counties — print the beach list, not a county list.

### Lines review cycle — LIVE REVISION TRIPWIRE [V]
Lines are re-established every 7–10 years, staggered, with old lines operative until new
ones are established (§ 48-39-280(C)–(D)). **Phase I lines effective 24 July 2026**
(Beaufort County: Hilton Head, Fripp, Hunting, Harbor, Daufuskie). **Phase II (greater
Charleston: Folly, Sullivan's, Isle of Palms, Kiawah, Seabrook, Dewees, Morris, plus the
Edisto set) running through 2028** — no proposed lines published as of September 2026.
Line appeal: within 1 year, $100 per property, by certified mail to the Division
(§ 48-39-280(F) as amended by 2026 Act No. 146). The viewer is approximate — "a
property-specific survey must be performed."

### Flood [V]
**South Carolina imposes NO state freeboard number.** "Freeboard" appears zero times in
Regs. Ch. 8 and Ch. 30; there is no floodplain chapter in Titles 48/49; § 6-9-135 was
repealed in 2010. The only SC flood modification is **Reg. 8-1218**, which amends R322.1
only and says the more restrictive of the code and a locally adopted flood ordinance
applies. **There is no state V-zone permit** — SC jurisdiction is the setback line, not
the FEMA zone ("V zone" appears zero times in Ch. 39 and Regs. Ch. 30).
Freeboard is therefore local. Only verified example: **Hilton Head Island = 3 ft**
(A/AE lowest floor and V-zone lowest horizontal member ≥3 ft above BFE or 13 ft MSL,
whichever is higher). SCDNR administers the NFIP (dnr.sc.gov/water/flood) and publishes
no jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction freeboard table.

## SEPTIC AND WELLS

Both are **SCDES**. R.61-56 and R.61-44 now carry statutory authority including
"2023 Act No. 60, effective July 1, 2024."

### Septic process [V]
- Application requires a boundary plat, deed or other legal document specifying lot size;
  a legal description with boundary lengths for lots ≤2 acres; written owner permission
  for Department access (§ 104.1(1)–(3)).
- **Backhoe pits are required above the Fall Line** separating the Piedmont from the
  Coastal Plain (§ 104.1(5)).
- **Before the site evaluation the applicant must**: clear and mark boundary lines and
  corners; post an identification marker in the front center of the lot; **stake the
  corners of the proposed building**; mark the proposed stub-out, septic tank and drain
  field area; locate the proposed or existing well; identify other structures that may
  influence the layout; and include a site sketch. Underbrush clearing may be required
  (§ 104.1(6)).
- Permit to construct AND approval to operate both required (§ 104.2(1)).
- **Two documents, both required**: a permit to construct AND an approval to operate.
  The **five-year** validity in § 104.2(3) is the shelf life of the *unbuilt* permit to
  construct. Once installed and approved to operate, "the construction and operation
  permit **remains in effect for the life of the onsite wastewater system** that it
  authorizes" (§ 101.1 definition of "Permit"). A finished system does not expire. [V]
- Covering a system not yet finally inspected is a violation; installation documentation
  due within **two business days** (§ 104.3).
- Tank: **minimum 1,000 gallons** net liquid capacity, sufficient for four bedrooms or
  fewer; **+250 gallons per bedroom over four** (§ 201.1(3)). Design flow **120 gpd per
  bedroom** (§ 501).
- Repair area **50%** of the original system (100% for community systems), free of
  impervious materials, buildings, setbacks and easements; ground between trenches does
  not count (§ 200.7).
- § 200.6(3): a wetlands jurisdictional determination may be required before permitting,
  with approval from the Corps or **SCDES Coastal Management** as applicable.

### Wells [V]
- **An owner may construct or abandon a well on their own property for their own personal
  use only, without an LLR license and without bonding** (R.61-44 definition of "well
  driller", and § C.1). Distinctive — contrast Louisiana.
- Still requires coverage under a **general permit** via a **Notice of Intent**. SCDES
  must transmit approval, comments or denial **within 48 hours** excluding weekends and
  state holidays, and "If notice is not given to the applicant by the end of the 48 hour
  period, coverage under the general permit… will be considered approved." A separate
  48 hours' notice of the installation date is required and may run concurrently
  (R.61-44 § D). Emergency replacement: NOI and fee within 24 hours (§ E).
- Construction (R.61-71 § F): grout the full annular space from a minimum depth of
  **20 feet**; grouted in place **within five days** of borehole completion; casing
  extends **at least one foot above land surface** (less permitted with a protective
  wellhead cover for an above-ground pump); casing set into the first confining layer or
  20 feet, whichever is greater; **sanitary seal** on top of the casing; disinfection on
  completion to a chlorine residual of **50–250 ppm for at least four hours**; durable
  identification plate showing driller company and certification number, completion date,
  total depth and casing depth; **Water Well Record Form 1903 within thirty days** after
  completion.
- Drillers are certified per **S.C. Code § 40-23-10 et seq.**; analytical data must come
  from an SC Certified Laboratory (R.61-81).
- R.61-113 Groundwater Use and Reporting is cross-referenced from R.61-71 for wells
  producing >3 million gallons in any month, but **residential wells are exempt outright
  at R.61-113 § J.1.d, with no volume qualifier** — the volume threshold never has to be
  reached or argued. Capacity-use-area designations cannot catch a residential well
  either. [V]
- Well general permit fee: **$70** (R.61-30 § (10)); septic site evaluation fee $150.
  Deliberately not printed — fees move. [V]

### Greater offsets by system standard — R.61-56 Appendices [V]
**The 125 ft figure attaches to WATER BODIES, not property lines** — it replaces the
75 ft in § 200.6(1)(d) for these standards:
```
septic_system | critical area line / tidal waters, or OHW of environmentally sensitive waters | 125 | App. F (240/241), H (260/261), N (420/421), O (431), P (601)
septic_system | OHW within the banks of environmentally sensitive waters                      | 125 | App. J (280/281), L (370/371)
septic_system | critical area line / tidal waters, where zone of saturation < 15 in           | 125 | App. Q (610)  [non-tidal stays at 75]
```
App. O (431) measures its 125 ft "from the outer edge of the aggregate bed within the
mound."

**The 75 ft PROPERTY LINE figure is conditional** — it applies to contiguous lots in new
subdivisions approved after the standard's effective date, and is measured from the fill
taper / aggregate bed, not the trench:
```
septic_system | property line, new-subdivision contiguous lots | 75 | App. L (370/371), N (420/421), O (431), P (601)
septic_system | property line, unconditional buffer            | 50 | App. P (601) only, measured from the retaining wall
```
For the fill-cap standards (E, F, H, K, L, N) the property-line setback is measured
**from where the fill taper meets natural grade**; the taper adds roughly 15–20 ft per
side (App. N § (2)(b)(iv)–(v)). On a small subdivision lot a mounded system can consume
the buildable area outright — this is the highest-value warning the Studio can surface.

**"Environmentally sensitive waters" is NOT a coastal term** — and this is the trap that
makes the 125 ft rule bite in the Upstate. R.61-56 defines it as outstanding resource
waters, shellfish harvesting waters and trout-natural waters "and including **lakes
greater than forty (40) acres in size** and the Atlantic Ocean, regardless of their
classifications." So a lakefront lot anywhere in the state on a fill-cap or mounded
system is inside the 125 ft setback. The Studio should trigger on lake area, not on
coastal geography. [V on the definition; the specific lake list is [H] — print the
40-acre rule, not a list of lakes.]
Note also two different water marks that are not interchangeable: § 200.6(1)(d) uses
"mean high water (within the banks) elevation," the appendices use "ordinary high water
elevation within the banks." The regulation defines neither. [V]

## OPEN QUESTIONS — for the next revision

1. **TOP PRIORITY. Does the adopted 2021 IRC supply a BFE + 1 ft floor in South
   Carolina?** Reg. 8-1218 modifies R322.1 only; R322.2.1 (lowest floor) and R322.3.2
   (coastal high-hazard lowest horizontal structural member) appear unmodified and would
   ride along. Verify against FEMA Building Science material for the 2021 I-Codes (which
   quote R322 verbatim), an ICC print copy, or a jurisdiction that republishes the text.
   If confirmed, the kit gains a genuinely useful statewide floor for coastal readers.
2. ~~Which R.61-56 system standards carry the greater setbacks?~~ **CLOSED** by the
   gap-fill pass — see the extraction above. The working assumption was wrong in a way
   worth recording: the **125 ft figure attaches to water bodies, not property lines**,
   and the 75 ft property-line figure is **conditional on new-subdivision contiguous
   lots**. Both the dossier and SC.3 were corrected. A third figure nobody had flagged:
   Appendix P carries an unconditional 50 ft property-line buffer.
3. **Is there a department-issued statewide owner-builder disclosure form?**
   § 40-59-260(C) says the disclosure is "provided by the department." Not found on
   llr.sc.gov — only county renderings. If a state form exists it belongs in the kit
   verbatim.
4. **What does "promptly" mean for the § 40-59-260(E) filing?** No day count. Charleston,
   Greenville and Sumter all fold it into permit issuance, which inverts the statute's
   placement. Sample more counties to see whether pre-permit recording is the norm.
5. **Can an owner-builder file the § 29-5-23 Notice of Project Commencement in their own
   name?** The statute is drafted for a contractor-led job. Ask two or three clerks of
   court.
6. **Do any counties restrict homeowner-performed electrical or plumbing work?**
   Charleston bars homeowner permits on manufactured homes; the City of Aiken publishes
   the clearest affirmative statement. Sample more.
7. **Does any SC county require workers' comp proof at residential permit?** Charleston
   does not. Unresolved elsewhere.
8. **Phase II beachfront lines** (greater Charleston) — none published as of September
   2026, running through 2028. Re-pull before every revision.
9. **Is a newer code edition pending?** R.8-240(A) requires adoption at least every four
   years; the current suite was adopted 6 October 2021 with a 1 January 2023
   implementation date, so a 2024/2025-edition cycle is plausibly in motion. Check the
   Council's agendas, minutes and final orders, and the State Register.
10. **Confirm the MASC → Carolinas AGC handoff (1 January 2026)** of municipal trade
    certifications and what it means for existing cards.
