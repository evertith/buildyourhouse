## 0. THE HEADLINE — WHAT MAKES THIS KIT DIFFERENT

Five findings that no Wisconsin owner-builder guide in circulation has right.

1. **[V] Wisconsin left the 2017 NEC on 1 September 2026 — the day before this
   kit was built.** s. SPS 316.007(1)(a) now incorporates *"NFPA 70 National
   Electrical Code, (NEC) – **2023**, including all Temporary Interim
   Amendments and Errata prior to January 1, 2025."* Amendment CR 26-016,
   Register June 2026 No. 846, **eff. 9-1-26**. Every existing guide, including
   our own state guide, says 2017. Six editions of change landed at once.

2. **[V] Wisconsin publishes two "Zone 1 / Zone 2" maps in the UDC and they are
   numbered in opposite directions.** Figure 321.02 (roof loads): **Zone 1 =
   the north, 40 psf; Zone 2 = the south, 30 psf.** s. SPS 322.31(1)(b)
   (energy): **zone 2 = the 15 named northern counties; zone 1 = everywhere
   else.** Carry a zone number across and you get both answers wrong in
   opposite directions. Checkable in a minute; ideal product-page highlight.

3. **[V] There is no permit-free town in Wisconsin, and the exemption has no
   holding period.** s. 101.651(3)(b) makes the department the residual
   enforcer; s. 101.65(2) forces larger municipalities to contract for services
   they do not perform. And the owner-builder exemption is one sentence with
   **no holding period, no resale bar, no affidavit** — the "2 years" that
   circulates is the continuing-education cycle for *certified contractors* in
   s. 101.654(1m)(b)1.

4. **[V] The four trade exemptions are four differently-worded rules, and the
   difference is the tense.** Dwelling contractor and HVAC say *"resides or
   will reside"*; electrical and plumbing say *"owns and occupies"* / *"owned
   and occupied"*. Neither of the latter two says "new construction". The kit
   prints all four side by side and refuses to resolve the ambiguity — but it
   does print the fact that settles the practical question on plumbing:
   s. SPS 320.09(9)(a)7. puts *"the name and license number of the Wisconsin
   master plumber"* on the permit at issuance.

5. **[V] Septic first — by statute.** s. 145.195(1): no municipality may issue
   a building permit for a structure needing a POWTS unless the septic permits
   *"have been obtained"*. Mirrored at s. SPS 320.09(9)(c). And the owner
   **may not install the POWTS** (s. SPS 383.21(2)(c)4. requires a named master
   plumber) while the owner **may drill their own well** (s. NR 812.10(1)(a)).
   That asymmetry is the shape of a rural Wisconsin build.

## 1. THE OWNER-BUILDER EXEMPTION

### 1.1 The duty and the exception [V]

> **s. 101.654(1)(a)** *"Subject to par. (b), no person may obtain a building
> permit unless the person annually obtains from the department a certificate
> of financial responsibility showing that the person is in compliance with
> sub. (2), completes the continuing education requirements described under
> sub. (1m), and furnishes to the issuer of the permit proof of completion of
> those continuing education requirements."*

> **s. 101.654(1)(b)** *"Paragraph (a) does not apply to an owner of a dwelling
> who resides or will reside in the dwelling and who applies for a building
> permit to perform work on that dwelling."*

Confirmed in the rule — **Note to s. SPS 305.31** repeats it verbatim.

### 1.2 Corrections to the working hypotheses [V]

| Hypothesis in the brief | What the source actually says |
|---|---|
| s. 101.654 is titled "Financial responsibility; certification of contractors" | Titled **"Contractor certification; education."** |
| The exemption is at s. 101.654(2)(a) | It is at **s. 101.654(1)(b)**. Sub. (2) is the *proof* an applicant supplies and contains no exemption |
| There is a ~2-year sale/rent restriction | **There is none.** Full text of s. 101.654, SPS 305.31 and SPS 305.315 read; no holding period, no resale bar, no affidavit, no dollar cap |
| Relevant rules are at SPS 305.60–305.65 | Those are inspector credentials. The dwelling-contractor rules are **SPS 305.31 and SPS 305.315** |
| HVAC is not state-licensed | **Wrong as stated.** s. 101.178(2) makes HVAC **registration** mandatory for anyone in the business; the exam-based *certification* is voluntary (s. 101.178(3)(a)) |
| Wis. Stat. s. 145.075 contains the plumbing exemption | **s. 145.075 does not exist.** The whole exemption is s. 145.06(4)(a) |
| UDC applies from 1 June 1980 | Both dates are real. **s. SPS 320.03**: ch. SPS 322 effective **12-1-1978**; chs. SPS 320, 321, 323, 324, 325 effective **6-1-1980**. Statute s. 101.615 sets the outer boundary at 12-1-1978 |
| Enforcement is eroded in small towns | **Wrong as a matter of law.** s. 101.651(3)(b) + s. 101.65(2) close every gap. What varies is *who* you call, not whether a permit is required |
| Electrical is SPS 324 | SPS 324 is a **pointer chapter** to ch. SPS 316 (as SPS 325 points to chs. SPS 381–387) |

### 1.3 What the exemption does and does not lift [V]

Lifted: **both** dwelling-contractor credentials — the exception clause sits in
the stem of s. SPS 305.31(1) and so reaches par. (a) (certification) and
par. (b) (qualifier). Not lifted: the permit (s. SPS 320.08(1)), the code
(s. 101.66(1) names *"every builder, designer, and **owner**"*), the
inspections (s. SPS 320.10), the cautionary statement (s. 101.65(1r)), and the
county sanitary permit.

**The real cost of the exemption:** a certified contractor has shown the
department a bond of *"not less than $5,000"* or liability cover of *"at least
$250,000 per occurrence"* (s. 101.654(2)(a)). An exempt owner has neither
standing behind the work.

### 1.4 The tense trap — all four texts [V]

| Trade | Words used | Reaches an unoccupied new house on its face? | Cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| Building / dwelling contractor | *"resides **or will reside**"* | **Yes**, expressly | s. 101.654(1)(b) |
| HVAC | *"**resides or will reside**"* | **Yes**, expressly | s. SPS 305.70(1)(b)2. |
| Electrical | *"premises that the property owner **owns and occupies** as a residence"* | **Not on its face** | s. 101.862(4)(a) |
| Plumbing | *"in a **one-family building owned and occupied** by him or her as his or her home or farm building"* | **Not on its face** | s. 145.06(4)(a) |

Three further limits, all [V]:
- The plumbing exemption reaches a **one-family** building only. An
  owner-builder putting up a **duplex** gets the dwelling-contractor exemption
  but not the plumbing one.
- Both electrical and plumbing exemptions can be **switched off by local
  ordinance** (*"unless a license or registration issued by the department is
  required by local ordinance"* / *"except where such license is required by
  local ordinance"*). Nothing lets a local ordinance widen them.
- **[H] How DSPS or a court resolves the tense question for new construction
  could not be verified.** The kit prints both texts, prints the drafting
  contrast against s. 101.654(1)(b), and sends the reader to the AHJ. It does
  **not** assert an answer either way. See OPEN QUESTIONS.

### 1.5 HVAC — the friendly trade, with a federal catch [V]

Registration is mandatory but requires **no examination** (SPS 305.70(2)–(3)
ask only for an application, fees, and that the applicant be a principal).
Certification is *"a **voluntary** program"* by the statute's own words. The
owner exemption uses the good wording. And HVAC is the **only** trade where
local licensing is pre-empted: s. 101.178(3)(d) bars a political subdivision
from requiring its own approval unless it did so before **1 November 1993**.

The catch is federal, printed as a Note to s. SPS 305.70: refrigerant work
requires *"a Type I, II, III, or Universal technician certification issued in
accordance with section 608 of the federal Clean Air Act and title 40 CFR part
82, subpart F."* State exemption ≠ federal exemption.

### 1.6 Septic — a hard no for the owner [V]

Three provisions close it: POWTS work *is* plumbing by definition
(s. 145.01(10)(a)2. — plumbing includes *"private on-site wastewater treatment
systems"*); the plumbing exemption reaches work *"in a one-family **building**"*
and a dispersal cell is not in the building; and the sanitary permit
application cannot be completed without *"documentation that the master plumber
or the master plumber-restricted service who is to be responsible for the
installation"* (s. SPS 383.21(2)(c)4.). Counties may not add their own
installer license (s. 145.04(2)).

### 1.7 Hiring help [V]

Worker's compensation triggers, s. 102.04(1)(b): **three or more employees**,
attaching *"on the day on which"*; or **$500 or more in wages in a calendar
quarter**, attaching *"on the 10th day of the month next succeeding such
quarter."* Farming has its own threshold at (1)(c). Duty to insure:
s. 102.28(2)(a).

The nine-part independent contractor test at s. 102.07(8)(b) is **conjunctive**
— *"meets **all** of the following conditions."* Paying by the hour, with your
tools, on your schedule fails conditions 1, 3, 4 and 6 simultaneously.

**[H]** Whether a person building their own home is engaged in a *"trade,
business, profession or occupation"* at all under s. 102.07(4)(a)2. — and so
whether their helpers are "employees" for counting purposes — is genuinely
open. s. 102.07(4)(b) narrows the escape (*"however casual, unusual, desultory
or isolated"*). No DSPS or DWD guidance and no case found. **Not resolved in
the kit**; the statutory text and thresholds are printed with a
confirm-with-DWD instruction.

### 1.8 Two Wisconsin oddities worth the price of the kit [V]

**Owner-milled lumber, s. 101.66(1m).** Ungraded load-bearing dimension lumber
may be used where it *"has been milled at the request of the person owning the
lumber for use in the construction of the dwelling, and the dwelling will be
inhabited by the person owning the lumber"*, or bought direct from the miller
by the future occupant. The miller must give a written certification and must
hold a **certificate of accomplishment** from the s. 36.25(48) lumber grading
program received *"within the 5 years before"*. The inspector may still
*"reject the use of the lumber, or authorize its use subject to more
restrictive construction requirements, including requirements as to size,
spacing, length of spans, and design."*

**The hunting-cabin exemption is grandfathered, not available.** s. 101.61(3)(d)
requires the structure to have been *"constructed before December 31, 1997"*,
or be an alteration to or replacement of one. **You cannot build a new one.**
And (a) requires that it *"is not used as a home or residence"*.

## 2. ENFORCEMENT — WHO INSPECTS

### 2.1 The statutory chain [V]

- **s. 101.65(1)(a)** — cities, villages, towns and counties **may** take
  jurisdiction by ordinance; *"a county ordinance shall apply in any city,
  village, or town that has not enacted those ordinances."*
- **s. 101.65(2)** — a municipality *"**shall** contract with the department
  for those inspection services which the municipality does not perform."*
- **s. 101.651(1)** — for this section, "municipality" means one with a
  **population of 2,500 or less**.
- **s. 101.651(2m)** — such a municipality must enforce, or pass a resolution
  asking the county, or fall to the department.
- **s. 101.651(3)(b)** — *"The department **shall** provide inspection services
  and **shall** enforce this subchapter throughout any municipality that does
  not exercise jurisdiction under sub. (2m) and that has not adopted a
  resolution under sub. (2m) (a)."*
- **s. SPS 320.08(1)** — the permit comes from the municipality *"or from a
  registered UDC inspection agency … in a municipality where the department has
  jurisdiction"*, obtained *"before any on-site construction, **including
  excavation for a structure**, may begin."*
- **s. SPS 320.08(2)** — *"A person who obtains a Wisconsin uniform building
  permit from a registered UDC inspection agency **shall retain the same
  agency** to conduct the inspections for the project."*
- **s. SPS 320.06(1)(b)** — a municipality declares one of four enforcement
  methods: individual, joint, **contract with a certified UDC inspector or
  independent inspection agency**, or contract with another municipality.
- Annotation on s. 101.65: *"Municipalities may contract with independent
  contractors to provide inspection services under this section. Giffin v.
  Poetzl, 2001 WI App 207."*

### 2.2 The two DSPS lookup documents [V]

This is the kit's most practically useful content and it is not widely known.

| **UDC Permit and Inspection Map** — `dsps.wi.gov/Documents/Programs/Maps/UDCPermitInspectionMap.pdf` | Revised **2 April 2026**; one page, all 72 counties shaded, headed *"Who is my contact for permitting and inspection services?"* |

**13 counties hold UDC delegation** [V]: Adams, Bayfield, Buffalo, Chippewa,
Eau Claire, Florence, Forest, Langlade, Marquette, Richland, Trempealeau,
Waupaca, Waushara.

**Named state-contracted agencies on the map** [V]: REM Inspecting (Burnett);
T.A.K. Inspections (Ashland, Sawyer, Rusk); RC Inspection Services (Iron,
Vilas); General Engineering (Clark-South, Wood, Juneau, Vernon, Sauk, Columbia,
Dane, Dodge, Jefferson, Waukesha, Washington, Fond du Lac, Green Lake,
Winnebago, Outagamie); CSC Assessment Services (Taylor, Clark-North, Marathon,
Portage). Remaining counties: email DSPS.

**Two reader traps** [V]:
- The **"Opt-Out"** value on the list refers to **camping units (ch. SPS 327)**,
  not the UDC. Chippewa, Eau Claire and Waushara show Opt-Out there and still
  hold full UDC delegation.
- **Clark County is split** north/south between two different agencies.

**No public spreadsheet exists.** The only .xlsx on the delegation pages is a
municipal fee-report template. The Delegated Agents page is written for
municipalities applying for delegation — do not send owners there. [V]

**DSPS's own words** [V]: *"The UDC is enforced in all Wisconsin
municipalities."* And from its owner brochure: *"Regardless of permit
requirements, state statutes require compliance with the UDC rules by owners
and builders even if there is no enforcement."*

### 2.3 Owner protections in the enforcement statutes [V]

- **s. 101.65(1c)** — a nonconforming municipal ordinance is unenforceable, and
  where a contract with a municipality requires compliance with one, *"the
  owner may waive the provision, and the provision, if waived, is void and
  unenforceable."*
- **s. 101.65(3)** — a municipality *"may not require that an applicant submit
  the form in **both** paper and electronic forms."*
- **s. 101.65(4)** — if the municipality misses the electronic filing deadline
  it *"shall **refund** to the person to whom the building permit was issued"*
  everything it kept. Conditioned on implementation of the s. 101.63(7m)
  system. **Not printed in the kit** — see DELIBERATELY NOT PRINTED.
- **s. SPS 320.02(2)(a)** — occupancy may not be restricted *"for any reason
  other than noncompliance with the provisions of this code."*
- **s. SPS 320.09(6)(c)** — *"a municipality exercising jurisdiction **may not
  require plans or calculations to be stamped or sealed** by an architect or
  engineer"*, except under s. SPS 321.33 (floodplain).

### Inspections [V] — s. SPS 320.10(3)(b) to (i), eight types

Erosion control (*"concurrently with all other required construction
inspections"*) · foundation excavation (plus bleeders where drain tile is
required) · foundation reinforcement · foundation (before backfill where
dampproofing, exterior insulation or drain tile are required) · **rough**, in
five categories (basement floor area; general construction including framing;
rough electrical; rough plumbing; rough HVAC) · insulation and vapor retarders
before concealment · final · installation (manufactured/modular only).

**The three clocks** — the kit's most valuable practical content:
- **Two business days**: *"Construction may proceed if the inspection has not
  taken place by the end of the second business day following the day of
  notification"* (s. SPS 320.10(2)(b)3.).
- **Five business days**: *"Occupancy may proceed in accordance with local
  ordinances if the inspection has not been completed by the end of the fifth
  business day following the day of notification"* (s. SPS 320.10(3)(h)2.).
- **Next workday** for the POWTS: the master plumber *"may proceed with the
  installation of the POWTS, including backfilling and covering"*
  (s. SPS 383.26(2)(e)).

The applicant may request **one combined rough or individual roughs**
(s. SPS 320.10(3)(f)3.), with a separate fee permitted for each (subd. 4.).

*"Code"* is defined at s. SPS 320.07(16) as **chs. SPS 320 to 325**.
SPS 320 administration · 321 construction · 322 energy · 323 HVAC ·
**324 electrical → pointer to ch. SPS 316** · **325 plumbing → pointer to

### 4.2 ⚠ The electrical transition [V]

> **s. SPS 316.007(1) PRIMARY STANDARDS.** *"The following standard is
> incorporated by reference into this chapter, subject to the modifications
> specified in this chapter: (a) National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) …
> **NFPA 70 National Electrical Code, (NEC) – 2023**, including all Temporary
> Interim Amendments and Errata prior to January 1, 2025."*

**CR 26-016, Register June 2026 No. 846, effective 1 September 2026.**

### 4.5 Structure — SPS 321 [V]

**Frost, s. SPS 321.16(1)(a):** *"Footings and foundations, **including those
for landings and stoops**, shall be placed below the frost penetration level or
at least **48 inches** below adjacent grade, **whichever is deeper**."*
Exceptions at (2): ASCE 32-01 frost-protected shallow foundation; footing under
a window areaway; bearing directly on bedrock with the rock cleaned, clay
removed from crevices to the frost level or 1.5× the crevice width whichever is
less, and water prevented from collecting.

**Table 321.02-1 — live load (psf):** Floors 40 · Garage floors 50 · Exterior
balconies, decks, porches 40 · Ceilings (with storage) 20 · Ceilings (without
storage) 5.

**Wind, s. SPS 321.02(1)(c):** *"either a horizontal and uplift pressure of 20
pounds per square foot acting over the surface area or the wind loads
determined in accordance with ASCE 7-05."*

**Snow, Figure 321.02 "Zone Map for Roof Loads"** — legend read off the
rendered figure: **Zone 1 = 40 P.S.F., Zone 2 = 30 P.S.F.**, with Zone 1
covering the northern part of the state.

### 4.6 ⚠ THE INVERTED-ZONE FINDING [V]

| | Roof/snow — Figure 321.02 | Energy — s. SPS 322.31(1)(b) |
|---|---|---|
| **Zone 1** | the **NORTH**, 40 psf | **everywhere else** (south/central) |
| **Zone 2** | the **SOUTH**, 30 psf | the **15 northern counties** |

s. SPS 322.31(1)(b) verbatim: *"In Tables 322.31-1 and 322.31-2, zone 2

is roof-load **Zone 2 (30 psf)** and energy **Zone 1 (R-20 walls, R-30
floors)**.

## 5. SEPTIC, WELLS AND LAND

### 5.1 POWTS [V]

Sanitary permit authority is **the county**: s. 145.01(5) defines the
responsible governmental unit as *"the county except that in a county with a
population of 750,000 or more these terms mean the city, village or town."*
The 750,000 threshold reaches **Milwaukee County** ([V] on the rule; [H] on the
county count — the kit prints the rule, not the county name).

s. 59.70(5)(a): every responsible unit *"shall enact an ordinance"* covering
*"the entire area"*, and *"no municipality may enact or enforce"* a POWTS
ordinance unless it is that responsible unit — a town cannot invent its own
septic rules. s. 145.20(1)(a) lets the county assign the program *"to any
office, department, committee, board, commission, position or employee"*,
which is why the office name differs in every county.

Permit runs to the **owner** (s. 145.19(1g)) — and *"No person may sell at
retail … a septic tank for installation in this state unless the purchaser
holds a valid sanitary permit."* Valid **2 years**, renewable, and *"shall
remain valid to the end of the established period, notwithstanding any change
in the state plumbing code"* (s. 145.19(7)); transferable with the land
(s. 145.19(8)); **$25 statewide groundwater fee** (s. 145.19(6)).

**s. 145.19(1r)** — the county holds prior percolation/soil test results and
*"shall accept the test results as the basis for a sanitary permit application
unless the soil at the test site is altered."* Worth asking before paying for a
new evaluation.

Soil work: **certified soil tester only** (s. SPS 385.10(1); Wis. Stat.
s. 145.045(1) since 1974). **Three soil profile evaluations minimum**, at least
one a full soil pit for flows ≤1,000 gpd (s. SPS 385.20(2)(b)); a boring *"may
not be created by means of a power auger"*.

**Vertical separation is 24 inches, not 3 feet** — s. SPS 383.44(3)(a). The
36-inch figure governed only from 12-1-1969 to 7-1-2000, and the code says so
in the Note to s. SPS 383.32(2)(b).

**Negative finding [V]: there is no soil-depth→system-type table in SPS 383.**
Wisconsin uses a performance model — the 24-inch separation plus a soil
application rate from Table 383.44-2 — and the system type (in-ground, at-grade,
mound) falls out of it. Mound geometry lives in component manuals recognized
under s. SPS 383.60(1), not in the rule.

Maintenance [V]: septic servicing is **volume-triggered** at *"1/3 of the tank
volume"* (s. SPS 383.54(3)(b)); the **3-year interval is a visual inspection
duty on pre-July-2000 systems** (s. SPS 383.54(4)(d)1.). Reporting sits on the
**owner**, within **30 calendar days** (s. SPS 383.55).

### 5.2 Wells [V]

**An owner may drill their own well**: s. NR 812.10(1)(a) — a license is not
required for *"An individual performing well drilling on real estate owned or
leased by that individual."* All construction standards still apply.

**But notification comes first**: Wis. Stat. s. 281.34(3)(a) — *"An owner shall
notify the department of the location of a well that is not a high capacity
well **before construction of the well begins**."* The driller must verify the
notification number exists before starting (s. NR 812.10(8)). The construction
report is filed by the driller with the department **and the owner** within
**30 days**, electronically (s. NR 812.10(11)).

**Counties may add a well permit** under s. 59.70(6)(b) and ch. NR 845.

**Water testing is state-required**, not merely a lender condition:
s. NR 812.46(1)(b) requires total coliform and nitrate samples after new well
construction.

**Casing minimums** (ss. NR 812.13, 812.14): unconsolidated **25 ft** (or 10 ft
below static water level when SWL >15 ft), min. diameter 2 in; sandstone
**30 ft**; crystalline **40 ft**; limestone/dolomite **40 ft**, or **60 ft**
where the bedrock top is less than 20 ft down; bedrock min. diameter 6 in.
Casing terminates *"at least 12 inches above the established ground surface"*
(s. NR 812.29(1)).

### 5.3 Shoreland, floodplain, driveway, stormwater [V]

**Shoreland** — s. 59.692(1c): *"each county **shall** zone by ordinance all
shorelands in its unincorporated area."* Shorelands = **1,000 ft** from a lake,
pond or flowage; **300 ft** from a river or stream or to the landward side of
the floodplain, whichever is greater (s. 59.692(1)(b)). "Structure" includes a
*"sidewalk, stairway, walkway, patio, deck, retaining wall, porch, or **fire
pit**"* (s. 59.692(1)(e)).

NR 115.05(1) minimums: **75-foot** setback from the OHWM, reducible to the
adjacent-lot average but *"may not be reduced to less than 35 feet"* ·
**35-foot** vegetative buffer · **15% impervious** within **300 feet** of the
OHWM, up to 30% with a county-approved mitigation plan *"recorded in the office
of the county register of deeds"* · unsewered lot minimum **100 ft** width and
**20,000 sq ft**; sewered 65 ft and 10,000 sq ft.

**⚠ Live rule-vs-statute conflict [V] on both texts.** NR 115.05(1)(c)2.b caps
the access and viewing corridor at *"the lesser of 30 percent of the shoreline
frontage or 200 feet"*, while Wis. Stat. s. 59.692(1f)(b)1. forbids an
ordinance from setting that maximum below *"10 feet or 35 percent of the
shoreline frontage, whichever is greater"*, subject to the same 200-foot
ceiling. The statutory floor exceeds the rule's ceiling; NR 115 has not been
conformed. **The kit prints the 200-foot cap as certain and sends the reader to
the county for the current corridor figure.**

**Floodplain** — NR 116.03(20): flood protection elevation is *"2 feet above
the regional flood elevation."* NR 116.13(2): lowest floor excluding basement
at or above that; fill *"not less than one foot above the regional flood
elevation"*, extending *"at least 15 feet beyond the limits of any structure"*,
with *"dryland access"*; *"no variance may be granted to allow any floor below
the regional flood elevation."* **NR 116.12(1) prohibits in a floodway: a
structure designed for human habitation, any sewage system, and any well for
human consumption.**

**Driveway** — Wis. Stat. s. 86.07(2)(a): a permit *"from the highway authority
maintaining the highway."* Town road → town board; county trunk → county
highway department; state trunk → WisDOT under ch. Trans 231. Residential rural
driveway spec, s. Trans 231.05: width *"not less than 16 feet nor greater than
24 feet"*, return radius *"not greater than 30 feet"*, approximately right
angles. A state permit *"shall not supersede more restrictive requirements
imposed by valid applicable local ordinances"* (s. Trans 231.01(4)).
s. 84.25 is **controlled-access highways**, not the driveway statute — its
relevance is that direct access may be unavailable.

**Erosion control** — under one acre it rides on the building permit
(s. SPS 321.125; plan review completed *"no later than the 15th working day"*,
s. 101.653(5)(a)4.). Statutory trigger: more restrictive practices required
*"on an area with a slope that is greater than **12 percent**"*
(s. 101.653(2)(b)). At **one acre or more**, authority transferred to DNR by
2013 Wis. Act 20 (Note to s. SPS 321.125): notice of intent *"received by the
department **at least 14 working days** prior to the commencement of any land
disturbing construction activities"* (s. NR 216.44(1)), plan complete first
(s. NR 216.44(2)), and *"**the application fees … shall be doubled**"* if filed
after disturbance begins (s. NR 216.43(4)).

### 6.1 Wisconsin is usable, with one hard limitation

**Two different site plans, two different rules, two different answers on
whether the owner may draw it.**

| | Building-permit site plan | Sanitary-permit site plan |
|---|---|---|
| Rule | s. SPS 320.09(5)(a) | s. SPS 385.40(3)(a) |
| **Owner may draw it?** | **YES** — and a municipality *"may not require plans or calculations to be stamped or sealed by an architect or engineer"* (s. SPS 320.09(6)(c)), except under s. SPS 321.33 (floodplain) | **NO** — credential-gated, see below |
| Scale | *"legible and drawn to scale **or dimensioned**"* — **no numeric scale specified** | *"drawn to scale **or fully dimensioned**"* — **no numeric scale specified**; minimum sheet size letter |

**The gate on the sanitary-permit plan, s. SPS 385.10(2) verbatim:**

> *"A site evaluation, **relative to the installation of a POWTS treatment,
> holding or dispersal component location, or to determine land slope or
> setback distances to topographic or other site features**, shall be performed
> by a Wisconsin registered architect, professional engineer, designer of
> plumbing systems, designer of private sewage systems or land surveyor; a
> certified soil tester or POWTS inspector; or a licensed master plumber or
> master plumber-restricted service."*

That is a closed list and the property owner is not on it. Because the
regulated act expressly includes *"determin[ing] land slope or setback
distances"*, **drawing the sanitary-permit site plan with setbacks on it is
itself the regulated activity.** For the tool: Wisconsin output must be framed
as a **planning drawing for the building permit and for the owner's own
layout**, explicitly not as the sanitary-permit site evaluation.

**Building-permit site plan required contents, s. SPS 320.09(5)(a) verbatim —
the complete statewide list, three items:**

> *"1. The location of the dwelling and any other buildings, **wells**, surface
> waters and **dispersal systems** on the site with respect to **property
> lines** and surface waters adjacent to the site.
> 2. The areas of **land-disturbing construction activity** and the location of
> all **erosion and sediment control measures** to be employed in order to
> comply with s. SPS 321.125.
> 3. The **pre-construction ground surface slope and direction of runoff
> flow** within the proposed areas of land disturbance."*

Plus s. SPS 320.09(6)(d): *"The name of the initial downstream receiving water
of the state from the dwelling shall be identified."*

## 9. LATE ADDENDUM — REVERSALS DURING THE BUILD

**9.1 The NEC edition reversed mid-research.** The brief anticipated the "2017
NEC trap" — several states are frozen on that edition and Wisconsin was one of
them. It stopped being true **on 1 September 2026**, one day before the kit was
built. Everything written about Wisconsin electrical before that date, including
our own state guide, is now stale. This is the single most valuable thing in the
kit and it has a short half-life: it is *new* information for roughly the next
year, after which it becomes ordinary.

**9.2 "Eroded enforcement in small towns" was wrong.** The brief's hypothesis
that UDC enforcement has decayed in small municipalities does not survive
s. 101.651(3)(b) and s. 101.65(2). The kit says so plainly and reframes the
question as *which of four models* covers the parcel. Wisconsin is the opposite
of Kentucky, Montana and Mississippi on this axis, and the kit's positioning
had to be rebuilt around that.

**9.3 The owner exemption is broader than assumed, and the septic answer
narrower.** The brief expected an owner-occupancy holding period; there is
none. The brief did not anticipate that an owner **cannot install their own
septic** while **can drill their own well** — the reverse of most states, and
the sharpest single fact in WI.1.

**9.4 The "3 feet of vertical separation" figure was wrong by a factor the code
itself explains.** 36 inches governed from 12-1-1969 to 7-1-2000. It is
**24 inches** now, and SPS 383.32(2)(b) prints the history. Any Wisconsin
source still saying 3 feet is quoting a rule that expired 26 years ago.

**9.5 The state guide's own energy table has an error.** It lists "Slab edge
(unheated) R-10/15". Table 322.31-1 gives **unheated slab R-10**; the R-10/15
value is the **heated** slab. See the guide error list in the handoff.

**9.6 A cover claim had to be corrected during the visual pass.** The cover
originally read "Then thirteen inspections". s. SPS 320.10(3) names **eight
inspection types**. Changed to "eight kinds of inspection" before ship — a
reminder that the render-and-read step catches claims the text sweep cannot.
