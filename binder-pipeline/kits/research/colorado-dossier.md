# Colorado Owner-Builder Dossier — research for the CO Permit Kit (six documents)

Compiled August 2026. Statute text was read from the OFFICIAL Colorado Revised Statutes 2026 title PDFs published
by the Office of Legislative Legal Services (olls.info/crs, linked from leg.colorado.gov/agencies/
office-legislative-legal-services/2026-crs-titles-download) — full titles downloaded and sections extracted verbatim
with `pdftotext -layout`. Board rules were read from the official PDFs in the Code of Colorado Regulations at
sos.state.co.us/CCR (the CCR site states the PDF is the official version). Agency facts verified against the
agency's own pages. Every claim is tagged:

  [V] = verified against a primary source (statute or rule text read directly, or the administering agency's own
  page). Safe to print with the citation.
  [H] = hedge — could not be pinned to a primary source, is county/city variable, or is a proven-negative that can
  only be stated carefully. Print only with the hedge language and a verification step.

WARNING FOR FUTURE EDITORS: Colorado repealed and reenacted its entire professions title in 2019 (HB 19-1172).
Pre-2019 citations (the old 12-23-xxx electrical, 12-58-xxx plumbing numbers) are dead, and third-party statute
mirrors lag the session laws. Do not cite colorado.public.law or similar; go to leg.colorado.gov.

THE STRUCTURAL HEADLINE (this is the kit's thesis) — THE COLORADO INVERSION: Colorado is the state where the permit
you expect does not exist and the permit you do not expect does. There is no state general-contractor license and
no statewide residential building code — whether a building permit exists at all is decided county-by-county and
city-by-city. BUT electrical and plumbing run on a separate, statewide track: the State Electrical Board and the
State Plumbing Board issue permits and inspect, by default, everywhere a local government does not run its own
qualifying program. So the owner-builder in unincorporated Colorado can face exactly this: no building permit
exists for the house, and a state permit is required before the first wire or the first pipe.

## CO.0 — Cover summary facts (the two-system structure in headlines)

1. [V] No state contractor license. CRS 2026 Title 12 (Professions and Occupations) licenses electricians
   (article 115) and plumbers (article 155) and contains no general-contractor, home-builder, residential-
   construction, HVAC/mechanical, or roofing article. Print as a dated absence ("As of August 2026 no Colorado
   state agency issues a general contractor or home builder license"), never as a timeless one.

2. [V] No statewide residential building code. C.R.S. 30-28-201(1): "A board of county commissioners **is
   authorized to** adopt ordinances and a building code … in all or part of the county, and not embraced within the
   limits of any incorporated city or town. Buildings or structures used for the sole purpose of providing shelter
   for agricultural implements, farm products, livestock, or poultry may be excepted." Permissive, not mandatory.

3. [V] The permit duty is contingent on adoption. C.R.S. 30-28-205(1): "**After the adoption of the building code**,
   it shall be unlawful to erect, construct, reconstruct, alter, or remodel any structure, dwelling, or building in
   the designated area … without first obtaining a building permit from the county building inspector." No
   adoption, no county building permit.

4. [V] Municipalities get their powers from C.R.S. 31-15-601 (an antique enumerated list of building and fire
   regulation powers); home-rule cities set their own under Colo. Const. art. XX.

5. [V] The state trade permits exist regardless. C.R.S. 12-115-120(1)(a)(I) and (2)(a) (electrical);
   12-155-120(1)(a) (plumbing AND gas piping). See CO.1.

6. [V] You may pull them yourself. "Qualified applicant" is defined to include "**a homeowner performing work on
   the homeowner's home**" — C.R.S. 12-115-120(11)(c) and 12-155-120(11)(c), both added by HB 22-1346, effective
   January 1, 2023.

7. [V] You may draw your own plans. C.R.S. 12-120-403(1)(a) puts "One-, two-, three-, and four-family dwellings,
   including accessory buildings commonly associated with those dwellings" outside the architects' practice act,
   and C.R.S. 30-28-205(3) requires an architect's or engineer's seal "unless the preparation of plans and
   specification is exempted by section 12-120-403." Local codes may still demand engineered structure/foundation.

## CO.1 — The owner-builder's legal position

### A. THE TRAP (Colorado's "2017-NEC-equivalent") — the exemption is conditioned on the inspection

This is the sharpest verified item in the kit and appears in no general owner-builder guide found.

- [V] C.R.S. 12-115-116(2), VERBATIM: "Nothing in this article 115 shall be construed to require any individual to
  hold a license before doing electrical work on his or her own property or residence **if all such electrical work,
  except for maintenance or repair of existing facilities, is inspected as provided in this article 115**; if,
  however, the property or residence is intended for sale or resale by a person engaged in the business of
  constructing or remodeling the facilities or structures or is rental property that is occupied or is to be
  occupied by tenants for lodging, either transient or permanent, or is generally open to the public, the owner
  shall be responsible for, and the property shall be subject to, all of the provisions of this article 115
  pertaining to inspection and licensing, unless specifically exempted therein."

  The load-bearing word is "if". Skip the permit and inspection and the exemption never applied to the work,
  leaving unlicensed electrical work behind the drywall.

- [V] C.R.S. 12-155-118(2) (plumbing), VERBATIM in relevant part: "Nothing in this article 155 requires an
  individual to hold a license to perform plumbing work on the individual's own property or residence … except
  that, if such property or residence is intended for sale or resale by a person engaged in the business of
  constructing or remodeling the facilities or structures or is rental property that is occupied or is to be
  occupied by tenants for lodging, either transient or permanent, or is a commercial or industrial building, the
  owner is responsible for and the property is subject to the provisions of this article 155 pertaining to
  **licensing**, unless specifically exempted therein."

  TWO DIFFERENCES, both real: (a) no inspection condition on the plumbing exemption; (b) the exclusion pulls the
  owner into "licensing" only, where the electrical one says "inspection and licensing."

- [V] NO OCCUPANCY CONDITION in either statute. Both say "own property or residence." Guides printing "a home you
  own and live in" are adding words — and the added words are impossible to satisfy for a house under construction.

- [V] The sale/rental exclusions, read together. 12-115-116(2) limits the sale exclusion to property "intended for
  sale or resale **by a person engaged in the business of constructing or remodeling**." But 12-115-116(4) carries
  no such qualifier: "If the property of any person, firm, or corporation is: Rental property or is developed for
  sale, lease, or rental; occupied or is to be occupied by tenants …; or generally open to the public, the property
  is subject to all the provisions of this article 115 pertaining to inspection and licensing; except that the
  maintenance or repair of existing property specified in this subsection (4) is not subject to this article 115."
  Print both; the safe rule is "build it to live in, not to sell or rent."

- [V] Maintenance/repair free pass. 12-115-116(6)(a): no license, no board inspection, no fees for "maintenance or
  repair of existing facilities on his or her own property or residence." 12-115-116(6)(c) defines the term
  narrowly: "to preserve or keep in good repair lawfully installed facilities by repairing or replacing components
  with new components that serve the same purpose." New construction is not that.

- [V] The stake. Practicing the profession of an electrician without an active license is a **class 2 misdemeanor**
  (C.R.S. 12-115-123 → 12-20-407(1)(a)(V)(B)); unlicensed plumbing likewise (12-20-407(1)(a)(IV)).

### B. The state electrical permit

- [V] Who issues it. C.R.S. 12-115-120(1)(a)(I): "An individual required to have electrical inspection under this
  article 115 shall apply to the board for an electrical permit … **except where an incorporated town or city,
  county, city and county, or qualified state institution of higher education has a building department that meets
  the minimum standards of this article 115 and that processes applications for building permits and inspections,
  in which case the individual shall apply to the building department**."
- [V] 12-115-120(2)(a): new construction "shall have the electrical portion of the installation … inspected by a
  state electrical inspector" except in a jurisdiction "having its own electrical code and inspection program equal
  to the minimum standards" of the article.
- [V] 12-115-120(2)(b): "**Prior to the commencement of any electrical installation**, the person making the
  installation, who must be a qualified applicant, shall apply for a permit and pay the required permit fee." State
  inspector must inspect "within three working days after the receipt of the application for inspection."
- [V] THE ENFORCEMENT MECHANISM — the meter. 12-115-120(1)(b),(c): on final approval the board notifies the
  utility, and "**A utility shall not provide service to any person required to have electrical inspection under
  this article 115 without proof of final approval**" — exceptions only for declared emergencies (max seven days)
  and approved tiny-home connections (24-32-3329).
- [V] 12-115-120(6)(a): board permits valid twelve months; up to three years if the applicant demonstrates AT
  APPLICATION that the work is substantial; six-month extension for extenuating circumstances notified before
  expiry. (6)(b): after expiry you must buy a new permit before any inspection.
- [V] 12-115-120(8): a local government may commence or cease its own electrical inspection program "**only as of
  July 1 of any year**," with written notice to the board "on or before October 1 of the preceding calendar year."
  This is why the jurisdiction list moves, and when.
- [V] 12-115-121(2)(a): the maximum fee a city/town/county may charge for electrical inspections "shall not be more
  than one hundred twenty dollars, as adjusted annually, starting January 1, 2021, based on the annual percentage
  change in the … consumer price index for Denver-Aurora-Lakewood," plus an optional tiered charge based on size or
  valuation with a multiplier of eight percent. PRINT THE MECHANISM, NOT A 2026 DOLLAR FIGURE.
- [V] 12-115-122(1)(q): applying for a permit when not a qualified applicant is itself a violation.

### C. The state plumbing permit — and it covers GAS PIPING

- [V] C.R.S. 12-155-120(1)(a): "Any plumbing **or gas piping** installation in any new construction or remodeling or
  repair … and except for new construction or remodeling or repair in any incorporated town or city, county, or
  city and county … **where the local entity … conducts inspections and issues plumbing permits** … must be
  inspected by a state plumbing inspector."
- [V] NOTE THE DIFFERENT CARVE-OUT TEST. Electrical defers to a local building department that "meets the minimum
  standards" and processes building permits; plumbing defers only where the local entity actually "conducts
  inspections and issues plumbing permits." A jurisdiction can run one program and not the other — and ten counties
  do (see CO.4).
- [V] 12-155-120(1)(c)(I): permit and fee before commencement. (1)(b): inspection within three working days.
  (3)(a): twelve-month permits, same extension routes. (11)(c): homeowner is a qualified applicant.
- [V] "Gas piping" defined at 12-155-103(2): "any arrangement of piping used to convey fuel gas, supplied by one
  meter, and each arrangement of gas piping serving a building, structure, or premises." Excludes connecting
  appliances where service connections already exist, and utility-owned mains. For a propane mountain house, the
  fuel-gas runs sit inside the state plumbing permit — genuinely surprising.
- [V] 12-155-106(5): the board adopts a separate **Colorado Fuel Gas Code**.

### D. The codes the state sets — floor, not ceiling

- [V] ELECTRICAL: no edition in statute. C.R.S. 12-115-107(2)(a)(I) has the board adopt rules "governed when
  appropriate by the standards in the most current edition of the national electrical code," and preserves local
  authority to enforce standards "**more stringent** than the minimum standards adopted by the board" (with a duty
  to furnish a copy to the board). The edition lives in rule.
- [V] **3 CCR 710-1, Rule 1.5(A)** (State Electrical Board Rules and Regulations; current version adopted
  05/27/2026, published 06/25/2026, effective 07/15/2026): "The Board hereby incorporates by reference the National
  Fire Protection Association standard number 70, hereafter known as the National Electrical Code, **2026
  Edition** … This Rule does not include later amendments to or editions of the National Electrical Code, 2026
  Edition. **The effective date shall be August 1, 2026.**"
- [V] PLUMBING: **3 CCR 720-1** — NOT 4 CCR 730-1. (This was an early wrong assumption in this project; the CCR
  page header reads "Division of Professions and Occupations - State Plumbing Board > 3 CCR 720-1 PLUMBING RULES
  AND REGULATIONS." Current version effective 02/14/2026.) The rule adopts, "with certain additions, revisions, and
  deletions," named chapters of the **IPC 2021 edition** (1st printing March 2020) and the **IRC 2021 edition**
  (1st printing January 2021) as the **Colorado Plumbing Code**; and named chapters of the **IFGC 2021 edition**
  and the IRC as the **Colorado Fuel Gas Code**. Neither includes later editions.
- [V] C.R.S. 12-155-106(1): the Colorado plumbing code "must represent the minimum standards … **throughout the
  state**"; (2): "Local governments are permitted to amend the code for their jurisdictions as long as the
  amendments are at least equal to the minimum requirements."
- [V] THE FIVE-YEAR GAP is a printable, checkable fact: statewide electrical minimum = 2026 NEC (eff. Aug 1, 2026);
  statewide plumbing and fuel-gas minimums = 2021-based model codes. Same house, two boards, five years apart.
- [V] ENERGY-CODE TIE-BREAKER (both added/amended by HB 22-1362, 2022): C.R.S. 12-115-107(2)(a)(II) and
  12-155-106(4.5) make the NEC/board standards and the Colorado plumbing code **prevail** over the 2021 IECC, the
  2024 IECC, the model electric ready and solar ready code (24-38.5-401(5)), and any local or state energy code in
  the event of conflict.
- [H] EV-READY: C.R.S. 12-115-107(3) requires an electrical-permit applicant to meet EV power transfer
  infrastructure requirements — but 12-115-107(3)(c)(I) limits it to "**multifamily buildings**." Do NOT print this
  as reaching a single-family owner-builder. 3 CCR 710-1 Rule 1.5(C) implements it, incorporating the Model
  Electric Ready and Solar Ready Code "as they existed on June 1, 2023."
- [V] FACTORY-BUILT: C.R.S. 12-155-106(6), added by SB 25-002 (eff. May 8, 2025) — once the state housing board
  adopts rules under 24-32-3304(1)(h)(III), the plumbing board has no jurisdiction over activity to construct or
  install a factory-built structure; external utility connections still need a licensed plumber under a registered
  plumbing contractor, and service-side gas piping a qualified gas piping installer. [H] Confirm whether the
  housing board rules are in effect before relying on this.

### E. The local layer — two VERIFIED owner-builder regimes

- [V] DENVER (Community Planning & Development, "Applying for Permits as a Homeowner", denvergov.org, read Aug
  2026): single-family only — a homeowner "cannot obtain a permit for work in accessory dwelling units (ADUs),
  townhomes, condos, or duplexes, **even for the unit where you live**"; "You must be the legal owner and
  resident"; permits "cannot be issued to trusts, LLCs, or corporations"; an **exam** is required before doing
  electrical, plumbing, or mechanical/HVAC work (a state license may substitute for electrical or plumbing; no exam
  for general construction or roofing/siding); "**You must occupy the home for at least one year after work is
  complete**"; "Homeowners can only receive a permit to construct a new single-family home **once every five
  years**" (renovations and additions excepted).
- [V] PIKES PEAK REGIONAL BUILDING DEPARTMENT (pprbd.org, Homeowner Permits, read Aug 2026): "As a homeowner you
  may obtain a permit only if you are performing the work on your primary residence, which you own and reside in.
  You cannot perform work on a rental property you own nor a home you do not reside in. … **It is illegal for a
  homeowner to obtain a permit for a contractor hired to do the work for you.**" Unpermitted work → "a Certificate
  of Alleged Non-compliance will be issued against the property and filed with the El Paso or Teller County Clerk
  and Recorder **placing a lien against the property**."
- [V] PPRBD SCOPE (a major Colorado peculiarity — the permit authority is neither the city nor the county):
  unincorporated El Paso County; the cities of Colorado Springs, Fountain, and Manitou Springs; the towns of Green
  Mountain Falls, Monument, and Palmer Lake; and in Teller County, the City of Woodland Park only.
  [H] CALHAN IS DISPUTED ON PPRBD'S OWN SITE: the site-wide header lists Calhan among the areas served; the
  Homeowner Permits page says "We do not service the townships of Ramah or Calhan, nor any other areas of Teller
  County." Print the agreed core and tell boundary-adjacent readers to get it in writing.
- [H] Neither regime is in any statute. Both are local policy and both can change without notice.

## CO.2 — Application: the land approvals gate everything

### A. WATER — the permit that decides whether the parcel is buildable

- [V] C.R.S. 37-92-602(1)(b) — exempt well: "Wells not exceeding fifteen gallons per minute of production and used
  for ordinary household purposes, fire protection, the watering of poultry, domestic animals, and livestock on
  farms and ranches and for the irrigation of not over one acre of home gardens and lawns but not used for more
  than three single-family dwellings."
- [V] **THE 35-ACRE LINE** — C.R.S. 37-92-602(3)(b)(II)(A). The state engineer presumes no material injury (which
  is what lets the permit issue) where the well is "the only well on a residential site, which well will be used
  **solely for ordinary household purposes inside a single-family dwelling and will not be used for irrigation**,"
  OR "the only well on a tract of land of **thirty-five acres or more**" used for the (1)(b) purposes, OR the only
  well on a qualifying cluster-development lot (ratio not exceeding one acre-foot of annual withdrawals per
  thirty-five acres). PLAIN READING: under 35 acres, household use inside the house only — no lawn, no garden
  irrigation, no livestock. This is the most consequential Colorado land-buying fact in the kit.
- [V] 37-92-602(1)(a): designated groundwater basins are carved out of the article entirely and run under Ground
  Water Commission rules (Designated Basins rules are 2 CCR 410-1; Denver Basin 2 CCR 402-6; small-capacity permits
  in designated basins 2 CCR 402-4; statewide nontributary 2 CCR 402-7 — all listed on dwr.colorado.gov).
- [V] 37-92-602(3)(b)(III): in a subdivision approved on or after June 1, 1972 whose water-supply plan the state
  engineer never recommended for approval, "the cumulative effect of all such wells in the subdivision shall be
  considered in determining material injury." Neighbors' wells count against yours.
- [V] 37-92-602(3)(a)(II): statutory application fees — one hundred dollars for a (3)(b) application, sixty dollars
  for a (3)(c) application. [H] Confirm against DWR's current fee schedule before sending payment.
- [V] TIMING — dwr.colorado.gov/services/well-permitting: "DWR reviews new and replacement well permit applications
  in the order they are received, and **review of complete applications may take up to 49 days**." Applications
  accepted by email; fees paid online; Applications eForms Dashboard. Named DWR documents: Beginner's Guide to Well
  Permits; Form Submittal, Payment Options and Fee Schedule; Emergency Well Permit Procedures; Fact Sheet for Home
  Buyers and Real Estate Professionals; Well Permit Search; Well Permit Map Viewer.
- [H] Whether a given parcel can get a well permit at all is parcel-specific. Never print an answer; print the
  verification step and the 49-day clock.

### B. WASTEWATER — statewide floor, mandatory local rules

- [V] C.R.S. 25-10-104(1): the division develops and the commission adopts rules "setting forth minimum standards
  … for on-site wastewater treatment systems within Colorado."
- [V] 25-10-104(2): "**Every local board of health in the state shall develop and adopt detailed rules** for
  on-site wastewater treatment systems within its area of jurisdiction." Rules must comply with the commission's
  and with 25-10-105 and 25-10-106; effective 45 days after final adoption unless the department objects.
- [V] 25-10-104(4): local rules adopted after commission action must be "no less stringent."
- [V] 25-10-106(1)(a)–(h) sets what every local process must contain: a written application with the data the board
  requires; "review of the application and inspection of the proposed site"; the studies and reports the agency may
  require (where soil profile and percolation work lives); a determination by "an environmental health specialist
  or a professional engineer"; issuance by the health officer; and "**final inspection of a system … after
  construction … has been completed, but before the system is placed in use**."
- [V] 25-10-109(1): "The local board of health **may** adopt rules that provide for the licensing of systems
  contractors." PERMISSIVE — so "can I install my own septic?" is a local question the statute does not answer.
- [V] 25-10-110: "The primary responsibility for the enforcement of this article … lies with local public health
  agencies and local boards of health."
- [H] Reg. 43 (5 CCR 1002-43) is the commission regulation commonly cited for OWTS minimums. NOT independently
  verified in this pass — the statutory chain above was verified instead, and the kit cites the statute. Verify the
  CCR series number before printing it.

### C. ENERGY CODE — the trigger everyone reports wrong

- [V] C.R.S. 30-28-211(3): every board of county commissioners that "has adopted and enforced one or more building
  codes, or that adopts and enforces one or more building codes after July 1, 2022, shall adopt and enforce an
  energy code."
- [V] 30-28-211(3.5)(a): a county with building codes that "**updates one or more building codes** on or after July
  1, 2023, and before July 1, 2026" must adopt an energy code achieving "equivalent or better energy performance
  than the 2021 international energy conservation code and the model electric ready and solar ready code language
  developed for adoption by the energy code board pursuant to section 24-38.5-401(5) **at the same time other
  building codes are updated**."
- [V] 30-28-211(3.5)(b): updates "on or after July 1, 2026" must reach "the model low energy and carbon code
  developed for adoption by the energy code board pursuant to section 24-38.5-401(6)."
- [V] 30-28-211(3.5)(c): RURAL-COUNTY CARVE-OUT — a county with population under thirty thousand (state
  demographer) that applied for and was NOT awarded a grant under 24-38.5-403 while the program was accepting
  applications may instead adopt "one of the last three most recent editions of the international energy
  conservation code."
- [V] 30-28-211(3.5)(g): adopting the wildfire resiliency code alone does NOT trigger the energy-code duty.
- [V] 30-28-211(5): exemptions include buildings otherwise exempt from the local code, buildings with no
  conditioned space, buildings not using electricity or fossil fuels for comfort heating, and listed historic
  buildings.
- [V] MUNICIPAL TWIN: C.R.S. 31-15-602(3), (3.5)(a)–(b) mirror the county provisions — but there is **no rural
  carve-out** for municipalities.
- [V] 30-28-201(3)–(4): counties must adopt an energy code meeting one of the three most recent IECC versions when
  adopting or updating any building code, and report their current building and energy codes to the Colorado Energy
  Office. (This reporting duty is why the Colorado Energy Office holds a statewide code inventory.)
- KEY CORRECTION FOR THE STATE GUIDE: the post-July-2026 standard is the **model low energy and carbon code**, not
  "the 2024 IECC"; and it is **not a deadline** — it triggers only on a code update.

### D. WILDFIRE — a new statewide layer (added after the first draft)

- [V] The **2025 Colorado Wildfire Resiliency Code**, published by the Division of Fire Prevention and Control
  (dfpc.colorado.gov/colorado-wildfire-resiliency-code). Scope, verbatim: "The provisions of this code shall apply
  to the construction, alteration, repair, maintenance and use of any building, structure or premises that contain
  occupiable and/or habitable space, or change in use resulting in an occupiable and/or habitable space, unless
  excepted, **within the wildland-urban interface areas of Colorado, as designated in this code**."
- [V] Structure: Ch. 1 scope/administration (incl. §103 Code Compliance Agency, §107 stop work order); Ch. 3
  Wildfire Hazard Identification (§302 WUI area, §303 mapping and applicability, §304 ground-truthing); Ch. 4
  Structure Hardening (§403 Class 1, §404 Class 2); Ch. 5 Site and Area Requirements; Appendix B construction
  documents; Appendix C inspection and enforcement.
- [V] §101.2.2: structure hardening for factory-built structures follows Division of Housing rules at 8 CCR 1302-1
  Rule 2; HUD-standard manufactured homes are exempt on first installation but subject if moved into an applicable
  area.
- [H] Which jurisdictions have adopted/are enforcing it, and who does plan review and inspection, is
  jurisdiction-specific. Source CWRC claims from DFPC only.

### E. Other application items

- [V] Plans: you may prepare your own (12-120-403(1)(a)); 12-120-403(2) expressly preserves local building-code
  power. Colorado departments routinely require an engineered soils report and a PE-stamped foundation because of
  expansive soils and snow load — a code requirement on the structure, not a licensing requirement on the
  draftsman.
- [V] What the state permit system asks for (dpo.colorado.gov/ElectricalPlumbingPermits): job-site address;
  "Directions to the job site, including the nearest cross streets"; a valid credit card. Electrical: square
  footage of living space to be wired, name of the power supplier, cost of materials and labor. Plumbing
  (residential): a fixture count — "traps, water hammer, arrestors, backflow preventers, water heaters, fuel gas
  outlets, and fuel gas pressure regulators." Permits bought online are "processed immediately upon payment"; hard
  copy "may be delayed up to 7 business days."
- [V] "**A separate permit is required for each detached structure on the same property.**" Garage, studio, barn,
  Building A/B/C — each its own permit, clearly named or numbered.
- [V] The online system cannot process permit transfers, extensions, or informational changes — those go by email
  to the permits office.
- [V] NOTICE TO HOMEOWNER (same page): "If you are a homeowner hiring an Electrical Contractor or Plumbing
  Contractor, you[r] contractor is responsible for obtaining the required permit(s). As the homeowner, you should
  not continue with the permit application process. Be aware that it is a violation of state law for an individual
  (other than the homeowner) that is not a registered Electrical or Plumbing Contractor to install an electrical or
  plumbing project on your property."

## CO.3 — Inspections

- [V] Four authorities can inspect one house under three statutes and none coordinates: the local building
  inspector (12-115-120(2)(a) context / 30-28-205(1)), the electrical inspector (state or local), the plumbing/gas
  inspector (decided separately), and local public health for OWTS (25-10-106(1)(h)).
- [V] SCHEDULING (dpo.colorado.gov/ElectricalPlumbingPermits): "**Do not contact the Division offices regarding
  inspection requests or scheduling. You must contact your inspector for scheduling information.**" The Division
  publishes an Electrical Inspection Map and Electrical Inspector Contact List, and the same pair for plumbing.
- [V] Three working days from receipt of the inspection application — 12-115-120(2)(b); 12-155-120(1)(b).
- [V] Pass → "certificate of approval" (12-115-120(3)(a); 12-155-120(2)(a)). These certificates are also the
  evidence that the electrical work WAS inspected — the condition the homeowner exemption depends on.
- [V] Fail → written notice of disapproval with reasons; posted at the installation site and filed with the board
  (12-115-120(3)(b), (4)); service may be ordered discontinued if hazardous; reinspection application and fee after
  correction; appeal to the board heard "within seven days after notice of appeal is filed"
  (12-115-120(3)(b)(I); 12-155-120(2)(b)(I)). 12-115-120(9) adds a route to ask the program director to review an
  inspector's conduct or failure to inspect.
- [V] PRESENCE POLICY (board policy, DPO page): "the Contractor or Contractor Representative, adult homeowner
  (above the age of 18) or homeowner's representative must be present for the inspection of **occupied dwelling
  units**." A house under construction is not an occupied dwelling — print it precisely.
- [V] A **Remote Video Inspection Waiver (Occupied Residential)** exists on the Division's forms page.
- [V] 12-115-120(10): inspections by any entity must include a contemporaneous review for compliance with
  12-115-109 and 12-115-115; each entity must publish its contemporaneous-review procedures.
- [V] THE FINISH LINE is the meter, not the final — 12-115-120(1)(b), (c).
- [V] OWTS final inspection before the system is placed in use — 25-10-106(1)(h).
- [H] The local inspection ladder is a TYPICAL composite. No Colorado statute enumerates residential inspections;
  each jurisdiction's adopted code and permit card govern. Label it typical every time.
- [V] Energy certificate: under the 2021 IECC as locally adopted (R401.3), the builder completes a permanent
  certificate of insulation R-values, window U-factors, and equipment efficiencies and posts it near the furnace,
  in a utility room, or another approved interior location; where placed on an electrical panel it must not
  obstruct the circuit directory. As owner-builder, you are the builder.

## THINGS DELIBERATELY NOT PRINTED

- Any roster of "counties with no building code." It rots, and the two most-repeated names in owner-builder lore —
  Costilla and Huerfano — both turn out to HAVE adopted codes. The kit gives the verification step instead.
- Any local permit fee, or any adopted code edition for a named jurisdiction. Both change; the July 1, 2026 wave of
  Front Range code updates would have invalidated a printed table within weeks.
- Any phone number, anywhere in the kit. Websites plus write-in rules only.
- "Jackson County enforces the 2017 NEC." Reported in local research, but a county enforcing 2017 would sit BELOW
  the state floor, which C.R.S. 12-115-107(2)(a)(I) forbids (locals may be more stringent, never weaker). Almost
  certainly a stale county document, not a live rule.
- The claim that Douglas County bundles electrical/plumbing/mechanical into the building permit. Structurally
  interesting and plausible, but not verifiable on the page fetched. Left out under the no-summaries rule.
- 2021 IECC prescriptive R-value tables. Not read directly in this pass; the live state guide prints one with no
  edition label, which is flagged as needs-verification rather than asserted wrong.
- Reg. 43's CCR series number (5 CCR 1002-43). Statutory chain verified instead; verify the series number before
  printing it.
