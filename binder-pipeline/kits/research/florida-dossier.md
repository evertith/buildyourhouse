# Florida Owner-Builder Permit Kit — Claims Dossier

Kit 14 of the 50-state program. Built September 2026. Six documents, 45 pages.

**Tag key.** `[V]` = read against a primary source, with the cite and URL given.
`[H]` = hearsay, inference, or a secondary rendering — **not printed as a flat
statement** anywhere in the kit without an explicit hedge. Anything that could
not reach `[V]` and could not be safely hedged is in DELIBERATELY NOT PRINTED.

**Statute year.** Florida publishes each year's compilation at a near-identical
URL. Everything here was read against the **2026** compilation (some sections
cross-checked against 2024/2025 — noted where the text is identical). The kit
cites "Fla. Stat. (2026)".

**One structural warning for the next editor.** Two of Florida's biggest
factual answers — the septic agency and the NEC edition — are *mid-transition*
and split. Neither has a single correct one-line answer. Both are handled in
the kit by printing the split rather than picking a side. Do not "simplify"
them later; the split is the fact.

## 1. THE OWNER-BUILDER EXEMPTION — the kit's spine

### 1.1 There are TWO exemptions, not one — [V]

The organizing insight of FL.1, and the thing essentially every Florida guide
gets wrong.

Chapter 489 is split into parts. **Part I** licenses the thirteen contractor
categories at § 489.105(3): general, building, residential, sheet metal,
roofing, Class A/B/C air-conditioning, mechanical, commercial pool/spa,
residential pool/spa, swimming pool servicing, plumbing, underground utility,
solar, pollutant storage, specialty. **Electrical is not among them** — it is
licensed under **Part II**, § 489.505.

Consequence: the Part I owner-builder exemption at § 489.103(7) cannot reach
electrical work, so Part II carries its own owner exemption at § 489.503(6).

- § 489.103: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0400-0499/0489/Sections/0489.103.html
- § 489.503: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.503
- § 489.105: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.105

### 1.2 The two exemptions do not match — [V], and this is the money fact

| | Part I (§ 489.103(7)) | Part II (§ 489.503(6)) |
|---|---|---|
| Structures | farm outbuilding, one- or two-family residence | farm outbuilding, single-family or duplex |
| Commercial cap | $75,000 | $75,000 |
| Whose use | "for the occupancy **or** use of such owners" | disclosure says "for your own use **and** occupancy" |
| Sale test trigger | "**any** such structure" within 1 year of completion | "**more than one** such structure" within 1 year |
| Evidentiary weight | "creates a **presumption**" | "is **prima facie evidence**" |
| Disclosure statement | 12 numbered paragraphs | a separate, shorter one |

Verbatim, Part I § 489.103(7)(a)1.: *"In an action brought under this part,
proof of the sale or lease, or offering for sale or lease, of any such
structure by the owner-builder within 1 year after completion of same creates a
presumption that the construction was undertaken for purposes of sale or
lease."*

Verbatim, Part II § 489.503(6)(a): *"…proof of the sale or lease, or offering
for sale or lease, of more than one such structure by the owner-builder within
1 year after completion of same is prima facie evidence that the construction
was undertaken for purposes of sale or lease."*

**Printed in FL.1 as a side-by-side table.** This comparison did not exist in
any secondary source found.

### 1.3 NO square-footage cap, NO dollar cap, NO frequency limit — [V] (as absences)

The $75,000 figure in both statutes attaches to **commercial** buildings only.
Neither part contains a frequency limit ("one exemption per N years") — that
hypothesis is **refuted**. The 1-year sale test is what polices serial use.

### 1.4 The four situations Part I covers — [V]

1. § 489.103(7)(a)1. — farm outbuilding, one- or two-family residence. The
   ordinary case.
2. § 489.103(7)(a)2. — repair/replace wood shakes, asphalt or fiberglass
   shingles on a one-, two-, or **three**-family residence, **only** where the
   property was damaged in a Governor-declared state of emergency.
3. § 489.103(7)(a)3. — solar panels, **only** where the local government
   participates in a "SunShot Initiative: Rooftop Solar Challenge" grant. Owner
   must still use a licensed electrical contractor for wiring/interconnection.
   **Hedged in the kit** — see DELIBERATELY NOT PRINTED #3.
4. § 489.103(7)(a)4. — finishing a permit where the listed contractor
   "substantially completed" the project, on local agency approval. **The owner
   is expressly NOT required to occupy for 1 year.** Mirrored for electrical at
   § 489.503(6)(b). Rarely covered anywhere; printed in FL.1.

### 1.5 The conditions — [V]

- **Direct, onsite supervision by the owner personally.** § 489.103(7)(b):
  *"The owner may not delegate the owner's responsibility to directly supervise
  all work to any other person unless that person is registered or certified
  under this part and the work being performed is within the scope of that
  person's license."*
- **Personal appearance.** § 489.103(7)(c): the owner *"must personally appear
  and sign the building permit application."* One narrow carve-out: the solar
  pathway at (7)(a)3. with an electronic confirmation system.
- **Identity verification at issuance.** *"A copy of the property owner's
  driver license, the notarized signature of the property owner, or other type
  of verification acceptable to the local permitting agency is required when
  the permit is issued."* Three options, agency's choice — the kit tells the
  reader to ask which, rather than asserting "notarized."
- **Enforcement.** § 489.103(7)(c): on violation the agency *"shall withhold
  final approval, revoke the permit, or pursue any action or remedy for
  unlicensed activity."* Withholding final approval = no CO on a finished house.

### 1.6 The 12-paragraph disclosure statement — [V], captured verbatim

Full text extracted and saved to the scratchpad at `flstat/489.103-disclosure.txt`.
Summarized paragraph-by-paragraph in FL.1 with the load-bearing clauses quoted.
The two that matter most:

- **¶6**: *"I may not hire an unlicensed person to act as my contractor or to
  supervise persons working on my building or residence. It is my
  responsibility to ensure that the persons whom I employ have the licenses
  required by law and by county or municipal ordinance."*
- **¶8**: *"Any person working on my building who is not licensed must work
  under my direct supervision and must be employed by me, which means that I
  must comply with laws requiring the withholding of federal income tax and
  social security contributions under the Federal Insurance Contributions Act
  (FICA) and must provide workers' compensation for the employee."*

Part II's separate disclosure saved at `flstat/489.503-disclosure.txt`.

### 1.7 Can an owner do their own plumbing, roofing, HVAC? — [V] on the text

**Yes on the statute's face, and the argument is structural.** § 489.103 opens
*"This part does not apply to:"* — *this part*, not *this section*. Plumbing,
mechanical, air-conditioning, roofing, sheet metal and pool contracting are all
licensed **inside Part I** at § 489.105(3)(d)–(q). When Part I stops applying
to the exempt owner, it stops applying for those trades too. That is precisely
why electrical — which sits outside Part I — needed its own exemption.

Reinforcing: § 489.105(3) defines a contractor as one who does the work *"for
compensation… for others or for resale to others"* — an owner working on their
own home for no compensation is outside the definition before any exemption is
reached.

**Hedge printed in the kit:** building departments vary in how readily they
accept owner-performed trade work in practice; some ask for a competence
demonstration. FL.1 says so and tells the reader to ask first.

**Anyone the owner PAYS** to perform a licensed trade must hold that license —
§ 489.113(2) bars an unlicensed sub from performing work in the enumerated
trade categories even under a licensee's supervision.

### 1.8 Certified vs. registered — [V]

§ 489.105(8): a **certified** contractor holds a state certificate of
competency and *"shall be allowed to contract in any jurisdiction in the state."*
§ 489.105(10): a **registered** contractor met a specific jurisdiction's local
competency requirements and *"may contract only in such jurisdictions."*

Practical point printed in FL.1: seeing "Registered" on the DBPR lookup does
not mean the license is good on your parcel.

### 1.9 Penalties and the owner's protections — [V]

- § 489.127(1)(f) — contracting without being registered or certified.
- § 489.127(1)(h) — *"Commence or perform work for which a building permit is
  required… without such building permit being in effect."* Applies to **any
  person**, including an owner-builder. Printed in FL.1 as a callout: clearing
  the lot while the application is in review is the version people actually do.
- § 489.127(2)(a)–(c) — first-degree misdemeanor; third-degree felony on a
  repeat offense **or an offense during a Governor-declared state of
  emergency**.
- § 489.127(3) — departmental stop-work order.
- § 489.127(4)(a)–(c) — a licensee may not let their license number be used by
  an unlicensed person, and may not pull a permit for work they are not
  performing. The "I'll pull it for you" trap, prohibited on the contractor's side.
- § 489.128(1),(2) — an unlicensed contractor's contract is *"unenforceable in
  law or in equity by the unlicensed contractor,"* and *"no lien or bond claim
  shall exist in favor of the unlicensed contractor."* Real owner protection.

## 2. WORKERS' COMPENSATION — the one genuine conflict, printed as a conflict

### 2.1 The tension — [V] on both sides

The § 489.103(7) disclosure ¶8 (above) tells the owner they *"must provide
workers' compensation for the employee."*

Chapter 440 says something that does not sit comfortably beside it:

- § 440.02(19)(b): *"A homeowner shall not be considered the employer of
  persons hired by the homeowner to carry out construction on the homeowner's
  own premises if those premises are not intended for immediate lease, sale, or
  resale."*
- § 440.02(10): *"'construction' does not mean a homeowner's act of
  construction or the result of a construction upon his or her own premises,
  provided such premises are not intended to be sold, resold, or leased by the
  owner within 1 year after the commencement of construction."*

Both verified independently by direct fetch of
https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/440.02.

**The kit prints both and refuses to resolve it**, telling the reader the gap
is real, the exposure runs one way (an injured worker), and this is worth one
conversation with a Florida insurance agent or attorney. This is the single
place in the kit where a confident answer would have been the wrong call.

### 2.2 Two different one-year clocks — [V], and a genuinely novel find

- § 489.103(7)(a)1. runs **from completion**.
- § 440.02(10) runs **from commencement of construction**.

On a fourteen-month build these expire months apart. Printed in FL.1 as its own
callout. Found in no secondary source.

### 2.3 § 440.103 — [V]

*"Every employer shall, as a condition to applying for and receiving a building
permit, show proof and certify to the permit issuer that it has secured
compensation for its employees."* Acceptable proof is exactly three things: a
carrier's certificate of coverage, a valid exemption certificate approved by
the department, or authority to self-insure. Given § 2.1's carve-out, whether
an exempt homeowner is an "employer" here is unresolved — the kit tells the
reader to expect the question on the local form and answer it accurately.

## 3. THE BUILDING CODE

### 3.1 Edition — [V]

**Florida Building Code, 8th Edition (2023), effective 31 December 2023.**
Source: the Florida Building Commission's own BCIS portal,
https://www.floridabuilding.org/c/default.aspx

- FBC **Residential** based on the **2021 IRC** — DBPR's published Analysis of
  Changes for the 8th Edition FBCR.
- FBC **Energy Conservation** based on the **2021 IECC** — same series.
- Wind now on **ASCE 7-22** (up from ASCE 7-16). *"Wind speeds are unchanged for
  most of Florida except for the panhandle area where wind speeds have increased
  slightly in some areas."*
- FBC **Building**'s base I-Code year: **[H]**, presumed 2021 IBC, not
  independently confirmed. **Not printed.**

### 3.2 TRIPWIRE — the 9th Edition (2026) — [V] that it is a draft

The Commission's own code menu labels it *"Draft 9th Edition (2026) FBC."* A
second 45-day comment period on glitch changes ran to **8 October 2026**. **No
effective date has been published.** § 553.73(7)(e) requires any adopting rule
to take effect no sooner than 6 months after publication of the updated code.

**The kit prints no effective date and says any guide giving one is guessing.**
The state guide currently claims "enforcement expected to begin December 31,
2026" — that is Guide Error #2 below.

### 3.3 NEC edition — SPLIT, and printed as a split — [V]/[H]

- As **originally adopted** (31 Dec 2023), FBC Building Chapter 27 referenced
  **NFPA 70-2020**.
- The Commission has **since revised the referenced standard to NFPA 70-2023**,
  per its own integrated Referenced Standards document for the Building volume
  (tracked-changes, reflecting supplements through the December 2025 meeting):
  *"70—[2020 struck] 23 National Electrical Code (NEC) as published September 1,
  2022."*
- The mechanism is lawful and specific: § 553.73(8)(a)6. lets the Commission
  adopt an updated NEC **outside** the triennial cycle.

**[H] on the precise current enforcement posture** — the evidence is a
Commission working document rather than a clean code citation. The kit
therefore prints **both layers**, explains why the reader will find both
answers in print, and tells them to ask their electrical plan reviewer which
edition their permit is reviewed against and write it down. This is more useful
than either bare answer.

### 3.4 Local amendment authority — [V], and distinctively Florida

- § 553.73(4)(a) — administrative amendments must be **more stringent**,
  transmitted to the Commission within 30 days.
- § 553.73(4)(b) — technical amendments: not more than once every 6 months,
  more stringent only, after a hearing advertised 10 days ahead, on evidence of
  a specific local need, no more stringent than necessary, and **may not
  introduce a new subject not addressed in the code**.
- § 553.73(4)(e) — **the good bit**: a local amendment *"is effective only
  until the adoption of the new edition of the Florida Building Code by the
  commission every third year,"* at which point the Commission adopts it
  statewide or rescinds it. **Local amendments expire on the code cycle.**
- § 553.73(7)(f) — wind resistance and water intrusion provisions *"may not be
  modified to diminish those construction requirements."*
- § 553.73(5) — flood amendments follow a separate, easier track and do not
  necessarily sunset.

### 3.5 Wind and opening protection — [V] with one deliberate gap

- Authoritative lookup is the **ASCE Wind Design Geodatabase / ASCE 7 Hazard
  Tool** (ascehazardtool.org, verified 200), not a static table. **The kit
  prints no county wind-speed table on purpose.**
- **Windborne debris region**: two-limb structure verified (proximity-to-water
  with Vult ≥ 130 mph, or Vult ≥ 140 mph anywhere). The 8th Edition **amended
  limb 1** — DBPR's Analysis of Changes: *"Item 1 of the definition has been
  revised to delete the term 'coastal' and clarify that an Exposure D condition
  must exist upwind of the water line. The net effect is that some inland areas
  where the wind speed is equal to or greater than 130 mph and located near
  large bodies of water with a fetch of 5000 ft or more will now be in a
  Windborne Debris Region."*
  **The exact current wording could not be pulled from a primary source** (the
  freely readable renderings still carry the superseded 7th-Edition text). The
  kit prints the *consequence* — an inland lakefront lot can be in the region —
  and tells the reader to get their parcel's status confirmed in writing. See
  DELIBERATELY NOT PRINTED #1.
- **Opening protection** (FBC Residential R301.2.1.2): Large Missile Test of
  ASTM E1996/E1886, or TAS 201/202/203, or AAMA 506; garage door glazing under
  ANSI/DASMA 115 or an approved standard. **Plywood exception is real**: 7/16"
  wood structural panels, precut, predrilled, permanently installed
  corrosion-resistant hardware, mean roof height ≤ 33 ft, Vult ≤ 180 mph.
  Storage sheds not for human habitation ≤ 720 sq ft excepted.
  **This refutes the state guide's "Miami-Dade: Impact glass mandatory."**

### 3.6 HVHZ — [V]

Broward and Miami-Dade counties, both entire. Its own **Test Protocols** volume
of the FBC, and a dedicated **HVHZ Uniform Permit Application** at FBC Building
§ 1525 (roofing at minimum). Miami-Dade's Product Control Division is a
Commission-approved evaluation entity by statute, § 553.842(8)(a) — which is
why the NOA and the statewide FL# systems overlap rather than compete.
**[H]** on exactly when an NOA alone suffices vs. a statewide FL# — kit tells
HVHZ readers to confirm with the local plan reviewer.

### 3.7 Product approval — [V], the kit's "surprise" item

§ 553.842(5), verbatim category list: *"panel walls, exterior doors, roofing,
skylights, windows, shutters, impact protective systems, and structural
components."*

- § 553.842(4): statewide approval *"shall preclude local jurisdictions from
  requiring further testing, evaluation, or submission of other evidence as a
  condition of using the product"* when used consistently with its approval.
- § 553.842(9): a building official denying an approved product must issue a
  **signed written report** stating the inconsistency.
- Product search verified live: https://www.floridabuilding.org/pr/pr_app_srch.aspx

**[H]** — a single statewide-standard "product approval schedule" form could
**not** be confirmed. Outside HVHZ the schedule format is set locally. The kit
says: expect to submit one, ask the department for their template.

### 3.8 Energy — [V]

- **Form R400-2023** — Residential Energy Conservation Code Documentation
  Checklist (all paths).
- **Form R402-2023** — prescriptive path; now carries SEER2/HSPF2 labeling.
- **R405 performance path** — output of Commission-approved software. **There
  is no static "Form R405 PDF"** — corrects a common assumption. 8th Edition
  tightened the path: max total e-Ratio 1.00 → **0.95**.
- **R406** — Energy Rating Index path.
- **Blower door mandatory**, max **7 ACH50** in Florida's climate zones, run
  after envelope penetrations are sealed, by an energy auditor/rater, Class A
  or B A/C or mechanical contractor, or a code-official-approved third party —
  **never owner self-certified**. **Below 3 ACH50 whole-house mechanical
  ventilation is required.** (FBC Energy Conservation R402.4.1.2.)
  **This refutes the state guide's "5 ACH or less."**
- Florida does **not** run on REScheck. The state guide lists it as a resource —
  Guide Error #8.
- **Duct leakage threshold NOT printed** — see DELIBERATELY NOT PRINTED #5.

### 3.9 Permit process — [V]

- **§ 553.792 review clock**, exact day counts: **30 business days** for a
  residential permit where the structure is **under 7,500 sq ft**; 60 business
  days at or above. Local government has **5 business days** to state
  deficiencies or the application is **automatically deemed complete**. Fee
  reduced **10% per business day** late; a deficiency notice gives the
  applicant 10 business days to revise, then 10 more for the government, with
  the penalty rising to **20% per business day**.
  **Note:** § 553.7921 is the Uniform Fire Alarm Permit Application statute and
  has nothing to do with house permit timing — do not cite it.
- **§ 553.791 private provider**: licensed building code administrator,
  engineer or architect. Notice in writing at permit application or by 2:00 p.m.
  two business days before the first inspection. *"The local jurisdiction must
  reduce the permit fee by the amount of cost savings… may not charge fees for
  building inspections."* Deemed-approved windows: **20 business days**
  generally, **10** if an engineer or architect sealed the affidavit, **5** for
  a single-trade review on a one- or two-family dwelling. CO in **2 business
  days** for a one- or two-family dwelling.
- **Permit life** (FBC Building administrative chapter, **[H]** rendering but
  corroborated as unchanged 7th→8th): application abandoned at **180 days**;
  permit invalid if work not commenced within **6 months** or suspended
  **6 months**; and the saver — *"Work shall be considered to be in active
  progress when the permit has received an approved inspection within 180
  days."* Kit flags these live in the administrative chapter, which a local
  government **may** amend.
- **§ 553.80(7)(a)**: permit fees *"may only be used for carrying out the local
  government's responsibilities in enforcing the Florida Building Code,"* and an
  owner or builder holding a permit **may bring a civil action** to enforce that.
- **Threshold buildings** (§ 553.71(12)) are over three stories or 50 feet — a
  house never qualifies, so the special-inspector regime does not apply.

### 3.10 Inspections — [V] statute / [H] rendering

FBC Building 110.3 is a **codified statewide minimum**, unlike most states in
this program. Foundation; framing; **sheathing including fasteners and dry-in**;
exterior wall coverings; roofing; final. Plus:
- **Flood hazard areas: elevation certification upon placement of the lowest
  floor before further vertical construction, AND a final certification at the
  final inspection.** Two documents, not one.
- **Impact-resistant coverings/systems**: the building official schedules
  inspections to confirm the system on the plans was installed per the
  manufacturer's instructions **and the product approval** — which is where the
  FL numbers get checked against reality.

### 3.11 Termite — [V]

FBC Residential **R318.1** (corrects a hypothesized "R318/R324"). A licensed
pest control company issues a **Certificate of Compliance** to the building
department. Soil treatment goes in **after** excavation, backfill and
compaction; disturbed soil must be retreated.

## 4. NOTICE OF COMMENCEMENT AND LIEN LAW

### 4.1 THREE thresholds, not one — [V]. Hypothesis was partly wrong.

- **> $2,500** — NOC recording required at all. Lives at **§ 713.02(5)**, not in
  § 713.13.
- **> $5,000** — a copy must be filed with the building department before the
  first inspection. **§ 713.135(1)(e)**. A *different, higher* threshold.
- **< $15,000** — HVAC repair/replacement carve-out from § 713.135(1) entirely.
  **The hypothesized $7,500 is wrong.**

### 4.2 The gate — [V]

§ 713.135(1)(e)1.: *"In the absence of the filing of a copy of the notice of
commencement, the issuing authority or a private provider performing inspection
services may not perform or approve subsequent inspections until the applicant
files… such copy with the issuing authority."*

But § 713.135(1)(f) and (1)(e)4.: recording may **not** be required as a
condition of applying for, processing, or issuing the permit. **The permit can
issue first; the NOC gates the inspections.** Both halves printed in FL.3.

### 4.3 Mechanics — [V]

- Recorded with the **clerk of the circuit court** before commencing work
  (§ 713.13(1)(a)); certified copy or notarized statement **posted at the site**.
- **Void if work is not commenced within 90 days of recording** (§ 713.13(2)).
- **Owner signs personally**: *"no one else may be permitted to sign in his or
  her stead"* (§ 713.13(1)(g)). Sworn; the statutory form carries a notary
  block and Florida permits online notarization.
- **Effective 1 year from recording** unless the notice states otherwise
  (§ 713.13(6)); amendable to extend (§ 713.13(5)(a)), but changing contractors
  requires a new notice.
- **Payments after expiration are improper payments** — the statutory form's
  WARNING TO OWNER: *"ANY PAYMENTS MADE BY THE OWNER AFTER THE EXPIRATION OF
  THE NOTICE OF COMMENCEMENT ARE CONSIDERED IMPROPER PAYMENTS… AND CAN RESULT
  IN YOUR PAYING TWICE."*
- § 713.13(1)(h): the permit issuer **must accept** a recorded NOC in the
  statutory form.

### 4.4 Lien deadlines — [V], all verbatim

- **45 days** — Notice to Owner from a lienor not in privity (§ 713.06(2)(a)).
  Failure to serve timely *"is a complete defense to enforcement of a lien."*
- **90 days** — claim of lien recorded *"not later than 90 days after the final
  furnishing"* (§ 713.08(5)).
- **1 year** — a lien does not continue longer than one year after recording
  unless suit is filed (§ 713.22(1)).
- **60 days** — the owner's counterpunch. A recorded **Notice of Contest of
  Lien** cuts the lienor's window to *"60 days from the date of service,"* after
  which the lien is *"extinguished automatically"* (§ 713.22(2)). Genuinely
  underused; printed prominently in FL.5.
- § 713.20 prescribes the **two** statutory waiver forms (progress payment,
  final payment); advance waiver of lien rights is **unenforceable**
  (§ 713.20(2)); and a person **may not require** a lienor to use a different
  form (§ 713.20(6)).
- § 713.06(3)(d) — Contractor's Final Payment Affidavit; the owner must retain
  final payment until furnished, or the property is exposed to the full amount
  of valid liens the owner had notice of.

**Why this belongs in a permit kit:** an owner-builder is their own contractor,
so the documents that normally flow *from* a GC *to* an owner have nobody to
flow from. FL.5 says so explicitly.

## 5. SEPTIC, WELLS, WATER

### 5.1 The agency answer is split, and both common answers are wrong — [V]

- The onsite sewage program transferred **from DOH to DEP effective 1 July
  2021** — **ch. 2020-150, Laws of Florida** (the Clean Waterways Act, SB 712).
  **This corrects the hypothesized "SB 1128 (2021)."**
- **But the transfer is phased and unfinished.** As of September 2026 DEP
  issues permits directly in **17 counties**: Bay, Calhoun, Escambia, Franklin,
  Gadsden, Gulf, Holmes, Jackson, Jefferson, Leon, Liberty, Marion, Okaloosa,
  Santa Rosa, Wakulla, Walton, Washington.
- **In the other 50 counties — every major metro — the applicant files with the
  Environmental Health program of the county health department.**
- DEP's own FAQ: *"The transition of the remaining 50 counties will occur
  depending on legislative approval."* No schedule.
- https://floridadep.gov/water/onsite-sewage/content/onsite-sewage-faq-permitting (verified 200)

**So "DOH regulates septic" is wrong, and "DEP regulates septic" is misleading
for 50 counties.** The kit prints the county list and tells the reader to
re-check before filing.

### 5.2 Rule chapter — [V]

**Chapter 62-6, F.A.C.**, formerly **64E-6**. Every rule in 62-6 carries a
"Formerly 64E-6.xxx" history note. County forms and older material still say
64E-6. Several core rules were amended **6-8-2026** and reviewed **8-3-26** —
this chapter moves fast; re-verify at each revision.

Statutory basis remains **§§ 381.0065–381.00655**, with § 381.0065(2)(d) now
defining "Department" as DEP.

### 5.3 THE SEQUENCING FACT — [V], and it reorders the whole build

§ 381.0065(4), Fla. Stat., verbatim: a municipality or political subdivision
*"may not issue a building or plumbing permit for any building that requires
the use of an onsite sewage treatment and disposal system unless the owner or
builder has received a construction permit for such system from the department.
A building or structure may not be occupied and a municipality, political
subdivision, or any state or federal agency may not authorize occupancy until
the department approves the final installation of the onsite sewage treatment
and disposal system."*

**Septic permit before building permit. Septic final approval before
occupancy.** Both statutory. This drives FL.4's filing-order table.

### 5.4 Owner may install their own septic — [V], and it is NOT in ch. 489

The exemption is in **§ 381.0065(4)** itself, not ch. 489 Part III (which was
read in full and contains no owner carve-out — only § 489.552 for licensed
plumbers):

*"A property owner who personally performs construction, maintenance, or
repairs to a system serving his or her own owner-occupied single-family
residence is exempt from registration requirements for performing such
construction, maintenance, or repairs on that residence, but is subject to all
permitting requirements."*

Conditions read literally: **personally**, **own**, **owner-occupied**,
**single-family**, and exempt from **registration only**.

**But the site evaluation for a NEW system cannot be self-performed** —
Rule 62-6.004(3) limits it to a Florida licensed engineer with soils training,
department personnel, a Master Septic Tank Contractor, a certified professional
soil scientist, or a person certified under § 381.0101. A plain Registered
Septic Tank Contractor may only do existing-system evaluations and repairs.

### 5.5 Forms — [V]

**DEP 4015** (construction permit application, eff. June 2026), **DEP 4016**
(construction permit and inspection, valid **18 months**), **DEP 4015A**
(private provider inspector notice), **DEP 4081/4081A** (operating permit).
**"DH 4015" is stale nomenclature** still appearing on one DEP page — do not
print it. Pull the current form from the flrules.org reference link inside
Rule 62-6.004 rather than the DEP FAQ hyperlink, which points at an older
reference number.

Operating permits are **not** required for an ordinary residential gravity
system outside a BMAP/impacted area; they are required annually for
commercial/industrial waste and biennially for aerobic treatment units and
performance-based systems.

### 5.6 Wells — [V]

- Permits from the **five water management districts** under Rule 62-532 and
  the districts' 40A/40B/40C/40D/40E-3 chapters. Rule 62-532.400(1): a permit
  is required before construction, repair or abandonment, applied for *"by the
  owner or by the water well contractor on behalf of the owner."*
- Contractor licensure: **§ 373.323**, by the district.
- **Owner exemption, § 373.326(2)** — narrow: a well *"2 inches or under in
  diameter, on the person's own or leased property, intended for use only in a
  single-family house which is his or her residence."* That is a driven well
  point, **not** the 4–6" cased well a modern household needs. And the
  exemption is from **licensure only** — the district permit still applies.
- **NEGATIVE FINDING — [V]:** Florida's rule does **not** mandate a
  bacteriological test before occupancy for a true single-family private well.
  The testing regime in Rule 64E-8.003(5) is scoped to **Multifamily** systems
  (3–4 residences) and Limited Use Public Water Systems. Lenders and title
  companies often require one anyway — that is a lending practice, not a state
  rule, and the kit does not conflate them. (Not printed in the kit; recorded
  here for the guide and for Site Plan Studio copy.)
- Note the drinking-water-quality program stayed with **DOH (ch. 64E-8)**; only
  the OSTDS program transferred to DEP.

## 6. COASTAL, FLOOD, IMPACT FEES

### 6.1 CCCL — [V]

- **§ 161.053**; **Rule 62B-33, F.A.C.** A **separate FDEP permit**, independent
  of the local building permit, for construction seaward of the coastal
  construction control line.
- **30-year erosion projection**: § 161.053(5)(b) bars a permit for a structure
  *"proposed for a location that… will be seaward of the seasonal high-water
  line within 30 years after the date of application."* Narrow exception for
  certain pre-existing platted single-family lots at § 161.053(5)(c).
- § 161.053(3): some local governments administer their own coastal codes "in
  lieu of" the state process under FDEP oversight — coastal buyers must check
  which applies.
- **Coastal building zone** (§ 161.54(1)) is a *different, larger* area: from
  the seasonal high-water line landward to **1,500 feet** landward of the CCCL —
  it triggers § 161.55 construction standards, **not** a separate FDEP permit.
  On coastal barrier islands it extends **5,000 feet** landward or the whole
  island, whichever is less; **all of Monroe County in the Keys** is included.
- Program page and map verified 200:
  https://floridadep.gov/rcp/coastal-construction-control-line

### 6.2 Flood — [V] mechanism, [H] the numbers

- FEMA Map Service Center verified 200: https://msc.fema.gov/portal/home
- Elevation Certificate is FEMA Form **FF-206-FY-22-152** (formerly 086-0-33).
- **Freeboard is local** — no single statewide figure was found. **Not printed.**
- **ASCE 24 edition referenced by the current FBC could not be verified** —
  code text was not reachable from a primary source. **Not printed.**
- The two-checkpoint elevation requirement **is** verified, but from
  FBC Building 110.3 (lowest floor, and final), not from a freeboard rule.

### 6.3 Impact fees — [V]

- **Collection may not be required earlier than the date the building permit
  issues** (§ 163.31801(4)(e)).
- Caps (§ 163.31801(6)): increase ≤ 25% phased over two annual increments;
  > 25% and ≤ 50% phased over four; **may not exceed 50%**; **not more than once
  every 4 years**; no retroactive increase. Exceeding requires a demonstrated-need
  study within 12 months, two noticed workshops, and a **two-thirds vote**.
- **90 days' notice** before a new or increased fee, and new or increased fees
  *"may not apply to current or pending permit applications submitted before the
  effective date"* unless the result reduces what is owed (§ 163.31801(4)(d)) —
  **filing before an announced increase locks the old rate.**
- § 163.31801(12): **water and sewer connection fees are not impact fees** and
  are not capped by this section.
- § 163.31801(13) requires every local government to publish its schedule and
  methodology — the reader's verified path to a real number.

## 7. SITE PLAN STUDIO EXTRACTION

Feeds `src/lib/siteplan/rules.ts`. **Primary rule: Rule 62-6.005, F.A.C.,
"Location and Installation," current version effective 6-8-2026, reviewed
8-3-26.** Detail page: https://www.flrules.org/gateway/ruleNo.asp?id=62-6.005
Governing statute: § 381.0065, F.S.

**Florida genuinely has numbers.** Unlike several states in this program, none
of the core separations needs to be null.

### 7.1 Setbacks FROM the septic system (tank and drainfield alike)

Rule 62-6.005 does **not** distinguish tank from drainfield for these — it sets
them for the "onsite sewage treatment and disposal system" as a whole.

| To | Distance | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| Private potable well, or multi-family water well | **75 ft** | 62-6.005(1)(a) |
| Public drinking water well, facility flow ≤ 2,000 gpd | **100 ft** | 62-6.005(1)(b) |
| Public drinking water well, facility flow > 2,000 gpd | **200 ft** | 62-6.005(1)(c) |
| Non-potable water well (irrigation / heat pump) | **50 ft** | 62-6.005(1)(d) |
| Storm sewer pipe | **10 ft**, and never less than **5 ft** | 62-6.005(1)(e) |
| Design high-water line of retention/detention areas, swales, or normally dry ditches | **15 ft** | 62-6.005(1)(f), (3) |
| Building foundations, including pilings for elevated structures | **5 ft** (and never under a building) | 62-6.005(2) |
| Mobile home walls, swimming pool walls | **5 ft** | 62-6.005(2) |
| Property lines | **5 ft** | 62-6.005(2) |
| Water storage tanks in ground contact, or potable water lines | **10 ft** (sealed-sleeve exception) | 62-6.005(2)(b) |
| Any water line, minimum | **24 in** | 62-6.005(2)(b) |
| Groundwater interceptor drains | **15 ft** | 62-6.005(2)(c) |
| Surface water bodies (lateral) | **75 ft** | 62-6.005(3) |

Effluent transmission line (schedule 40 PVC), 62-6.005(8): **25 ft** from
private potable wells, irrigation wells or surface water bodies; **2 ft** from
property lines and building foundations; **5 ft** from potable water lines and
stormwater lines.

Verbatim anchor, 62-6.005(1): *"Onsite sewage treatment and disposal systems
established after the effective date of the rule must be placed no closer than
the minimum distances indicated for the following: (a) Seventy-five feet from a
private potable well… or a multi-family water well…"*

Verbatim, 62-6.005(2): *"Onsite sewage treatment and disposal systems must not
be located under buildings or within 5 feet of building foundations, including
pilings for elevated structures, or within 5 feet of mobile home walls,
swimming pool walls, or within 5 feet of property lines except where property
lines abut utility easements which do not contain underground utilities…"*

**Exception to carry into the tool's copy:** sidewalks, decks and patios are
**not** subject to the 5-foot setback, but a drainfield may not be installed
beneath them (62-6.005(2)(a)).

### 7.2 Well-type definitions — these change which number applies

Rule 62-6.002, eff. 7-9-2025:
- **(47)(a) Private potable well** — used by **one or two** residences, one of
  which may be a rental. → 75 ft.
- **(47)(c) Multi-family water well** — used by **three or four** residences. →
  75 ft.
- **(47)(b) Public drinking water well** — any other drinking water system,
  including a *limited use public water system*. → 100 or 200 ft by flow.
- **(42) Non-potable water well** — irrigation only, or heat pump supply/return.
  → 50 ft. **Not a second-tier drinking well.**

### 7.3 Surface water — an important negative finding

**NEGATIVE FINDING [V]:** 62-6.005 sets **no different numeric setback for
tidal vs. non-tidal** surface water. Both are the flat **75 ft** as a defined
class ("surface water bodies," Rule 62-6.002(57)).

**Critical distinction for the tool:** a **normally dry roadside ditch or
stormwater retention area is 15 ft**, not 75 ft. A **year-round pond, lake,
canal or tidal creek is 75 ft**. Getting this wrong in either direction
misplaces the drainfield.

### 7.4 Lot size and density

- **Minimum lot area 1/2 acre (21,780 sq ft)** for the § 381.0065(4)(a) case —
  Rule 62-6.005(7)(a): *"Contiguous area outside of the lot must not be included
  in the determination of the lot area."*
- **Density is a flow allowance, not a lots-per-acre rule** — Rule
  62-6.005(7)(c): authorized daily sewage flow = net usable area ×
  **2,500 gallons per acre per day** on a public drinking water system, or
  × **1,500 gallons per acre per day** on a limited-use public system, private
  potable well, multi-family well, cistern or other private supply.
  **Use the flow formula. Do not print a "four lots per acre" figure** — see
  DELIBERATELY NOT PRINTED #6.

### 7.5 Unobstructed area — Florida's version of a reserve field

Rule 62-6.005(4): the minimum unobstructed area must *"be at least 1.5 times as
large as the drainfield absorption area required by rule"* (inclusive of the
drainfield), be **contiguous** to the drainfield, and be **in addition to** the
setbacks in (1), (2) and (3).

**NEGATIVE FINDING [V]:** Florida has **no separately-sized 100% replacement
reserve area** requirement. The 1.5× unobstructed area *is* the repair buffer.
Do not model a second full-size reserve field for Florida.

### 7.6 Vertical separation — drives filled/mound systems

- **Wet-season water table at least 24 in below the bottom of the drainfield**
  (Rule 62-6.006(2)). A system may not go where the undrained naturally
  occurring wet-season table is at or above existing grade — unless permitted
  fill brings the final lot elevation up, in which case approval is based on
  final elevation. This is exactly why filled and mound systems are common in
  Florida.
- **Effective soil depth 42 in or more below the bottom of the drainfield**
  (Rule 62-6.006(1)).
- **Maximum depth from the bottom of the drainfield to finished grade: 30 in**
  after settling; **minimum earth cover 6 in** over drainfield, distribution box
  or header pipe in standard subsurface systems (Rule 62-6.014(5)(f)).

### 7.7 Well-side cross-check, and a live rule defect worth knowing

Rule 62-532.400(7) says wells *"shall be located to comply with the setback
distances in Table I at the end of this chapter"* — **but the rule that housed
Table I (62-532.900) was repealed effective 2-16-2012, and 62-532.400(7) was
never amended to match.** The cross-reference now points at a repealed table.

The last adopted Table I (10-7-2010) still cites the pre-transfer "64E-6.005"
numbering, and its figures cross-validate cleanly: **75 ft** private/multifamily
well to OSTDS, **50 ft** irrigation/geothermal well to OSTDS, **200/100 ft**
public supply well by flow.

**Recommendation: drive the tool from 62-6.005, which is actively maintained.**
Cite Table I only as corroboration, never as the authority.

### 7.8 Well construction details (secondary, for completeness)

Rule 62-532.500(4)(b)2.: casing terminus projects **at least 12 in above
finished grade** (or, if enclosed, to a point **18 in or less below** grade),
sealed watertight. Grouting (62-532.500(3)(i)4.): annular space filled bottom to
top with a nominal **2 in** of neat cement grout for casing ≥ 4 in outside
diameter; **1 in** for smaller.

### 7.9 What to leave NULL for Florida

- **Freeboard above BFE** — local ordinance, no statewide number.
- **Minimum lot frontage/dimension for a private-well lot** — the ½-acre area is
  verified; the companion dimension clause is not.
- **Any tidal-specific surface water setback** — none exists; do not invent one.
- **A second reserve drainfield area** — does not exist as such; model 1.5×.

### 7.10 Setbacks are FLOORS

DEP's own FAQ notes *"many counties have local ordinances that may exceed state
requirements for OSTDS."* Every number above is a statewide minimum a county may
increase. The tool should say so.
