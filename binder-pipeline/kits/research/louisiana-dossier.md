## WARNING FOR FUTURE EDITORS — FIVE MINEFIELDS

  1. **THE STATUTE NUMBERS CHANGED ON 1 AUGUST 2026 AND ALMOST NOTHING HAS CAUGHT UP.**
     **Acts 2026, No. 881 (HB 1186)** enacted Chapter 62 of Title 37 (R.S. 37:3727–3750) and, by
     its Section 5, repealed R.S. 40:1730.21 through 1730.40.2 **"in its entirety."** The agency
     was renamed from the Louisiana State Uniform Construction Code **Council (LSUCCC)** to the
     Louisiana Uniform Construction Code **Commission (LUCCC)**. *Every* third-party page, every
     code-adoption tracker, and many parish permit pages still cite the Title 40 numbers — i.e.
     repealed law. **[V]**

  2. **legis.la.gov LAGS ITS OWN LEGISLATURE.** As of 2 September 2026 the Legislature's statute
     browser still served R.S. 40:1730.21–.32 as though live (complete with "eff. until July 1,
     2026" notes from an *earlier* act) and returned **nothing at all** for a Title 37 / §3727
     citation lookup. A control lookup of R.S. 37:2157 resolved fine, so the tool works — the
     Title 37 chapter simply has not been loaded. **Do not conclude from a failed lookup that the
     recodification did not happen.** Read the enrolled Act text instead. **[V]**

  3. **THE CONTRACTOR CHAPTER WAS REWRITTEN THREE TIMES IN FOUR YEARS.** Acts 2022 No. 195, Acts
     2024 No. 178, Acts 2025 No. 422. The exemption statute everyone cites — **R.S. 37:2170** —
     and R.S. 37:2171 with it were **repealed by Acts 2022, No. 195, §2**. The owner-builder
     exemption now lives at **R.S. 37:2157(A)(13)**. The threshold everyone quotes, $75,000, is
     now **$50,000**. **[V]**

  4. **THE SANITARY CODE MIXES MANDATORY AND ADVISORY VERBS IN THE SAME TABLE OF NUMBERS.**
     LAC 51:XIII is an old engineering handbook converted into rule text. Some separation
     distances say "shall"/"must"; others say "should" or "it is desirable." Printing an advisory
     number as a requirement is the single easiest way to get this state wrong. The verb audit is
     in the SITE PLAN section below and must be preserved in any revision. **[V]**

  5. **SEPARATION DISTANCES ARE SPREAD ACROSS FOUR DIFFERENT PARTS OF TWO DIFFERENT LAC TITLES**,
     and Part XIII (sewage) cross-references Part XII (water) for everything involving a well.
     Anyone who reads only the sewage Part produces an incomplete table; anyone who reads a blog
     produces a merged, wrong one. There is also a **second, independent well table** in
     LAC 56:I.315 published by a different agency, and a **third** set of water-line distances in
     LAC 51:XIV.613. Apply the stricter of any conflicting pair. **[V]**

## 1. THE STATUTORY FRAMEWORK

**[V] The construction-code program is now La. R.S. 37:3727–3750**, "Building Code Adoption &
Inspector Licensing Law," Chapter 62 of Title 37. Enacted by Acts 2026, No. 881 (HB 1186, Rep.
Jacob Landry), 2026 Regular Session. Act text quoted from the enrolled PDF at
`legis.la.gov/legis/ViewDocument.aspx?d=1481512`.

  - Act 881, Section 5, verbatim: *"Part IV-A of Chapter 8 of Title 40 of the Louisiana Revised
    Statutes of 1950, comprised of R.S. 40:1730.21 through 1730.40.2, is hereby repealed in its
    entirety."*
  - **Effective 1 August 2026.** The Act carries no general effective-date clause (only a Section 7
    carve-out putting R.S. 37:3730(C)(1) at 1 January 2027), so the Louisiana default applies. The
    agency confirms the date in its own editor's note to the 2023 NEC / 2024 I-Code adoption
    language: *"Effective August 1, 2026, R.S. 40:1730.21 through 40:1730.40.2 were repealed and
    the authority of the Louisiana Uniform Construction Code Commission was enabled by
    R.S. 37:3727 through 37:3750 pursuant to Act 881 of the 2026 Regular Session."* **[V]**

**[V] Statewide, mandatory, no local opt-out.** R.S. 37:3737(A)(1), verbatim: *"Notwithstanding any
law to the contrary relating to the authority of local governments to enforce building codes, all
municipalities and parishes in this state shall enforce only the Uniform Construction Code provided
in this Chapter … Nothing in this Chapter allows any local government to avoid enforcement, or to
otherwise amend any of the mandatory construction code provisions required in this Chapter, or to
prohibit licensed contractors or homeowners from using licensed private inspectors."*

**[V] The old parish exemption-by-affidavit is gone.** R.S. 37:3741 still carries the vestigial
section heading "…affidavit for exemption," but the operative text contains no such affidavit. The
only surviving affidavit provisions in the chapter are the contractor-license-exemption affidavit
(§3748(D)) and the private-recreational-structure affidavit (LAC 55:VI.503.B.1). **Do not print the
"some parishes are exempt by affidavit" folklore.**

**[V] Parish must appoint a building official for its unincorporated area.** R.S. 37:3741, verbatim:
*"A public entity shall appoint a person to act as its building official to oversee the
unincorporated area of the public entity. Only a licensed inspector holding a general license
classification may be appointed…"*

**[V] Enforcement may be contracted out.** R.S. 37:3740 authorizes an agreement with another public
entity or with a licensed private inspector. R.S. 37:3737(G) confirms a regional planning commission
may hold the building-official role.

**[V] THE OWNER-BUILDER'S PRIVATE-INSPECTOR ROUTE — the most useful sentence in the state.**
R.S. 37:3737(A)(1), verbatim: *"Licensed contractors, and homeowners exempted from the contractor
licensing law pursuant to R.S. 37:2157, may establish agreements with private inspectors to conduct
plan reviews, inspections, and enforce the State Uniform Construction Code."* With the condition,
verbatim: *"A local jurisdiction shall not accept an inspection report or plan review … from a
private inspector unless that inspector has a contract to provide inspection services with that
jurisdiction … or has registered with that jurisdiction."*

**[V] The State Fire Marshal is NOT a statewide residential fallback.** R.S. 37:3735(A)(1): the fire
marshal *"may"* enter an agreement by letter of intent to enforce on behalf of a parish — permissive,
and requiring an agreement. Its own standing jurisdiction expressly **excludes** one- and two-family
dwellings: R.S. 37:3735(C), *"The fire marshal shall enforce the fire protection, egress, and
accessibility provisions of the Uniform Construction Code, excluding the provisions for one- and
two-family dwellings…"*

**[V] All 64 parish governments are registered code-enforcement jurisdictions.** Every one of the 64
was run through the Commission's public jurisdiction registry; all 64 returned a credential number.
This is the strongest single piece of evidence that no Louisiana parish is off the permitting map.

## 2. CONTRACTOR LICENSING AND THE OWNER-BUILDER EXEMPTION

**[V] Threshold: $50,000, not $75,000.** R.S. 37:2150.1(19)(a) defines "residential contractor" by
reference to a project value of *"fifty thousand dollars or more"*; R.S. 37:2150.1(4)(a)(ii) does the
same for any new residential structure.

**[V] What counts toward the threshold — quoted because it is counter-intuitive.**
R.S. 37:2150.1(3), verbatim: *"The project value includes the entire cost of the labor, materials,
rentals, and all direct and indirect project expenses. The cost of materials, rentals, and direct and
indirect expenses shall be included regardless of who pays the costs or if they are donated."*
Owner-supplied and donated materials count. **[H] The statute assigns no dollar value to the owner's
own uncompensated labor, and none is printed.**

**[V] Other thresholds:** improvements/repairs to an existing residence and home improvement,
**$7,500** (37:2150.1(4)(a)(iii), (10), (11), (19)(b)(i)); electrical contractor, mechanical
contractor and plumbing contractor, **$10,000** each (37:2150.1(6), (13), (16)).

**[V] The exemption: R.S. 37:2157(A)(13)**, quoted in full in LA.1. Key readings:
  - It uses **management verbs** — "supervise, superintend, oversee, direct, or in any manner assume
    charge" — so the owner may **hire and direct subcontractors**. The contrast that proves it is in
    the same section: §2157(A)(15)(a) exempts an owner who *"physically performs"* home improvement
    work. Those words are absent from (13).
  - **No holding period and no anti-resale clause.** The "will not be for sale or rent" language
    exists in Louisiana law but in §2157(A)(2), the *general* owner exemption, which also demands
    controlled access and is unusable on a house lot. Do not let the two be conflated.
  - **One residence per year, measured from the date the certificate of occupancy issues.** Two
    express escapes: change in legal marital status; change in employment requiring relocation more
    than fifty miles from the personal residence.
  - **§2157(B):** *"The provisions of this Section shall not be construed to waive local and state
    health and life safety code requirements."*

**[V] The affidavit.** Statutory hooks: R.S. 37:2157(A)(13) final sentence and R.S. 37:2160(C)
(*"submitted to the local building permit official prior to the issuance of a permit"*). The actual
document is the LSLBC **"AFFIDAVIT CLAIMING EXEMPTION FROM LICENSURE,"** revision of **1 August
2025**, **no form number**, **notarised**, structured as a header plus **eleven initialed
statements**. It states it is *"the only document authorized by the Louisiana State Licensing Board
for Contractors pursuant to La. R.S. 37:2160(C) to claim exemption from licensure."*
**Trap:** the same forms page carries a **Church Owner/Builder Affidavit** implementing a different
exemption (§2157(A)(3)) — and the words "Owner/Builder" appear in the *church* form's title, not the
homeowner's.

**[V] Why the permit clerk insists.** R.S. 37:2160(B): *"A local building department shall not issue
a building permit to any person who does not hold an active license … Nothing in this Section shall
prohibit a local building department from issuing a permit for work that does not require a license
pursuant to this Chapter."* The prohibition is on the office, and the affidavit is the door.
R.S. 37:2160(A) lets board staff inspect each local permit official's list of issued permits **every
month**.

**[V] Penalty.** R.S. 37:2163(C)(1): misdemeanour, fine *"not to exceed five hundred dollars per day
of violation,"* or three months, or both. (C)(2) escalates where harm exceeds $300.

**[H] Subcontractors do not ride on the owner's exemption.** R.S. 37:2157(A)(17) exempts subs only
*"for a residential construction license holder"* — and an exempt owner is not a license holder —
and it carves out electrical, mechanical and plumbing regardless. The board's own affidavit says the
same in plainer words. **No case, AG opinion or board rule construing (A)(17) against an exempt owner
was found**, so the kit frames it as the safe reading rather than a settled holding.

## 3. THE THREE-CHAPTER STRUCTURE — the framing that makes Louisiana make sense

This is the organising insight of LA.1 and it is not stated anywhere else. An owner-builder's
position is set by three separate bodies of law, and the contractor exemption reaches only the first.

  1. **Contractor licensing** — R.S. 37:2150 et seq., LSLBC. §2157(A)(13) switches this off.
  2. **Plumbing and gas fitting** — R.S. 37:1361 et seq., State Plumbing Board. **§2157 does not
     reach this chapter at all.**
  3. **Individual sewage** — LAC 51:XIII, state health officer.

**[V] ELECTRICIANS: there is no Louisiana journeyman or master electrician license.** No state board
of electrical examiners exists. Full-text search of the Revised Statutes returns 9 sections for
"journeyman plumber" (control) and **0** for "journeyman electrician" or "master electrician."
Individual credentialing is municipal and **optional** — R.S. 33:4782, verbatim: *"All municipalities,
except New Orleans, may enact ordinances for the purpose of regulating persons pursuing or engaged in
the business of installing wires or apparatus to convey electric current… The municipalities may in
their discretion vest the board with full power, control, and regulation of the business. The power
or final inspection of any of the work shall be vested solely in the city electrician."* Note
"may enact," "in their discretion," and the express New Orleans carve-out.

**[V] PLUMBING: the homeowner allowance is a DEFINITIONAL exclusion, not an exemption.** There is no
homeowner carve-out in the licensing sections — R.S. 37:1367(A)(1), (A)(2) and (B) require the
board's license of any natural person doing residential-limited, journeyman or master plumbing work,
naming no owner. The allowance is in R.S. 37:1377(D), verbatim: *"For purposes of this Chapter the
definition of plumbing given above will not include: … (8) Work done by an individual on his own
personal residence."* Two neighbors matter: **(D)(2) drilling of water wells** is also outside
"plumbing," and **(D)(9)**, the owner exclusion for *maintenance* work on property generally,
*"shall specifically not include construction or installation"* — a limit paragraph (8) does not
carry.

**[V] GAS FITTING has its own, true homeowner exemption.** R.S. 37:1367(J)(2): *"The provisions of
this Subsection shall not apply to work performed by persons on their own residences."* It attaches
only to the gas-fitter subsection.

**[H] The plumbing chapter's reach is geographic.** R.S. 37:1375, verbatim: *"This Chapter shall
apply to: A. All cities, towns, villages, communities and public sewerage and/or water districts in
the State of Louisiana. B. All areas within one mile of the boundary of any city or sewer or water
districts, referred to above; and all areas within one mile of the community, sewerage or water
facilities of the areas referred to above."* Last amended 1964/1968; **"communities" is not defined**.
The kit prints the text and tells the reader to get a written answer from the board rather than
concluding on their own that a parcel is outside the chapter's reach.

**[V] SEPTIC: owner self-install is allowed, except a mechanical plant.** LAC 51:XIII.705.A,
verbatim: *"Such a license shall not be required, however, for an individual wishing to install an
individual sewerage system, other than an individual mechanical plant, for his own private, personal
use. Individual mechanical plants shall be installed and maintenance provided by licensed individual
sewerage system installers and/or maintenance providers only."* §705.B keeps the owner responsible
for the permit and approved plans regardless.

**[V] LDH's operational statement is stricter than the rule reads.** Form SF-10ST (rev. 07/25),
item 1: the owner *"may install a septic tank (non-mechanical) system for their own personal use only
if the property owner does the actual installation. **Hiring an unlicensed person to perform any part
of the installation of your septic tank will not be allowed under this exception and the system in
question will not be approved by this office.**"* Item 4: systems *"must be inspected by an Office of
Public Health sanitarian before any portion of the system is covered."*

**[V] WELLS ARE THE MIRROR IMAGE — you may NOT drill your own.** LAC 56:I.307.A: *"The following
wells and holes shall be drilled or constructed by a licensed contractor (driller)… 1. all water
wells, regardless of use or type."* No pre-drilling permit for a domestic well; notification is due
**within 60 days AFTER** installation in a non-critical ground water area (LAC 43:VI.701.C), and
registration is the **contractor's** duty (LAC 56:I.105.A).
**[H] Do not print the hand-auger route as DIY.** LAC 56:I.105.B.2 exempts driven/hand-augered wells
from **registration**; that is not an exemption from the **licensed-driller** rule in §307.A.

### 4a. THE FREEBOARD QUESTION — the hypothesis in the brief was WRONG in both directions

Two competing wrong claims circulate: that Louisiana requires BFE + 1 ft statewide, and that
Louisiana *removed* its statewide freeboard so elevation is purely local. **Neither matches the
current text.**

**[V] Louisiana's amendment to 2021 IRC Section R322.1**, verbatim (final sentence):
*"Buildings and structures located in whole or in part in identified floodways shall be designed and
constructed in accordance with ASCE 24-14. **The local jurisdictions, utilizing flood plain managers,
shall have the authority to adopt higher freeboard amounts as needed (CRS, etc.) but shall not have
the authority to adopt freeboard amounts less than those required in ASCE-24-14**"*

So: **no number is written into Louisiana's code, but there IS a statewide floor**, expressed by
reference to ASCE 24-14, and locals may go above it and may not go under it. The parallel commercial
provision amends IBC §1612.2 identically. The policy is carried forward, not dropped: the
Commission's approved 2024 IRC language re-enacts the identical sentence (edition-neutral, "ASCE 24")
against renumbered §R306.1.

**[V] Statutory hook:** R.S. 37:3738(B)(3), verbatim: *"For residential or commercial construction,
the standards published by the Federal Emergency Management Agency for the National Flood Insurance
Program apply."*

**[V] Citation caution:** the two official printings head the same amendment text differently — the
Office of the State Register's LAC Title 17 says "Amend — Section R322.2.1, General," the Commission's
compiled volume says "Amend — Section R322.1, General." Cite it as *"Louisiana's amendment to IRC
Section R322, LAC 17:I.107"* and quote the text rather than leaning on the sub-number.

**[H] Whether an EARLIER cycle carried an explicit numeric freeboard that was later dropped could not
be settled.** LAC 55:VI Chapters 1–3 have been removed from the published LAC (moved to Title 17), so
superseded text is not in the current compilation. **Do not print any sentence of the form "Louisiana
repealed its statewide freeboard in [year] by Act [N]."** Also verified: **R.S. 40:1730.28(A)(3)(g),
"Repealed by Acts 2022, No. 635," is NOT a freeboard repealer** — Act 635 is HB 803, captioned
"BUILDING CODES: Provides relative to the State Building Energy Conservation Code."

### 4b. WIND — there is no Louisiana wind table

**[V] Louisiana adopts the 2021 IRC's wind provisions unamended for dwellings.** Exhaustive search of
LAC Title 17 found **no** amendment to IRC §R301.2, Table R301.2, Figures R301.2(2)–(5), §R301.2.1.1
or §R301.2.1.2. Ultimate design wind speed comes from IRC Figure R301.2(2) with the site value entered
by the jurisdiction into **Table R301.2, which the 2021 IRC prints blank for the jurisdiction to
complete and which Louisiana did not fill in statewide.** Wind-borne debris region is the unamended
IRC definition. **There is no official Louisiana wind-speed-by-parish table in the LAC.** The kit
therefore tells the buyer to get the design wind speed and the debris determination in writing from
the permit office, and prints no table.

**[V] A wind number that IS statutory:** R.S. 37:3738(B)(1)(b) sizes the residential-accessory-
structure exemption at **200 sq ft** and excludes structures *"constructed in regions where the
ultimate design windspeed … equal or exceed one hundred thirty miles per hour in hurricane-prone
regions."* **Change worth flagging:** the repealed R.S. 40:1730.30(A)(2) allowed **500 sq ft** on a
**100 mph basic-wind-speed** trigger. Anyone planning a shed on the old numbers will be wrong.

**[V-negative] The shutter-affidavit occupancy allowance is gone.** The old R.S. 40:1730.23(F) let a
jurisdiction grant occupancy on an owner's affidavit that shutters were ordered. The word "shutter"
appears **zero** times in the enrolled text of Act 881. Do not print it as current law.

  - **Absorption field → embankment / cut slope: no statewide number.**
  - **Well → building/foundation: no number, narrative standard only.** LAC 56:I.321.A (and
    LAC 51:XII.169.D.1.b): *"A well shall be located far enough from a building to allow reworking or
    rehabilitation with a drilling rig. A well shall not be located below ground surface, such as in
    pits and basements, and shall not be located within the foundation of a building…"*
  - **Well → pasture: no number.** "Barnyards" appear in the §327.A.2 lead-in list with no figure.
    "Feed lots, manure piles" get 100 ft.
  - **Mound system and subsurface drip: NO statewide table at all.** LAC 51:XIII.733.E — case by case,
    plans to OPH engineering services.
  - **No statewide "must connect to public sewer if within X feet."** LAC 51:XIII.511.A requires
    connection where a community system is *"available"* and defines availability by four factors —
    *"the separation (both horizontal and vertical) of the structure in question and the sewer main
    or lateral, political or geographic or legally created boundaries, and the available capacity"* —
    applied case by case by the state health officer. **Print the four factors; print no distance.**
  - **The 50 ft ownership / 100 ft control radius in LAC 51:XII.169.D.2 is a PUBLIC water system rule**
    (Chapter 2, Public Water System Construction). It does **not** apply to a domestic well, and it is
    a frequent contaminator of third-party "private well" advice.

### 5d. MINIMUM LOT SIZE — Louisiana has one, and it is strict [V]

LAC 51:XIII.511.B, the gate on using an individual system at all:
  - **§511.B.4 — baseline for a single lot: 22,500 sq ft area and 125 ft frontage.**
  - §511.B.3 — large lots: one acre or more with 125 ft frontage.
  - §511.B.6 — **only in parishes whose governing authority "has enacted and enforces a formal sewage
    permitting system"**: 22,500/80; or **16,000/80 with a mechanical plant**; or **12,000/60 with a
    mechanical plant followed by 50 ft of modified absorption field**.
  - §511.B.2 — total anticipated design flow not exceeding **1,500 gpd**.

**Interaction worth printing:** two of the three reduced sizes require a mechanical plant, and a
mechanical plant may not be owner-installed (§705.A). A small Louisiana lot therefore pushes an
owner-builder off the self-install path without anyone saying so.

### 5e. PLOT-PLAN CONTENT, AND OWNER-DRAWN ACCEPTANCE [V]

**There is NO statewide plot-plan content rule in the LAC.** The list below is LDH/OPH's own applicant
packet ("Information Packet for Applicants"), Section IV, Step Two — agency practice, not codified
rule. `mustShow`:
  1. All structures — houses, buildings, driveways, pool, barns — with proposed use and location
  2. All potable water lines, existing or proposed
  3. Private water well, clearly shown, **plus the piping from it to any structures**
  4. Sewage treatment system location relative to all potable water sources
  5. Written distances from the system to **all potable water sources, property boundaries, and
     structures**
  6. Complete description of the **effluent outfall pattern** (oxidation pond or sand filter)
  7. All system parts **clearly labelled**
  8. Trees, landscaping or anything else limiting installation
  9. Property boundaries marked so the sanitarian can see where the lot ends

**Explicit negative findings on plot-plan content:** no **north arrow**, no stated **scale**, no
**contours**, no **soil map**, no **flood-zone notation** is required by this list. Adding them is
harmless; *requiring* them is wrong.

**`ownerDrawnAccepted` — the answer is a SPLIT, and this is worth real money to a buyer:**
  - **The site plan may be owner-drawn and hand-drawn.** Verbatim, twice: *"Your plans can be hand
    drawn as long as they are legible and include all necessary information."* and *"It is not
    necessary that you have your plans drawn by a professional; however, if you do them yourself,
    they must be clearly labeled with all necessary information shown, and they must be legible."*
    The packet's own example is a visibly hand-drawn sketch.
  - **The property PLAT may not be self-produced.** Step One: *"The plat must have the State Surveyors
    Seal or it must be designated as a 'true copy' by the Clerk of Court's office."* Reinforced in
    rule for subdivision lots at LAC 51:XIII.701.E.
  - **The money-saving line:** a recorded plat you already own, certified a "true copy" by the clerk
    of court, satisfies this **without hiring a surveyor**.

**`setbacksNote`:** building setbacks are local zoning. Lot size, off-lot discharge bans and any
building-permit plot-plan requirements are parish-level; the packet says so twice, including
*"Some parishes do not allow any off lot discharges."* **The off-lot discharge ban is the single
biggest parish variable in Louisiana** — it decides whether an oxidation pond, sand filter or
ATU-with-discharge is legal on the lot at all.

  1. **Any statewide freeboard number.** The floor is ASCE 24-14, a copyrighted standard, and the

  2. **A wind-speed-by-parish table.** None exists in the LAC; Table R301.2 is jurisdiction-completed
     and Louisiana did not fill it in. A transcribed secondary table is exactly the number that gets a
     house built wrong. **[V-negative]**
  3. **Any claim that Louisiana repealed a statewide freeboard in a named year by a named Act.**
     Could not be sourced; the superseded LAC text is no longer published. **[H]**
  4. **The shutter-affidavit occupancy allowance.** Not carried forward into Act 881. **[V-negative]**
  5. **A "must connect to public sewer within X feet" distance.** No such number exists. **[V-negative]**
  6. **Deep permit-page URLs, one per parish.** They split across table columns, they rot fastest of

  7. **Office names for 27 parishes** whose official page could not be read during verification. They
     are named as a list with the reason, not guessed at.
  8. **Any phone number.** Product rule.
  9. **A statewide required-inspection schedule.** Louisiana does not publish one for dwellings. The
     kit prints one parish's published list, labelled as such, plus a working log to amend locally.

  10. **R.S. 37:2157(A)(14)**, exempting residential contractors *"in areas or municipalities that do

  11. **Any dollar value for the owner's own sweat equity** toward the $50,000 threshold. No primary

  13. **Named third-party inspection providers as recommendations.** They are named only where a

## LATE ADDENDUM — 2 September 2026: the recodification, settled

The first pass of this research recorded the Title 37 renumbering as **[H] hedged**, because the
Commission's compiled volume used R.S. 37:3727 et seq. while legis.la.gov still served Title 40 and
returned nothing for a Title 37 lookup. Two candidate acts were checked and **both eliminated**:

  - **Acts 2024, No. 534 (SB489)** amends R.S. 40:1578.6, 1578.7, 1730.22(A), 1730.23(A),
    1730.28(A)(1), 1730.39, 1733, 1737, 1738 **in place**, keeps Title 40 numbering, and repeals only
    R.S. 40:1732 and 1734–1736. Effective 1 July 2026. **Not the recodification.**
  - **Acts 2025, No. 120 (SB228)** enacted R.S. 37:2166 placing the Council administratively inside
    the LSLBC and amended R.S. 40:1730.22. Still calls the body a **Council**. **Not it either.**

**Acts 2026, No. 881 (HB 1186) is the act.** Its own title paragraph enacts *"Chapter 62 of Title 37
of the Louisiana Revised Statutes of 1950, to be comprised of R.S. 37:3727 through 3750"* and repeals
*"Part IV-A of Chapter 8 of Title 40 … comprised of R.S. 40:1730.21 through 1730.40.2."* Section 5
repeals that Part *"in its entirety."* Effective **1 August 2026**.

**Consequence for the kit, applied:** the finding was upgraded from [H] to **[V]** and every citation
in LA.2, LA.3, LA.4 and LA.5 was flipped from the belt-and-suspenders "Title 40 / Title 37" dual form
to the Title 37 number alone. The LA.2 callout was rewritten from "one law, two statute numbers" to
"the statute number changed on 1 August 2026 — almost nothing has caught up," which is both accurate
and a stronger selling point: it tells a buyer that essentially every competing Louisiana source is
citing repealed law. Quotations of the stamped-plans rule and the sprinkler ban were replaced with the
**current** Title 37 wording, which differs from the repealed Title 40 wording in more than the
section number ("commission" for "council"; "a municipality or parish shall not" for "Further, no
municipality or parish shall"; "licensed architect or engineer" for "certified architect or
engineer").

## LATE ADDENDUM 2 — 2 September 2026: corrections after the codes pass closed

Three facts printed in the first build were wrong or overstated and have been corrected.

**1. [V] Residential plumbing runs through IRC Part VII, not the IPC.** Louisiana adopts the IRC
*"not including Part I-Administrative, and Part VIII-Electrical"* (R.S. 37:3733(A)(3)) — so Part VII,
the plumbing chapters, is adopted, and that is what a one- or two-family dwelling plumbs to. The IPC
is adopted for the buildings the IRC does not cover. The first cut of LA.2 implied the IPC governed
the house. Louisiana amends the IRC plumbing chapters heavily: compost toilets prohibited (§P2725.1),
minimum 4-inch building sewer (§P3005.2.2.1), new separation sections §P2914.1–.4 carrying 25 ft,
10 ft and 50 ft figures, lead-free written into the definitions, and freeze protection keyed to the
locally-completed Table R301.2 (§P2603.5).

**2. [H] The 2021 IECC effective date cannot be stated to the day.** The Commission's edition sheet
says 1 July 2023; a Louisiana Register citation inside the same publication says 1 August 2023. The
kit now prints **"mid-2023"** and tells the reader to get the date in writing if it matters. Do not
restate either specific day.

**3. [H] The 1 January 2027 next cycle is SCHEDULED, not enacted.** The Commission approved adoption
language for the 2024 I-Codes and 2023 NEC on 7 July 2026 and the approved rule text carries a
1 January 2027 effective date, but the rule still has to complete Administrative Procedure Act
promulgation and legislative oversight under R.S. 37:3732(A)(2) and appear in the *Louisiana
Register*. No such publication existed at verification. The kit's headline now says so explicitly.

### Findings added to the kit in the same pass — all [V]

- **Continuous sheathing is mandatory on every Louisiana house.** Amendment to IRC §R602.10:
  *"In Climate Zone 2A, one and two family dwellings shall be continuously sheathed with a minimum
  7/16" wood structural panels (Table R602.10.4 CS-WSP), or it's structural equivalent as per an
  ICC-ESR and approved by the local building official."* Since every parish is Climate Zone 2A this
  reaches every house, and it takes let-in, portal-frame-only and gypsum-only bracing off the table.
  **The single biggest cost and technique delta versus the unamended IRC.** Companions: 6 mil ASTM
  E1745 Class A under-slab vapor retarder (§R506.2.3); ring-shank roof-sheathing nails required
  wherever wind design is required (Table R602.3(1)); no climate exemption from decay-resistant
  lumber (§R317.1).
- **There is no state inspection schedule, and the reason is precise.** IRC Part I-Administrative is
  IRC Chapter 1, which carries **R109 Inspections**. Louisiana does not adopt Part I, so it does not
  adopt R109, and the repealed predecessor statute expressly barred the agency from adding an
  excluded part back. The schedule comes from the parish's own administrative ordinance under
  R.S. 37:3736(A).
- **The inspector must physically show up.** R.S. 37:3737(H): a licensed inspector shall conduct all
  inspections and *"be present on site for all inspections other than roofing inspections,
  reinspections where that inspector previously visited the site, and emergency utility reconnection
  inspections."* Geotagged photo or video is accepted for those three cases only.
- **Roof work is separately mandated.** R.S. 37:3737(I)(2) requires the jurisdiction to permit and
  inspect dwellings for roof construction and reroofing to IRC Chapters 8 and 9.
- **Local stringency needs the Commission's approval.** R.S. 37:3748(E): *"If a governing authority or
  any municipality or parish finds that the state minimum standards do not meet its needs, the local
  government may with approval of the commission provide requirements more stringent than those
  specified by the state."*
- **Inspector over-enforcement is a named violation.** R.S. 37:3747(A)(11)(a) makes each of these a
  violation of the authority of the commission: *"(i) Enforcing a code requirement in an area or
  circumstance not specified in that requirement. (ii) Enforcing a requirement in a manner that is
  more stringent than or exceeding the code requirement. (iii) Refusing to implement or adhere to an
  interpretation of the Uniform Construction Code issued by the commission. (iv) Enforcing a code
  official's preference in the method or manner of installation if that preference is not required by
  the Uniform Construction Code or contradicts a manufacturer's installation instructions or
  specifications."*
- **Energy verifications need certified testers.** Blower-door and duct-leakage testing must be
  performed by individuals certified by a nationally recognized organization, and the building
  official is directed to accept their written reports. A Louisiana insulation certificate must be
  posted in a utility area — a physical artifact a final inspector looks for.
- **Carbon monoxide alarms are triggered by a standby generator.** Adopted §R315.3 exception requires
  alarms in and outside each sleeping area whenever a fuel-fired standby generator or transfer switch
  is installed. Common in Louisiana; a checkable inspection item.
