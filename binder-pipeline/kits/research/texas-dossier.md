# Texas Owner-Builder Dossier — research for the TX Permit Kit (six documents)

Compiled August 2026. Statute text was read from the Texas Legislature's statute service (statutes.capitol.texas.gov
front end; documents served from tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/) in August 2026 — full chapters downloaded and
sections extracted verbatim. Agency facts verified against the agency's own pages. Every claim is tagged:

  [V] = verified against a primary source (statute text read directly, or the administering agency's own page). Safe
  to print with the citation.
  [H] = hedge — could not be pinned to a primary source, is county/city variable, or is a proven-negative that can
  only be stated carefully. Print only with the hedge language and a verification step.

THE STRUCTURAL HEADLINE (this is the kit's thesis): Texas has no state general-contractor license and no state
building permit for houses — but it is NOT a no-rules state. The rules attach to (1) the three licensed trades, (2)
the land (septic, floodplain, driveway, well), (3) the coast (windstorm), (4) energy (a statewide energy code that
applies even where no permit exists), and (5) the homestead lien system. Which of these bind you depends almost
entirely on one question: is the lot inside a city's limits or not?

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
## TX.0 — Cover summary facts (the two-track structure in headlines)

1. [V] Texas licenses no general contractors and issues no state residential building permit. TDLR's own A-Z list of
   regulated programs (tdlr.texas.gov) contains no general-contractor, home-builder, or residential-construction
   program; the Texas Residential Construction Commission was abolished when its statute expired in 2009. Anyone —
   including you — may build a house. (Careful: this is a provable-absence claim; print it as "Texas has no statewide
   general contractor license" and cite TDLR's program list, not as "no license is ever required," because three
   trades ARE licensed.)

2. [V] TRACK A — inside a city: the International Residential Code "as it existed on May 1, 2012" is adopted by
   statute as the municipal residential building code (Local Gov't Code § 214.212(a)); cities may amend it and adopt
   newer editions (§ 214.212(c)–(e)), and the big cities all have. The city issues the permit and must act within 45
   days (§ 214.904).

3. [V] TRACK B — unincorporated county: counties generally CANNOT require a building permit for a single-family house.
   Even the opt-in county building-standards subchapter says it "may not be construed to require prior approval by the
   county before the beginning of new residential construction" (Local Gov't Code § 233.153(d)(1)). What binds you
   instead: septic permit (Health & Safety Code § 366.051), floodplain development permit (Water Code §§ 16.315,
   16.3145), the statewide energy code (Health & Safety Code §§ 388.003–.004), trade licensing, and — on the coast —
   TDI windstorm certification (Insurance Code Ch. 2210).

4. [V] The three licensed trades follow you everywhere in Texas: electrical (Occupations Code Ch. 1305), plumbing (Ch.
   1301), HVAC (Ch. 1302). Each has a homeowner carve-out — and each carve-out has different words with different
   traps (see TX.1).

5. [V] The homestead lien system is the owner-builder's hidden contract law: no one can fix a lien on your homestead
   unless the contract is in writing, signed BEFORE work begins, signed by BOTH spouses if married, and filed with the
   county clerk (Property Code § 53.254; Tex. Const. art. XVI § 50(a)(5)).

6. [V] The energy code applies even where no building permit exists: outside cities, compliance is shown by an
   accredited program certificate, a private code-certified inspector, or the builder's own certification form — and
   the builder must keep the documentation for three years and give the owner a copy (Health & Safety Code § 388.004).

## TX.1 — "No license, but…" (the owner-builder position in Texas)

### A. The absence: no GC license, no state building permit

- [V] TDLR's public program list (homepage A-Z, tdlr.texas.gov) has no general-contractor or home-builder program.
  TDLR's construction-side programs are: Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractors, Electricians, Water Well
  Drillers/Pump Installers (plus elevators, boilers, industrialized housing). Verified against tdlr.texas.gov August
  2026.
- [H] Print the negative with a hedge: "As of August 2026 no Texas state agency issues a general contractor or home
  builder license; the Texas Residential Construction Commission's statute expired in 2009 and was not replaced.
  Verify nothing has changed at tdlr.texas.gov." (Legislatures can create licenses; a printed absolute would age
  badly.)
- [V] There is also no statewide permit counter: the permit statutes are all municipal (Local Gov't Code Ch. 214) and
  the county statutes affirmatively deny permit power for houses (Ch. 233, below). Corroborated by the Texas State Law
  Library building-codes guide (guides.sll.texas.gov): "State law adopts a minimum residential code requirement for
  all cities and some unincorporated areas of Texas" — codes, not permits.

### B. Electrical — Occupations Code Ch. 1305 (TDLR)

- [V] License required: "Except as provided by Section 1305.003, a person or business may not perform or offer to
  perform electrical work … unless the person or business holds an appropriate license issued or recognized under this
  chapter." (§ 1305.151)
- [V] The homeowner exemption, VERBATIM — § 1305.003(a)(6): this chapter does not apply to "work not specifically
  regulated by a municipal ordinance that is performed in or on a dwelling by a person who owns and resides in the
  dwelling."
- [V] TDLR's own gloss (tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/exemptions.htm): "A person who perform[s] electrical work on a
  dwelling that they own and reside in is not required by the state to be licensed as electricians," with the caveat
  that municipal or regional regulations may override the exemptions per § 1305.201.
- THE TWO TRAPS in (a)(6) — print both:
  - [V] "not specifically regulated by a municipal ordinance": a city ordinance can take the exemption away entirely.
    Houston does — the City of Houston issues electrical permits only to registered/licensed master electricians; a
    homeowner cannot pull an electrical permit in Houston (Houston Permitting Center, Electrical Fixture Permit /
    Meter & Loop pages and the Houston Electrical Code administrative provisions).
  - [H] "owns and RESIDES in the dwelling": a new house under construction is a dwelling you do not yet reside in.
    TDLR has not published a formal interpretation on new construction; the plain text reads as existing-residence
    work. HEDGE HARD: tell the buyer building in an unincorporated area that the safe reading is that wiring a NEW
    house they do not yet occupy is NOT clearly covered by (a)(6), and to either hire a licensed electrician or put
    the question to TDLR in writing before relying on it. (Contrast: the plumbing and HVAC exemptions use
    "homestead"/"home" ownership language, not residence-in-the-dwelling.)
- [V] Even exempt/unincorporated work has a code: "Electrical work performed in an unincorporated area of the state
  must be installed in accordance with standards at least as stringent as the requirements of the state electrical
  code as adopted under Section 1305.101." (§ 1305.201(e)) TDLR must adopt each revised National Electrical Code after
  publication (§ 1305.101(a)(2)); by rule (16 TAC § 73.100) the 2023 NEC is the state electrical code, effective
  September 1, 2023.
- [V] Cities may require inspections and local licenses but may not charge a licensed electrician a registration fee
  (§ 1305.201(a), (f)) and municipal electrical work must follow local ordinances (§ 1305.201(d)).

### C. Plumbing — Occupations Code Ch. 1301 (TSBPE — NOT TDLR)

- [V] VERIFY-QUESTION ANSWERED: plumbing is still the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners in 2026. § 1301.003 (as
  amended 2021 by H.B. 636 and 2023 by S.B. 1659): "Unless continued in existence as provided by [the Sunset Act], the
  board is abolished and this chapter expires September 1, 2033." TSBPE survived the 2019 sunset scare and was
  continued; tsbpe.texas.gov is live and active (April 2026 announcements on the site). Do NOT write that plumbing
  moved to TDLR — it did not.
- [V] The homestead exemption, VERBATIM — § 1301.051: "PLUMBING BY PROPERTY OWNER IN HOMESTEAD. A property owner is
  not required to be licensed under this chapter to perform plumbing in the property owner's homestead." One sentence,
  no occupancy or resale conditions in the statute itself.
- [H] Scope of "homestead" for a house under construction: Texas homestead law can attach to land intended and
  prepared for homestead use before the house exists, but neither Ch. 1301 nor TSBPE rules define it for this
  exemption. Hedge: "own homestead" plainly covers your own home; for a new build on land you own and intend as your
  homestead the exemption is generally understood to apply, but confirm with TSBPE (tsbpe.texas.gov) before relying on
  it — and city permits/inspections apply regardless.
- [V] The rural exemption is NOT for owner-builders: § 1301.052 exempts unlicensed plumbing outside
  municipalities/small towns only for work "other than plumbing performed in conjunction with new construction,
  repair, or remodeling" on the listed rural properties. A new house is new construction — this exemption does not
  cover it. (The homestead exemption in § 1301.051 is the one that matters.)
- [V] Codes: the board adopts the Uniform Plumbing Code and International Plumbing Code (May 31, 2001 editions
  baseline) and "by rule may adopt later editions" (§ 1301.255(a), (b)); plumbing installed by licensees in areas not
  otherwise regulated must follow the board-adopted code (§ 1301.255(c)); municipalities may amend locally (§
  1301.255(d)).
- [H] Which UPC/IPC edition TSBPE currently enforces: board rules (22 TAC Ch. 361) adopt editions by rule and have
  been amended repeatedly; third-party sources say the 2021 editions. Verify the current edition in the TSBPE board
  rules PDF at tsbpe.texas.gov before printing an edition year.

### D. HVAC — Occupations Code Ch. 1302 (TDLR)

- [V] License required to engage in air conditioning and refrigeration contracting (§ 1302.251(a)); a state license is
  valid statewide and cities may not require a municipal license on top of it (§ 1302.251(b)).
- [V] The homeowner exemption, VERBATIM — § 1302.053: "HOMEOWNERS. This chapter does not apply to a person who engages
  in air conditioning and refrigeration contracting in a building owned solely by the person as the person's home."
- [H] "owned solely … as the person's home" — narrower words than the plumbing exemption. Flag two readings without
  resolving them: (i) jointly titled property ("owned solely by the person") and (ii) a new build not yet "the
  person's home." No TDLR published interpretation found. Hedge and advise confirming with TDLR for a new build.
- [V] Exempt work is still permit-able locally: "Work performed by a person who is exempt from this chapter is subject
  to any permit, inspection, or approval required by a municipal ordinance." (§ 1302.052)

### E. Water wells — Occupations Code Ch. 1901 (TDLR)

- [V] The own-property carve-out is built into the definition: "'Water well driller' … does not include a person who:
  (A) drills, bores, cores, or constructs a water well on the person's own property for the person's own use" (§
  1901.001(15)(A)). So a landowner may drill their own well without a TDLR license.
- [H] Groundwater conservation districts are the real gate: most of Texas is covered by a district (Water Code Ch. 36)
  and districts commonly require registration or a permit before ANY well is drilled, even exempt domestic wells, plus
  spacing rules. District-by-district; the kit must send the buyer to their district (lookup via TWDB/TCEQ district
  maps) before drilling.

### F. Municipal contractor registration and owner-builder ("homestead") permits

- [V] Fort Worth: anyone pulling building/mechanical/plumbing/electrical permits must hold a Development Services
  "Contractor Registration" (fortworthtexas.gov, Contractor Registration page) — and Fort Worth publishes "Homestead
  Permit Affidavit" forms by which the homeowner certifies they own and live at the address and will personally
  perform the work under a specific permit (Development Services "Homestead Affidavits" PDF — mechanical and plumbing
  versions).
- [V] San Antonio: contractors must register with Development Services before permitting; the city issues Class I /
  Class II "Home Improvement Contractor" licenses for residential work (sa.gov DSD Contractor pages). Homeowners
  "attesting that they own and will occupy or rent their residence for a period of 12 months after project completion
  can request a homeowner's permit … and will be responsible for all inspections" (sa.gov DSD Residential Permits
  page).
- [V] Houston: a homeowner may obtain the plumbing permit and do the work on a residence they "own, occupy, and have …
  registered as their homestead" — the Homeowner's Plumbing and Irrigation System Permit Application, form CE1284
  (houstonpermittingcenter.org) — but electrical permits issue only to licensed masters (see B above).
- [H] The label differs city to city ("homeowner permit," "homestead affidavit," "owner-builder"); some cities have no
  homeowner path for some trades. The kit's rule: ask the permit office two questions in writing — "may the
  owner-occupant pull this permit personally, and which trades are excluded?"

### G. The 2023–2025 preemption laws (context, lightly)

- [V] HB 2127 (2023), the "Texas Regulatory Consistency Act," preempts city regulation in fields occupied by several
  state codes (including the Occupations Code). A Travis County district court held it unconstitutional in 2023; the
  Third Court of Appeals reversed and upheld the law in July
  2025. [H] Its practical effect on trade permitting is still unsettled in 2026 — print no concrete "city X can no
        longer require Y" claim; express authorizations like § 1305.201 were not repealed.
- [V] SB 2038 (2023): landowners in most ETJ areas may petition for release from a city's ETJ (Local Gov't Code §
  42.102); if the city sits on a valid petition, "the area is released by operation of law" (§ 42.105(d)). 2025's HB
  2512 narrowed applicability (§ 42.101) — check exclusions first.
- [V] HB 3699 (2023) tightened municipal platting: cities must publish the complete plat-application checklist and Ch.
  212's plat trigger was narrowed (enrolled bill analysis, capitol.texas.gov). Relevant at the "is my lot legally
  platted / must it be?" step in TRACK A and the ETJ.

## TX.2 — Permit application checklist (two tracks)

### TRACK A — lot inside a city's limits

- [V] The statutory floor: IRC "as it existed on May 1, 2012" is "adopted as a municipal residential building code in
  this state" and "applies to all construction, alteration, remodeling, enlargement, and repair of residential
  structures in a municipality" (Local Gov't Code § 214.212(a), (b)). Cities may adopt local amendments only after a
  public hearing and by ordinance (§ 214.212(c), (e)), and may adopt post-2012 ICC editions (§ 214.212(d)). Cities
  over 100,000 must post notice 21 days before adopting/amending a model code (§ 214.217).
- [H] Which IRC edition YOUR city enforces is local (Houston: 2021 codes family per Houston Permitting Center; others
  vary). The kit prints the verify step, not a table of editions that will rot.
- [V] The 45-day shot clock, printable: the city must grant, deny, give written reasons, or reach a written agreement
  within 45 days of a permit application, and must refund fees if it blows the extended deadline (§ 214.904).
- Common checklist shape (each item [V] as to the named city's published requirements; [H] as a universal):
  - Application in the city's portal — Houston: iPermits/ProjectDox; San Antonio: BuildSA; Fort Worth: Accela; Dallas:
    DallasNow (May 2025).
  - Plan content: site/plot plan with setbacks, foundation plan (engineered in the expansive-soil metros — [H]
    city-variable but near-universal), floor plans, elevations, wall sections, window/door schedule.
  - Energy-code compliance docs — cities administer and enforce the energy code and report to SECO (Health & Safety
    Code § 388.003(c)).
  - Contractor registration numbers for the GC (you, if the city allows an owner-builder) and each trade;
    homeowner/homestead affidavits where the city allows the owner to pull trade permits (TX.1.F).
  - Plat/legal-lot verification; replat before permit if not legally platted (Ch. 212; HB 3699 publication duty).
  - Floodplain development permit/elevation certificate if in an SFHA — cities must maintain NFIP-eligibility
    ordinances (Water Code § 16.3145).
  - Driveway/curb cut per city standard; tree rules (San Antonio requires a tree inspection before CO — sa.gov).

### TRACK B — lot in the unincorporated county

THE FRAME (print first): [V] no county building permit exists for a single-family house — county fire-code permits
expressly apply only to commercial, public, and 4+ unit buildings (Local Gov't Code § 233.062(a), § 233.063), and
Subchapter F, where adopted, "may not be construed to … require prior approval by the county" (§ 233.153(d)(1)). What
follows is the list of things that ARE required.

1. SEPTIC (OSSF) — the permit that functions as the de facto building permit:
   - [V] "A person must hold a permit and an approved plan to construct, alter, repair, extend, or operate an on-site
     sewage disposal system." (Health & Safety Code § 366.051(a)) Issued by the local "authorized agent" (usually the
     county) or by TCEQ where no agent exists (§ 366.051(b)); rules at 30 TAC Ch. 285; site evaluation first;
     authorized-agent lookup map on tceq.texas.gov.
   - [V] The 10-acre exemption, precisely: permitting sections do not apply to an OSSF "of a single residence that is
     located on a land tract that is 10 acres or larger in which the field line or sewage disposal line is not closer
     than 100 feet of the property line," with effluent retained on-site, no nuisance, no groundwater pollution (§
     366.052(a), (b)).
   - [V] TCEQ's application form where TCEQ is the permitting authority: TCEQ Form 0235 (tceq.texas.gov). Counties use
     their own packets.
   - [V] Aerobic-system owners: maintenance obligations under § 366.0515 (owner may self-maintain, with penalties for
     violations in counties ≥ 40,000 population — § 366.0515(g)).

2. FLOODPLAIN — county development permit:
   - [V] Every county must adopt NFIP-eligibility orders (Water Code § 16.3145) and may enforce floodplain management
     rules (§ 16.315); violating the subchapter is a Class C misdemeanor, each day a separate offense (§ 16.3221).
     Practically: if any part of the site is in an SFHA, a county floodplain development permit + elevation
     certificate before construction. Example: Montgomery County requires a development permit for ALL land
     development, in and outside the floodplain (mctx.org Environmental Health, Residential Development Permit pages).

3. ENERGY CODE — yes, even here (the fact that surprises everyone):
   - [V] The state energy code for single-family construction is the energy efficiency chapter (Ch. 11) of the IRC;
     SECO adopted the 2015 edition effective September 1, 2016, and it remains the statewide standard as of August
     2026 (SECO single-family page, comptroller.texas.gov; Health & Safety Code § 388.003(a)). SECO may adopt the
     latest edition no more than once every six years, with a ≥ 9-month lead and a cost analysis (§ 388.003(a), (a-1),
     as amended by S.B. 783, 2025).
   - [H] SECO opened a public-input process on newer editions in late 2025 (Texas Register, Oct. 24, 2025 "In
     Addition"); an adoption could change the edition during the kit's life. Print "2015 IRC Chapter 11 as of August
     2026 — check SECO's page."
   - [V] Enforcement outside cities, VERBATIM (§ 388.004(a)): "For construction outside of the local jurisdiction of a
     municipality: (1) a building certified by a national, state, or local accredited energy efficiency program shall
     be considered in compliance; (2) a building with inspections from private code-certified inspectors using the
     energy efficiency chapter of the International Residential Code or International Energy Conservation Code shall
     be considered in compliance; and (3) a builder who does not have access to either of the above methods for a
     building shall certify compliance using a form provided by the laboratory, enumerating the code-compliance
     features of the building."
   - [V] And the retention duty (§ 388.004(b)): the builder keeps the original compliance documentation until the
     third anniversary and gives the owner a copy. "The laboratory" = the Energy Systems Laboratory at Texas A&M
     (esl.tamu.edu), which publishes the builder self-certification form for unincorporated areas.
   - [V] The ERI compliance path with declining scores (ERI ≤ 57/57/61 by climate zone on or after Sept. 1, 2025) is
     in § 388.003(j).

4. WINDSTORM — only in the designated catastrophe area:
   - [V] The area: the 14 first-tier coastal counties — Aransas, Brazoria, Calhoun, Cameron, Chambers, Galveston,
     Jefferson, Kenedy, Kleberg, Matagorda, Nueces, Refugio, San Patricio, Willacy — plus parts of Harris County east
     of SH 146 (La Porte, Morgan's Point, Pasadena, Seabrook, Shoreacres) (TDI windstorm pages, tdi.texas.gov/wind).
   - [V] Notice BEFORE construction, VERBATIM (Insurance Code § 2210.2515(b)): "A person shall provide written notice
     on a form prescribed by and submitted to the department of the person's intent to construct … a structure for
     which the person is seeking coverage under this chapter before the person begins to construct … the structure."
     TDI's form for this is the WPI-1 Application for Certificate of Compliance.
   - [V] Why it matters, VERBATIM (§ 2210.251(a)): "to be considered insurable property eligible for windstorm and
     hail insurance coverage from the association [TWIA], a structure that is constructed … on or after January 1,
     1988, must comply with the plan of operation"; the certificate of compliance under § 2210.2515 "is evidence of
     insurability of the structure by the association" (§ 2210.251(g)).
   - [V] Two certificate routes (§ 2210.2515(c), (d)): completed improvement via a licensed professional engineer's
     sealed design/affirmation or post-construction evaluation, or ongoing improvement via a TDI-appointed qualified
     inspector inspecting during construction; TDI issues WPI-8/WPI-8-E certificates (searchable on tdi.texas.gov).
     Skipping this and trying to buy TWIA later is the classic coastal owner-builder disaster.

5. DRIVEWAY:
   - [V] Onto a STATE-system highway (including FM roads): TxDOT permit required before constructing an access
     driveway (43 TAC § 11.52); application on TxDOT Form 1058, "Permit to Construct Access Driveway Facilities on
     Highway Right of Way," filed with the local TxDOT district/area office; no work in the right of way until the
     executed permit is in hand with 24-hour notice to TxDOT.
   - [H] Onto a COUNTY road: many counties require a driveway/culvert permit through the county engineer or road &
     bridge department (Comal County's Engineer's Office lists it among its standard permits). County-variable —
     verify with the county road department.

6. WELL: [V] landowner may drill on own property for own use without a TDLR license (Occupations Code §
   1901.001(15)(A)); [H] groundwater conservation district registration/permit and spacing rules likely apply — check
   the local district first (TX.1.E).

7. COUNTY BUILDING STANDARDS WHERE ADOPTED (Local Gov't Code Ch. 233, Subchapter F):
   - [V] Opt-in only: the subchapter applies "only to a county that has adopted a resolution or order requiring the
     application of the provisions of this subchapter" (§ 233.152).
   - [V] Where adopted, new single-family construction must conform to the IRC as of May 1, 2008 or the county seat's
     edition (§ 233.153(a), as amended 2025 by H.B. 3680); the BUILDER (that's the owner-builder) hires one of six
     listed inspector types (licensed engineer, registered architect, TREC inspector, municipal plumbing inspector,
     political- subdivision building inspector, or ICC-certified residential combination inspector) for a minimum of
     three inspections — foundation before concrete, framing/mechanical before drywall, and final (§ 233.154(a));
     county notice forms before starting and within 10 days after final if the county requires them (§ 233.154(b),
     (c)); enforcement is by injunction/misdemeanor, not permit denial (§§ 233.155, 233.157), and no county fee is
     allowed (§ 233.153(f)).
   - [V] Example of an opted-in county: El Paso County's residential inspection program under Subchapter F, effective
     January 1, 2013 (epcounty.com Public Works). [H] No statewide roster of opted-in counties exists — the kit's
     verify step is one call to the county: "has the commissioners court adopted a resolution under Local Government
     Code chapter 233, subchapter F?"

8. TRADES STILL LICENSED OUT HERE: [V] electrical work must meet the state electrical code (2023 NEC) even in
   unincorporated areas (§ 1305.201(e)); plumbing by licensees must follow the board-adopted code (§ 1301.255(c)); the
   homeowner exemptions of TX.1 are the only unlicensed path, with the hedges noted there.

### The lien/homestead block (Texas-specific, load-bearing — goes in TX.2)

- [V] Constitutional baseline (Tex. Const. art. XVI § 50(a)(5)): the homestead is protected from forced sale except,
  among others, for "work and material used in constructing new improvements thereon, if contracted for in writing, or
  work and material used to repair or renovate existing improvements thereon if" the (A)–(D) conditions are met (both
  spouses' consent, 5-day wait after credit application, 3-day rescission, closing at a lender/attorney/title office).
  NOTE THE STRUCTURE: for NEW improvements the constitution requires the written contract; the (A)–(D) conditions
  attach to repair/renovation of EXISTING improvements. Print this distinction exactly — most summaries get it wrong.
- [V] Statutory mechanics (Property Code § 53.254): to fix a lien on a homestead the contract must be written,
  "executed before the material is furnished or the labor is performed," signed by both spouses if married, and filed
  with the county clerk; lien affidavits against a homestead must carry the 10-point bold notice "THIS IS NOT A LIEN…"
  (§ 53.254(f)), and subcontractor notices must include the statutory warning about the owner's 10 percent retainage
  (§ 53.254(g)).
- Practical translation for the owner-builder hiring subs on their future homestead: [V] a sub or supplier who wants
  lien rights needs that recorded pre-work written contract; [H] whether a not-yet-occupied house is already
  "homestead" for lien purposes is fact-dependent (intent + preparation can establish homestead before occupancy) —
  the kit tells the buyer to behave as if homestead rules apply (written contracts, both spouses, before work, every
  trade) and to reserve 10 percent retainage for 30 days after completion (the duty reflected in the § 53.254(g)
  notice).
- Selling later:
  - [V] The seller's-disclosure statute (Property Code § 5.008) does not apply to a transfer "of a new residence of
    not more than one dwelling unit which has not previously been occupied for residential purposes" (§ 5.008(e)).
  - [V]/[H] Implied warranties: a builder-vendor selling a new house gives common-law implied warranties of good
    workmanship and habitability (Humber v. Morton, 426 S.W.2d 554 (Tex. 1968); habitability is effectively
    non-waivable except for known defects, Centex Homes v. Buecher, 95 S.W.3d 266 (Tex. 2002)). [H] Whether a one-off
    owner-builder who later sells is a "builder-vendor" is fact-specific — hedge: an owner-builder who sells soon
    after completion risks being treated as one; there is no statutory new-home warranty in Texas (the TRCC scheme
    died in 2009).

## TX.3 — Inspection sequence

### Municipal track (typical IRC ladder, citing published city lists)

- [V] San Antonio publishes a Residential Inspection Guide and diagram (docsonline.sanantonio.gov): inspections at
  each covered stage, and "for residential projects to be considered completed, final inspections required include
  Building, Electrical, Mechanical, Gas, Plumbing and a Tree Inspection" before the Certificate of Occupancy and
  utility transfer.
- [V] Fort Worth publishes a General Inspections List (fortworthtexas.gov): foundation approved with steel in place
  before concrete; framing called only after trades rough-ins; final inspection "finals out" all permits and supports
  the CO.
- [H] The generic ladder to print (labelled as typical, verify locally): T-pole/temporary power → plumbing rough
  (underground, before slab) → foundation/pre-pour → framing + electrical/plumbing/mechanical rough + windstorm
  nailing where applicable → insulation/energy → wallboard (some cities) → gas test → sewer/water yard lines → trade
  finals → building final → CO. Exact names and count vary by city.
- [V] CO in cities: San Antonio requires the CO application after all finals (sa.gov); Fort Worth issues the CO at
  final-out (fortworthtexas.gov).

### County track (there is no ladder — there are five separate inspectors)

- [V] OSSF: the authorized agent (county) inspects the septic installation under 30 TAC Ch. 285 before the system is
  covered/used; construction without the permit and approved plan is unlawful (Health & Safety Code §§ 366.004,
  366.051).
- [V] Electrical: NOBODY inspects by default. The licensing law still applies and the work must meet the 2023 NEC (§
  1305.201(e)), but no state agency performs residential electrical inspections in unincorporated areas — TDLR
  licenses people, not houses. The practical inspectors are the utility (below), the lender, and the insurer. Print
  exactly this: "licensing applies; routine state inspection does not exist."
- [V] Windstorm (coastal): TDI/engineer inspections at each construction phase after the WPI-1 notice; contact TDI for
  each phase when TDI is the inspector (tdi.texas.gov/wind inspection-process page); certificate WPI-8/WPI-8-E at the
  end (Insurance Code § 2210.2515).
- [V] Energy: the § 388.004 path — accredited program (e.g., ENERGY STAR / ERI rating), private code-certified
  inspector, or the ESL builder self-certification form; keep records three years (§ 388.004(b)).
- [V] Subchapter F counties: the three private inspections (foundation pre-pour, framing/mechanical pre-drywall,
  completion) arranged and paid for by the builder, with county notices (§ 233.154) — El Paso County program as the
  worked example.
- [V] Utility meter release as de facto final: electric cooperatives require a passing meter-loop inspection before
  energizing new construction — example policy: Bluebonnet Electric Cooperative's tariff requires an inspection of the
  meter loop "for all new and existing locations," with a $125 re-inspection fee if it fails (bluebonnet.coop tariff,
  Section III). [H] Requirements differ by utility — some co-ops require a licensed electrician's certification or a
  third-party inspection; the kit's step is "call your electric provider's new-construction desk before you wire."
- [H] Lender/insurer inspections: construction-loan draw inspections and the insurer's replacement-cost/4-point style
  requirements are, in practice, the only whole-house quality gates in the county track. Frame as practical reality,
  not law.
- [V] No CO exists in unincorporated areas — no statute creates one; the occupancy gates are the OSSF approval (can't
  lawfully use the septic without the permit — § 366.051), the meter release, and (coastal) the windstorm certificate
  for insurance.

## Open questions / hedges (the complete list, for the doc writers)

1. Homeowner ELECTRICAL exemption on a new build: § 1305.003(a)(6) requires owning AND residing in the dwelling; a
   house under construction is arguably neither. No TDLR interpretation found. Every TX doc that touches owner-wiring
   must carry this hedge. (Biggest legal-risk item in the kit.)
2. Homeowner HVAC ("owned solely … as the person's home" — joint title, new build) and plumbing ("homestead" scope
   for a not-yet-occupied new build) exemptions — unresolved edges; hedge identically, verify with TDLR/TSBPE.
3. Current UPC/IPC edition enforced by TSBPE board rule — verify at tsbpe.texas.gov before printing an edition year.
4. SECO may adopt a post-2015 energy code edition during the kit's shelf life (public-input process began Oct. 2025;
   ≥ 9-month lead time built into § 388.003). Print "as of August 2026" language.
5. No statewide roster of counties opted into LG Code Ch. 233 Subchapter F — kit instructs a direct county question.
   (El Paso County verified as opted-in; the 2025 HB 3680 amendments may drive new adoptions.)
6. HB 2127's practical reach is still being litigated/applied in 2026 — print no concrete permitting consequences.
7. Local-variable items — always "verify locally": each city's IRC edition; county driveway/culvert permits on county
   roads; groundwater district well rules (check before drilling even an exempt well); utility meter-release
   requirements (Bluebonnet tariff is the verified printed example).
8. WPI-3's current status at TDI — verify before listing it as a live form.
9. Whether an owner-builder who sells shortly after completion owes Humber/Buecher implied warranties as a
   "builder-vendor" — fact-specific; frame as risk, not rule.
10. Houston general-contractor registration requirements (as opposed to trade permitting) — conflicting secondary
    sources; confirm at houstonpermittingcenter.org before printing.
11. Proving the no-GC-license negative: cite TDLR's program list + TRCC expiration, dated "as of August 2026" — never
    as a timeless absolute.
