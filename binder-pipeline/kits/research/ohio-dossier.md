## THE OHIO THESIS, IN FOUR SENTENCES

1. Ohio writes **one residential code for the whole state** — the Residential
   Code of Ohio, OAC Chapter 4101:8 — and applies it by its own terms to
   "every one-, two-, or three-family dwelling."
2. **Enforcement is optional and applied for.** A municipality, township or
   county obtains certification from the Board of Building Standards; the Board
   may not require residential certification of a department that does not
   enforce the residential code.
3. **Where no residential-certified department has jurisdiction, the code itself
   excuses you** from submitting construction documents, seeking approvals,
   requesting inspections and obtaining a certificate of occupancy — and the
   statute says the same thing independently.
4. **The state's trade licenses do not reach your house either.** Ohio licenses
   five trades, but only on a "construction project," which is defined to
   exclude a residential building. The residential licensing question is
   entirely local.

The distinctive Ohio move, versus Arkansas or Michigan, is that **both halves of
this are written down explicitly** rather than inferred from permissive verbs.

## 1. LICENSING — THE DEFINITION CHAIN

### 1.1 No state general contractor license [V]

Ohio's only state construction-licensing chapter is R.C. Chapter 4740. It
creates no general contractor, builder, roofer, framer, concrete, drywall or
excavation license. R.C. 4740.14 refers to "general contractors" as a real
occupational category while licensing none of them.

### 1.2 The four-link chain that excludes your house [V]

This is the kit's headline finding and every link is checkable at
codes.ohio.gov in about five minutes.

| # | Section | Text |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | R.C. 4740.13(A) | "No person shall act as or claim to be **a type of contractor that this chapter licenses** unless that person holds… a license issued pursuant to this chapter." |
| 2 | R.C. 4740.01(A), (D) | A license is issued as a "**heating, ventilating, and air conditioning contractor, refrigeration contractor, electrical contractor, plumbing contractor, or hydronics contractor**." Five trades. |
| 3 | R.C. 4740.01(B), (F) | Each is defined only by work on a "construction project" — and "'Construction project' means a construction project involving a building or structure subject to Chapter 3781… **but not an industrialized unit or a residential building as defined in section 3781.06**." |
| 4 | R.C. 3781.06(C)(9) | "'Residential building' means a **one-family, two-family, or three-family dwelling house, and any accessory structure incidental to that dwelling house**." |

**Consequence [V]:** the Ohio electrical, plumbing, HVAC, refrigeration and
hydronics licenses are commercial licenses. As a matter of state law they are
not required for work on a 1-, 2- or 3-family dwelling.

**Corroborated by the agency itself [V].** The Ohio Construction Industry
Licensing Board's own page: *"The Licensing Board regulates **commercial**
contractors who perform the above five specialty trades… **All local building
and health departments regulate residential contractors.** However, the State
Licensing Law allows those building and health departments to require the State
commercial license for their residential contractors."*

### 1.3 Local regulation is expressly preserved [V]

R.C. 4740.12(B): nothing in the chapter limits any local ordinance that
"regulates the installation, repair, maintenance, or alteration of plumbing
systems, hydronics systems, electrical systems, heating, ventilating, and air
conditioning systems, or refrigeration systems" or "requires the registration
and assessment of a registration or license fee of tradespersons."

Penalty for unlicensed commercial contracting: R.C. 4740.99 — minor misdemeanor
first violation, fourth-degree misdemeanor thereafter. Carve-outs at R.C.
4740.13(D): control wiring under 25 volts; fire/burglar alarm, cabling,
tele-data, sound, communication, landscape lighting and irrigation under 50
volts.

### 1.4 Contracts — R.C. Chapter 4722 [V]

Imposes **nothing** on an owner-builder: a "supplier" contracts with an owner
"for compensation," and you do not contract with yourself. It regulates
everyone you hire, at **$25,000 or more**.

- 4722.02(A) — written contract, nine mandatory contents, including the
  supplier's certificate of insurance showing ≥ $250,000 general liability.
- 4722.02(B) — change-order estimate once unforeseen excess costs cumulatively
  exceed $5,000.
- 4722.04 — deposit capped at 10% of contract price (75% for non-returnable
  special-order items).
- 4722.03(A) — eleven prohibited practices.
- 4722.08(A) — rescission, or actual economic damages plus up to $5,000
  noneconomic. Fees only for a **knowing** violation.

**The cost-plus trap [V].** All of it evaporates on a cost-plus contract:
4722.02(C) (written contract and change orders), 4722.03(B) (excess costs),
4722.04 (deposit cap) each carve cost-plus out expressly.

**CSPA inversion [V].** R.C. 1345.01(A) excludes "transactions involving a home
construction service contract as defined in section 4722.01" from the Consumer
Sales Practices Act. The bigger the job, the narrower the remedy.

*Drafting note:* 4722.01(C) says "exceeding twenty-five thousand dollars" and
4722.02(A) says "equals or exceeds." Kit prints "$25,000 or more" and hangs
nothing on the edge case.

### 1.5 Liens — R.C. Chapter 1311 [V]

- **No Notice of Commencement** for a home construction contract — 1311.04(O),
  and 1311.05(E) excludes notices of furnishing too. Lenders often require one
  anyway, which 1311.04(O) expressly contemplates.
- **1311.011(B)(1) — the payment defense.** Paid the original contractor in full
  before receiving a copy of a lien affidavit? Subs' and suppliers' liens are
  "void and the property wholly discharged."
- **(B)(2)** — partial payment caps all liens at the unpaid balance, minus the
  cost to complete.
- **(B)(3)** — a lienholder who fails to release within 30 days of written
  notice of full payment "is liable to the owner… for all damages," including
  court costs and attorney fees.
- **(B)(6)** — you may withhold payment pending a sworn affidavit that everyone
  below is paid, and your rights are not prejudiced by not asking.
- **(B)(7), (B)(9)** — joint checks blessed; releases valid without separate
  consideration.
- **1311.06(B)(1)** — lien affidavit within **60 days** for a one- or two-family
  dwelling; **(B)(3)** — **75 days** residual.

### 1.6 ★ THE THREE-FAMILY SEAM [V]

Ohio uses "one-, two-, or three-family" as its unit in the building code
(3781.06(C)(9)), the trade licenses (4740.01(F)) and the contract act
(4722.01(F)). **Chapter 1311 does not.** The payment defense reaches a "single-
or double-family dwelling" (1311.011(A)(1)) and the 60-day window reaches "a
one- or two-family dwelling" (1311.06(B)(1)). A three-family dwelling therefore
gets **neither** — 75 days, and no payment defense. Printed prominently in OH.1
and OH.5.

## 2. THE CODE AND ITS ENFORCEMENT

### 2.1 The RCO's identity [V]

OAC 4101:8-1-01 §101.1: *"Chapters 4101:8-1 to 4101:8-25, 4101:8-29, 4101:8-34,
and 4101:8-44… are designated as the 'Residential Code of Ohio for One-, Two-,
and Three-Family Dwellings'… **The 2018 edition of the 'International
Residential Code', first printing, Chapters 2 through 24, 29, and 44**… is used
as the basis of this document as is incorporated fully except as modified in
italic herein."*

Effective **1 July 2019**. Amendments live: Foundations **1 March 2024**;
Electrical and Referenced Standards **15 April 2024**.

**No appendices were adopted** — which is why the RCO has no radon provisions.

### 2.2 ★ THE NEC EDITION — RESOLVED [V]

**The current residential electrical standard in Ohio is the 2023 National
Electrical Code (NFPA 70—2023), effective 15 April 2024.**

- OAC 4101:8-34-01 §3401.1 incorporates NFPA 70 **wholesale** — Ohio has no
  IRC-style electrical chapters at all — then applies eight amendments.
- OAC 4101:8-44-01 pins the edition. On printed page 31 the NFPA table reads
  **70—~~17~~<u>23</u>**: the 17 struck through, the 23 underlined.

**Why every prior pass hedged, and why this matters beyond Ohio.** The Board
files its rules as PDFs of the *amendment*, with deletions struck and additions
underlined on the same line. Any text extraction flattens that to **"70—1723"**,
which a careful reader then resolves to 2017 because 17 comes first. The finding
required rendering the page to an image and reading it. Both this builder and
the codes research agent confirmed it independently and visually.

Internal corroboration [V]: Ohio's amendments modify NEC 215.18 and 225.42,
sections that **do not exist in the 2017 NEC**, and 210.8(A) is enumerated
"(A)(1) through (A)(12)," a count reached only in 2023.

**Energy did not move.** On page 29 of the same rule, `IECC—18` carries **no
strikethrough**. Residential energy remains the 2018 IECC.

### 2.3 Ohio's amendments to the 2023 NEC [V]

Eight, from OAC 4101:8-34-01. The ones that matter to a budget:

- **215.18(A), 225.42(A), 230.67(A)** rewritten to "**Where provided as part
  of**" — converting the 2023 NEC's mandatory whole-house surge protection into
  a conditional. **Ohio does not require an SPD.**
- **210.8(F) Exception No. 2** — "GFCI protection is not required for listed
  HVAC equipment." (Outdoor condensers.)
- **210.64 Exception No. 1** — no service-equipment receptacle required in 1-,
  2- or 3-family dwellings.
- **210.12(A) Exception No. 2** — kitchen-countertop-only circuits need no AFCI.
- **210.8(A)(5)** — sump-pump single receptacle exempt where a GFCI duplex is
  within 6 ft; **210.8(A)(2)** — ceiling garage-door-opener receptacle excepted.
- **210.8(D) deleted**; **422.5 item (6) deleted**.
- §3401.1 — any NEC reference to "one- and two-family dwellings" includes
  three-family; where the NEC names a one- or two-family dwelling specifically,
  a three-family dwelling is regulated as multi-family.

### 2.5 Plumbing — a separate code on a newer cycle [V]

RCO §2501.1 replaces the model code's plumbing chapters with a pointer to the
**Ohio Plumbing Code**, with one Ohio change (shower pan lining, §2501.1.1).

**The April 2024 amendment unpinned the edition.** The referenced-codes table's
pin — "codified and published as the 2017 Ohio Plumbing Code, effective
11-1-2017" — is struck through in the filed rule. The Ohio Plumbing Code now in
effect is OAC 4101:3, **effective 15 October 2025, based on the 2021
International Plumbing Code**. So residential plumbing runs on a *newer* model
cycle than the rest of the house.

**Enforcement is a separate track [V].** R.C. 3781.03(C) names four possible
enforcers — the Division of Industrial Compliance, boards of health of health
districts, certified municipal building-inspection departments, and county
building departments under an R.C. 3703.01(C)(1) contract. Neither the state
division nor a health district needs a building certification to act. In the
Board's own dataset the named plumbing entity is a **county health district in
roughly two thirds** of residential-certified jurisdictions.

### 2.6 ★ THE ENFORCEMENT GAP, IN TWO TEXTS [V]

**OAC 4101:8-1-01 §101.5:** *"Jurisdiction without a certified residential
building department. If no municipal, township, or county building department is
certified by the Board of Building Standards for residential buildings in
accordance with section 3781.10(E) of the Revised Code has jurisdiction, **the
owner is not required to make submission of construction documents, seek
approvals, request inspections, or obtain certificates of occupancy required in
this Chapter**."* (Grammar is the state's, quoted as filed.)

**R.C. 3791.04(A)(1)** — the same rule in statute, and the asymmetry:
- (a) no **nonresidential**-certified department → submissions go to the
  **superintendent of industrial compliance**.
- (b) no **residential**-certified department → "**the owner is not required to
  make the submissions**."

Ohio built a state backstop for commercial work and deliberately did not build
one for houses.

### 2.7 Certification is voluntary, and comes in three flavors [V]

- R.C. 3781.10(E)(10) — "Certification shall be granted **upon application** by
  the municipal corporation, the board of township trustees, or the board of
  county commissioners."
- R.C. 3781.10(E)(3) — the Board "shall not require a building department… to be
  certified for residential building code enforcement if that building
  department does not enforce the state residential building code."
- R.C. 3781.10(E)(2) — nonresidential; residential new construction;
  residential repair and alteration. (E)(8) — jurisdiction extends "only with
  respect to the types of buildings and subject matters for which they are
  certified."
- R.C. 3781.10(E)(7) — enforcement may be contracted out, including to the state
  Division of Industrial Compliance under R.C. 121.083(B).
- R.C. 3781.10(E)(15) — third-party examiners are permitted; their fees are "the
  responsibility of the building owner"; issuing the certificate of plan
  approval and the certificate of occupancy "remains the exclusive authority" of
  the certified department.

**Refuted [V]:** the widely repeated claim that municipalities must enforce
while counties and townships may opt out. R.C. 3781.10(E)(10) treats all three
layers identically, and 446 of 921 municipal records are not
residential-certified.

### 2.9 Permits, timelines and protections [V]

- **R.C. 3791.04(A)(2)(b)** — "No seal is required for any plans… submitted for
  approval for **any residential buildings**."
- **R.C. 3791.04(E)** — plan approval is a "license"; failure to approve "within
  thirty days" is "an adjudication order denying the issuance of a license"
  requiring a hearing; a denial "shall specify the reasons."
- **R.C. 3791.04(D)** — approved plans are "conclusively presumed to comply"
  absent fraud or a serious safety or sanitation hazard, and lock you to "any
  rule in effect at the time of approval." Also authorizes foundation-only and
  partial approvals.
- **R.C. 3791.04(G)** — conditional approval to proceed to the point of the
  objection, where the objection is a conflicting interpretation.
- **R.C. 3791.04(C)** — approval invalid if work has not commenced within 12
  months, or is suspended more than 6 months. One 12-month and two 6-month
  extensions, each requested **at least 10 days before expiry**.
- **R.C. 3791.04(J)** — fine not more than $500 for building without approval.
- **RCO §108.1** — "**failure of the inspectors to inspect the work within four
  days**, exclusive of Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, after the work is
  ready for inspection, **allows the work to proceed**."
- **Ohio calls it "approval," not "permit"** — the RCO issues a *certificate of
  plan approval* (§105.5).

### 2.10 Required inspections — RCO §108.2 [V]

The official must hand the owner a project-specific list at plan approval,
drawn from §§108.2.1–108.2.12.

- **108.2.1 Lot line markers** — "**Before any work is started**… all boundary
  lines shall be clearly marked at their intersections with permanent markers or
  with markers which are offset at a distance which is of record with the
  owner." A survey requirement, first on the list.
- 108.2.2 footing/foundation · 108.2.3 slab and under-floor (before concrete or
  subfloor) · 108.2.4 lowest floor elevation · **108.2.5 frame — after "the
  rough electrical, plumbing, heating wires, pipes and ducts **are approved**"**
  · 108.2.6 lath/gypsum before taping · 108.2.7 fire-resistant penetrations ·
  108.2.8 energy · 108.2.9 building service equipment · 108.2.10 other.
- 108.2.11 — findings noted on an **on-site inspection record**; the official
  then issues the certificate of occupancy under §111.

**The sequencing trap:** in Ohio the rough trades clear **before** framing does.

### 2.11 Appeals [V]

Local board of building appeals (RCO §110.1); the 30-day deemed denial
(R.C. 3791.04(E)); and R.C. 3781.01(B)–(D) — a local authority may add
residential rules only if non-conflicting, and **any person** may ask the Board
of Building Standards to rule on a conflict, which it must do within **60 days**;
a conflicting rule not necessary for health or safety "is not valid and the
local governing authority may not enforce" it.

Also R.C. 307.37 (county building code, amended 9 April 2025) builds a drainage
review into the county permit application: if not completed within thirty days,
the construction "shall be deemed to have no adverse effects on existing surface
or subsurface drainage."

### 2.12 Agricultural exemption [V]

R.C. 3781.06(B)(1) exempts "buildings or structures that are incident to the use
for agricultural purposes of the land on which [they] are located, provided
those buildings or structures are not used in the business of retail trade"
(50% test on gross income from own-farm products). "Agricultural purposes" is
broad (3781.06(C)(1)). **It never reaches the dwelling.**

Note the tension: R.C. 3781.06(C)(11) defines "accessory structure" as one
"**attached to** a residential building," while RCO §102.10 exempts *detached*
accessory structures ≤ 200 sq ft. Unresolved; the kit sends the reader to ask.

## 3. SEWAGE AND PRIVATE WATER

### 3.1 Household sewage treatment systems — OAC Chapter 3701-29 [V]

Authority R.C. Chapter 3718; rules effective **1 January 2015** (3701-29-13
effective 30 September 2019). Permits issued by the **local board of health**;
boards may adopt **more stringent** rules (R.C. 3718.02(B); OAC 3701-29-22), so
every number is a statewide floor.

- **3701-29-06(D)** — an HSTS "shall serve only a one family, two-family, or
  three-family dwelling."
- **Soil evaluation — 3701-29-07(A)** — only a Soil Science Society of America
  certified soil scientist or associated professional, an equivalently
  registered soil professional, a registered sanitarian employed by the board of
  health, or a person under an approved certification program. **The homeowner
  may not do their own.** The owner may hire independently rather than use the
  district (3701-29-07(B)).
- **Design — 3701-29-10(A)** — no professional engineer required and no
  registered-designer credential exists; the test is functional. The designer
  must visit the site and stake or flag the absorption area (3701-29-10(C)), and
  the site drawing must show isolation distances met by **both** the system and
  the replacement area (3701-29-10(C)(9)).
- **★ Installation — 3701-29-09(D) and 3701-29-03(H).** Installation "shall only
  be conducted by a **registered installer**." The homeowner provision is one
  sentence: *"When the registered installer performs the duties of an installer
  on only the registrant's personal residence, the board of health **may** waive
  paragraphs (C)(1), (C)(4), and (C)(6) of this rule."* The subject is *the
  registered installer* — **there is no blanket homeowner exemption.** Waivable:
  fee, $500,000 general liability, surety bond. **Not** waivable: ODH testing
  requirements (C)(2), system-specific training (C)(3), continuing education
  (C)(5). And "may," not "shall."
- **Operation permit — 3701-29-09(I)(4)** — term set by the board of health,
  **not to exceed ten years**. It is *not* an annual permit by state rule.
- **3701-29-09(H)** — the board re-inspects "not later than twelve months after
  the approval of the installation."
- **3701-29-09(F)** — the installer must provide a signed as-built drawing.
- **★ Replacement area — 3701-29-06(G)(1)** — every new system needs a *second*
  fully compliant area, meeting every isolation distance, identified at design,
  staked, and protected. A board of health **may not waive it** for new lots.
- **No statewide minimum lot size** [V by exhaustive read of 3701-29-08].

### 3.2 Private water systems — OAC Chapter 3701-28 [V]

Authority R.C. 3701.344; rules effective **1 January 2020**. Permits from the
**local board of health**.

- **3701-28-03(A)** — "no work shall commence until a valid permit has been
  issued and approved." Review clock **10 business days**, or 15 where plans are
  required. A **pre-construction site review** with the district to confirm
  isolation distances is mandatory (3701-28-03(A)(3)(a)).
- **3701-28-03(B)** — the application's site plan must show distances from
  roadways, rights-of-way, buildings, driveways, sewage systems, sewers,
  existing and sealed wells, oil and gas wells, fuel and chemical tanks,
  streams, lakes, ponds, ditches, leaching pits, privies, manure ponds, lagoons
  and piles, lot lines and easements.
- **3701-28-03(J)(3)** — the emergency exception is unavailable "at locations
  where there are currently no existing or partially constructed structures" —
  so an owner-builder on raw land cannot use it.
- **★ Owner work splits in two [V].** Non-drilling work (pump, pressure tank,
  distribution, treatment, spring, pond, hauled-water tank): "shall obtain a
  registration to perform work, but are **exempt from the bonding and business
  liability insurance requirements**" (3701-28-18(A)(3)). **Drilling**: "shall
  obtain a registration… but are **exempt from the business liability insurance
  requirements**" — and nothing else (3701-28-18(A)(4)). **The surety bond still
  applies to an owner-driller**, and a new registrant receives a mandatory ODH
  field assessment during construction (3701-28-04(G)).

*Fee and bond figures appear in the research files but are deliberately not
printed in the kit — they are set by rule and change.*

## 4. SITE PLAN STUDIO EXTRACTION

For `src/lib/siteplan/rules.ts`. **Ohio qualifies as a real-rules state.** Every
number below is verbatim from the OAC and every one is a **statewide minimum
that the local health district may increase** (OAC 3701-29-22).

### 4.1 Structural note — Ohio's septic setbacks are grouped, not tabular

Unlike most states, Ohio does not publish a row-per-feature septic table. The
entire statewide scheme is four paragraphs at **OAC 3701-29-06(G)(3)(a)–(d)**
that group many features under two numbers. The **well** side is different —
OAC 3701-28-07(J) Table 1 is a genuine 34-row table.

### 4.8 Reconciliation check [V]

The two chapters agree at the boundary: OAC 3701-29-06(G)(3)(c) puts all STS
components 50 ft from a water supply source, and OAC 3701-28-07(J) Table 1 row 2
puts a well 50 ft from any STS component. No conflict to resolve.

Note the asymmetry worth encoding: **10 ft applies to a *properly sealed*
(abandoned) well; a live well is a "water supply source" at 50 ft.**

## 5. WHERE TO FILE

- **Board of Building Standards** (Commerce → Industrial Compliance) — writes
  the RCO, certifies departments, publishes the address lookup. `com.ohio.gov`
- **Building Department Look Up** — address-searchable, reports residential and
  commercial certification separately and names the plumbing entity.
  `maps.ohio.gov`
- **Division of Industrial Compliance** — the backstop for **nonresidential**
  plan review only. **It cannot issue a residential permit.** Its own plan-review
  page never mentions 1-, 2- or 3-family dwellings and requires sealed drawings,
  which R.C. 3791.04(A)(2)(b) waives for houses.
- **OCILB** — commercial trade licenses; verification at `elicense.ohio.gov`
- **Local health districts** — septic and private water, statewide. Locator at
  `odh.ohio.gov`. **R.C. 3709.01:** "Each city constitutes a city health
  district. The townships and villages in each county shall be combined into a
  general health district." Districts also merge, so "one per county" is wrong.
- **Ohio EPA** — construction stormwater general permit **OHC000006**, effective
  23 April 2023 to 22 April 2028, threshold **one acre** *or less within a
  larger common plan of development*. Big Darby Creek and Olentangy River
  watersheds have separate permits. `epa.ohio.gov`
- **ODNR** — water well logs `waterwells.ohiodnr.gov`; floodplain coordination
  (the permit is issued locally). `ohiodnr.gov`
- **ODOT** — Right-of-Way & Utility Permit for a state-route driveway;
  `transportation.ohio.gov/about/districts`
- **County engineer** — R.C. 5543.16: "The owners of land shall construct and
  keep in repair all approaches or driveways from the public roads, **under the
  direction of the county engineer**." Township roads: R.C. 5571.02.
- **Zoning** — townships R.C. Ch. 519, counties R.C. Ch. 303, **both only in
  unincorporated territory**. R.C. 505.73 lets a township adopt a property
  *maintenance* code, which must govern "subject matter not addressed by the
  state residential building code" — **not a new-construction permit power**.
- **Board of Building Appeals** — `bbalookup.com.ohio.gov`
- **Ohio 811** — `oups.org`
- **County auditor** — parcel data; Ohio has **no statewide parcel portal**.

*Portal warning [V]: the Commerce, Health, EPA and Natural Resources sites share
a platform on which short marketing-style URLs frequently return a page that
loads normally and says "No content found." Every address printed in the kit was
checked as a live page, not merely as a link.*

## DELIBERATELY NOT PRINTED

- **Any permit or health-district fee.** Local and annual. The kit names which
  fees exist — which is stable and useful — and leaves amounts to write-in lines.
- **Any statewide frost depth.** The RCO's cell is blank by design. Any source
  quoting "32 to 42 inches" as the Ohio code is quoting something not in it.
- **Any ground snow load or seismic design category** as a statewide figure —
  blank for the same reason.
- **Any count of Ohio's local health districts.** The ODH locator renders
  client-side; no official total was retrievable; the commonly repeated "about
  110" could not be confirmed.
- **A township denominator** for certification (see §2.8).
- **Whether a specific county contracts enforcement to the state.** R.C.
  3781.10(E)(7) permits it; no roster of who does was found.
- **Whether a jurisdiction's residential certification covers remodels.** R.C.
  3781.10(E)(2) creates separate new-construction and repair-and-alteration
  certificates, but the published dataset exposes a single flag.
- **"There are no permits at all" in the uncovered counties.** The verified claim
  is narrower and is what the kit prints: no building plan submission, approval,
  inspection or certificate of occupancy under R.C. 3791.04(A)(1)(b) and RCO
  §101.5. Septic, well, plumbing, zoning, floodplain, sewer tap and driveway
  access all survive.
- **"Your health district issues your plumbing permit"** as a universal rule.
  R.C. 3781.03(C) lists four possible enforcers.
- **Municipalities-must / counties-may.** Refuted; see §2.7.
- **Deck and ledger construction requirements.** The §102.10 deck *exemption* is
  verified; Ohio's renumbering hid the construction provisions and they were not
  located. The kit prints the exemption and no construction rule.
- **Any phone number, anywhere.**

## OPEN QUESTIONS

1. **Is a 2021-IRC-based RCO filed or pending?** Unresolved. codes.ohio.gov
   publishes rules on their effective date, so a filed-but-pending package is
   invisible there; the Register of Ohio search endpoint is session-gated and the
   Board's proposed-rules page renders client-side. The circumstantial case is
   strong — the commercial building code and the plumbing code both moved to
   2021 I-Codes on 15 October 2025, and the RCO's five-year review date of
   28 December 2024 has passed — but it is [H]. **Method for a human:**
   registerofohio.state.oh.us → Search Rules → Agency 4101, Division 8; plus
   JCARR agendas. **This is the top revision tripwire for Ohio.**
2. **Detached outbuildings over 200 sq ft.** R.C. 3781.06(C)(11) defines an
   accessory structure as *attached*; RCO §102.10 exempts *detached* ones ≤ 200
   sq ft. The interaction is genuinely unresolved.
3. **How R.C. 4740.12(A) interacts with a purely residential local trade
   license.** The subsection conditions a fee-charging local registration on
   also requiring the state license — but the state license does not exist for
   houses. Cities plainly do issue residential trade licenses. Not settled in
   text; the kit tells readers to comply locally rather than argue it.
4. **Whether the CSPA reaches sub-$25,000 residential work.** Follows from the
   interaction of R.C. 1345.01(A) and 4722.01(C), but no case was found squarely
   holding it. Kit hedges appropriately.
5. **Ohio Plumbing Code content for 1-3 family dwellings.** The pointer and the
   current edition are verified; the OPC's own residential provisions were not
   read chapter by chapter.

## LATE ADDENDUM — findings that arrived after the first draft

- **The NEC redline mechanism generalizes.** The Board files every rule as an
  amendment PDF with strikethrough and underline. This corrupts flat text
  extraction for *any* Ohio building-code fact, not just the NEC edition. Two
  further instances were caught by the same method: the plumbing-code edition pin
  (struck out entirely in April 2024) and the deleted 600/400 sq ft frost
  exemptions in the March 2024 Foundations amendment. **Any future Ohio work must
  read the rule PDFs as rendered pages.** The kit prints this warning in its
  closing note on every document.
- **Frost depth is a negative finding, not a missing one.** The blank cell is
  deliberate and footnoted; this corrects our own state guide, which prints a
  range that has no basis in the code.
- **Radon likewise.** The RCO adopted no IRC appendices, so Appendix F is simply
  not part of Ohio's code.
- **Stairs are more permissive than the model code** (8¼ / 9 against 7¾ / 10).
  This is the rare Ohio amendment that gives an owner-builder something.
- **The plumbing code is on a newer cycle than the building code** — 2021 IPC
  against the 2018 IRC. Counterintuitive and worth watching at revision time.
