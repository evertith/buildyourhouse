# Arizona Owner-Builder Permit Guide

Arizona combines owner-builder-friendly laws with unique desert construction requirements. From Phoenix's urban sprawl to rural areas with minimal regulations, Arizona offers diverse building opportunities with generally reasonable permitting processes and year-round construction seasons.

## Arizona Building Code Overview

Arizona uses a **statewide minimum code** with local jurisdictions allowed to adopt more current versions.

### Current Code Adoption (2025)

**Statewide Minimum**:
- **2018 International Residential Code** (with Arizona amendments)
- **2018 International Energy Conservation Code**
- **2020 National Electrical Code** with Arizona amendments

**Major Cities** (often adopt newer):
- Phoenix, Scottsdale, Tucson, Mesa: May use 2021 IRC
- Smaller jurisdictions: Usually 2018 IRC
- Rural areas: Minimal or no codes

### Key Arizona Amendments

1. **Desert Climate**: Hot, arid climate considerations
2. **Energy Code**: Tailored for desert cooling loads (minimal heating)
3. **Wildfire Protection**: Extensive requirements in WUI zones
4. **Water Conservation**: Landscape and plumbing requirements
5. **Monsoon Drainage**: Critical drainage requirements for flash floods
6. **Termite Protection**: Required in most areas (subterranean termites)
7. **Expansive Soils**: Common in Phoenix area, requires engineering

## Arizona Owner-Builder Laws

Arizona has strong owner-builder exemptions with clear regulations.

### Legal Rights (A.R.S. § 32-1121)

Property owners may:
- Build, improve, or repair single-family residence on property they own
- Pull permits as owner-builder without contractor license
- Perform work themselves or hire subcontractors
- Act as their own general contractor

### Critical Restrictions

**Owner-Occupancy Requirement**:
- Must occupy as primary residence or intend to within reasonable time
- Cannot build spec homes for sale
- 18-month occupancy period recommended before sale

**Disclosure Requirements**:
- If selling within 2 years, must disclose owner-built status
- Must use Owner-Builder Declaration form with permit application

**Licensing Requirements**:

**Must Be Licensed**:
- **Electrical work**: Licensed electrician required (limited exemptions for homeowner)
- **Plumbing work**: Licensed plumber required
- **HVAC work**: Licensed contractor required
- **Roofing**: Licensed roofer required for commercial insurance-rated roofs (residential can be DIY in many areas)

**Owner Can Perform**:
- Framing, foundation, concrete work
- Painting, flooring, tile
- Finish carpentry
- Landscaping

**License Verification**: Arizona Registrar of Contractors - www.azroc.gov

### Insurance Considerations

- Workers' comp not required for casual labor
- Liability insurance recommended
- Builder's risk insurance strongly recommended (fire risk in desert)

## Processing Timelines

Arizona timelines are generally reasonable, especially in smaller jurisdictions.

### Plan Review

**Major Cities** (Phoenix, Scottsdale, Tucson):
- First review: 15-30 business days
- Resubmittal: 10-20 business days
- Total to approval: 5-10 weeks

**Counties**:
- First review: 10-20 business days
- Total to approval: 3-7 weeks

**Small Cities/Towns**:
- First review: 5-15 business days
- Total to approval: 2-5 weeks

**Rural/No Code Areas**:
- No review required

### Permit Validity

- 6-12 months without inspection (varies by jurisdiction)
- Extensions available if progressing

## Energy Code Requirements

Arizona's energy code focuses on cooling loads (Climate Zone 2B - hot-dry).

### 2018 IECC Requirements (Zone 2B)

**Insulation Minimums**:
- Wall: R-13 (lower than most states due to minimal heating)
- Ceiling: R-38 (R-30 acceptable in some areas)
- Floor: R-13 (rarely needed)

**Windows**:
- U-Factor: 0.40 or less
- SHGC: 0.25 or less (critical for cooling loads)

**Air Sealing**: 5 ACH or less

**HVAC**:
- High SEER ratings recommended (16+ SEER) - AC runs 6-9 months
- Proper duct sealing critical
- Duct testing required in most jurisdictions

### Arizona-Specific Energy Strategies

**Critical for Desert**:
- **Solar heat gain control**: Low-SHGC windows, overhangs, shade structures
- **Radiant barrier**: Highly recommended in attic (can reduce cooling costs 10-15%)
- **Light-colored roofing**: Reflects solar radiation
- **High-efficiency AC**: AC is primary energy use
- **Proper insulation**: Focus on ceiling/attic (where heat enters)

**Solar Panels**: No state requirement (unlike California), but excellent ROI in Arizona sunshine

## Special Arizona Considerations

### Expansive Soils (Phoenix Metro)

Much of Phoenix metro sits on expansive clay.

**Soils Report**: $800-$2,000 (recommended or required)
**Engineered Foundation**: $2,500-$5,000 for design
**Post-Tension Slab**: Common solution, $8-$12/sq ft

**Cost Impact**: $8,000-$18,000 for engineered foundation

### Monsoon Drainage

Arizona monsoons bring intense rainfall - drainage critical.

**Requirements**:
- Proper lot grading (positive drainage away from house)
- Swales or French drains often required
- Retention/detention basins in some developments
- Failure to plan for drainage = foundation problems

**Cost**: $2,000-$8,000 for proper drainage system

### Desert Landscaping (Xeriscaping)

Many jurisdictions require water-efficient landscaping.

**Requirements**:
- Native/drought-tolerant plants
- Drip irrigation (not spray)
- Mulch/rock coverage
- Limits on turf grass

**Benefits**: Lower water bills, less maintenance

### Termite Protection

Arizona has subterranean termites - take seriously.

**Pre-Treatment**:
- Chemical soil treatment before slab
- Cost: $500-$1,000
- Licensed applicator required

**Alternative**: Pressure-treated lumber, physical barriers

### Wildfire Protection (WUI Zones)

Many Arizona areas are Wildland-Urban Interface zones.

**Affected Areas**:
- Most mountain areas (Prescott, Flagstaff, Payson, Show Low)
- Desert edge areas
- Rural areas with vegetation

**Requirements**:
- Class A fire-rated roof
- Ignition-resistant construction
- Defensible space (30-100 feet clearance)
- Non-combustible materials within 5 feet of house

**Cost Impact**: $10,000-$30,000 for WUI compliance

### Water Availability

**Well Water** (rural areas):
- Depth varies: 200-1,000+ feet
- Cost: $20-$50/foot
- Total: $6,000-$50,000+ (deeper in some areas)
- Water rights can be complex - research before buying land

**Municipal Water**: Available in urban/suburban areas

**Hauled Water**: Some very rural areas rely on hauled water (tanks)

### Septic Systems

Common in rural Arizona.

**Types**:
- Conventional: $6,000-$12,000
- Alternative systems (engineered): $12,000-$25,000

**Permit**: $500-$1,200
**Timeline**: 4-8 weeks

### Off-Grid Considerations

Arizona is popular for off-grid building:
- Solar panels (excellent sun)
- Battery storage
- Well or hauled water
- Septic system
- Propane for cooking/backup

**Cost**: $30,000-$80,000 for complete off-grid systems

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I build without permits in Arizona?**
A: In some rural unincorporated areas, yes. However:
- Harder to finance and insure
- Harder to sell later
- All liability on you
- Still must meet septic requirements if using septic

**Q: How much can I save as owner-builder?**
A: 20-30% typical in Arizona due to high contractor markups. On $300K build, that's $60,000-$90,000.

**Q: Is financing available?**
A: Difficult but possible. Local credit unions, USDA rural loans. Expect 20-25% down.

**Q: Should I build in a WUI fire zone?**
A: Understand the costs ($10,000-$30,000 extra) and insurance challenges. Fire risk is real - recent wildfires have destroyed entire developments.

**Q: How deep will my well be?**
A: Varies dramatically:
- Phoenix area: 200-600 feet
- Tucson area: 400-800 feet
- Rural areas: 300-1,000+ feet
- Research depth in your specific area before buying land
